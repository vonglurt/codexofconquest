## I. Voice Rules

These rules govern all writing in this arc. They supplement the existing Roll2Hit vignette principles (second person, present tense, no named emotions, objects carry the weight) with rules specific to this character.

**1. The body is specific.**  
Paul is small, bald, physically unremarkable, and has been beaten with rods three times, stoned once, shipwrecked three times, and bitten by a venomous snake. He has a recurring physical condition — likely his eyes — that makes him visibly unwell in certain climates. His opponents have noted his weak bodily presence. His letters are the opposite of weak. This paradox is the character. Do not resolve it. Let it stand in the text.

**2. Work is not suffering.**  
He makes tents. Specifically: goat-hair cloth, heavy, with seams that must hold in wind. He cuts, he stitches, he measures. This is deliberate — he refuses to be a financial burden on the communities he builds. When he is working, he is not suffering. He has made peace with the specific work. The workshop is a rest from the argument. Show this.

**3. The suffering is enumerated, not dramatized.**  
His own accounting: "Thrice beaten with rods. Once stoned. Three shipwrecks. A night and a day in the deep." The style is inventory, not elegy. When something bad happens, describe the next action. After the stoning at Lythros: *he gets up*. Then he takes the road northeast. The sequence is what matters. Document the sequence.

**4. He notices things.**  
The warrant letters in the inner pocket. The stairs at Phillam (seven, then a landing, then five more). The inscription on the altar (administrative, a hedge against omission). The loaf of bread he can smell but cannot see during the blind days. The mark his father made in the Tarsis lintel. He has a surveyor's attention. Use it.

**5. His rhetoric has a structure.**  
He starts with the crowd's premise — what they already believe, what they already built, what they already know. He moves through it without dismissing it. He lands somewhere the crowd did not expect but can follow. The Aethon speech is the model. When he speaks in-game, the dialogue should follow this structure: find the entry point, move, land.

**6. The conversion scene is external, not internal.**  
He does not describe what it felt like. He describes what happened: fell to the ground, heard a voice, could not see. He says this to multiple crowds, in essentially the same words, without elaboration. He does not embellish. The restraint is the testimony. When the node text approaches the conversion, describe the external facts only. Do not go inside.

**7. He argues with people he respects.**  
At Herath (Jerusalem visit 2), he argues with Peter publicly — "I opposed him to his face." This is not hostility; it is the way he expresses respect for a question. NPCs he likes are NPCs he pushes back on. Barnach, Silar, Timael — all of them get pushback at some point. The warmth is inside the argument, not around it.

**8. The letters are not secondary.**  
He writes from prison. He writes from house arrest. He writes on the road. The letters reach places he cannot walk to, which means they extend his range in ways his legs do not. The letter is not a consolation for being stuck. It is the most effective tool he has. When writing involves a letter, name what the letter carries.

## II. Node Texts

---

### HR — Herath *(the law-city; pre-conversion)*

The city is organized around authority in the literal sense: the buildings that house it stand at the center, and the streets radiate outward from them by function. The archives. The courts. The rooms used for detention, which are referred to by a different name.

You have offices here. An actual room with a desk, with an assistant who maintains the warrant ledger. You have been efficient. The ledger reflects this. Fourteen warrants issued in the last quarter. Fourteen correctly processed.

The people in the corridor look slightly to your left when they pass you. This is standard — you have observed it for long enough to know it is not about you specifically. People who have done nothing wrong still behave as if they have when they see someone carrying your authority. You have stopped taking this as information.

---

### KS — Kesra *(the road; the blind days; the basket)*

The lower road in Kesra leads to the textile district. This is where the address is — the house where the man you want has been seen. You have the address written on the second warrant. The city is large enough to need an address.

You walk the street twice before you stop walking it. The street bends twice, then runs straight, and the bending is the thing the city decided was worth naming. You have the number. You will not find the number. This will become clear on the third pass.

After: a room on the lower road. Seven stairs, a landing, five more. The bread on the table arrived on the first day. You know it is still there because you can smell it. You have been blind for two days and have become precise about information. The warrants are in the inner pocket. You have not moved them. You will need to do something about the warrants.

On the third day, a man stops on the landing. This is notable — no one else has stopped on the landing. He is deciding something. Then he continues up.

---

### DR — The Dust Roads *(three months; no record)*

The caravan route goes east and south. There is no other documentation from this period.

You are in the desert for three months. The desert does not keep records, and you do not keep records during this period either. What can be said: you leave Kesra as one thing. You come back having had three months to locate what needs to be built.

The desert is useful for this in a specific way — it has no opinion about what you believed four months ago, and it has no opinion about what you believe now. This is not comfort exactly. It is a clean surface. You need a clean surface.

---

### TS — Tarsis *(home; the lintel; Barnach arrives)*

You were born in this city. The house has changed hands twice since your family left. The stone facing is the same. You look for your father's mark in the lintel as a reflex — it was made when the stone was set, a small horizontal line with a notch in the center. It is still there. It will be there next time.

Barnach finds you here. He has come from Herath. He is looking for a specific person: someone who can argue from inside the old system and inside the new one simultaneously, without needing the argument translated. He has concluded you are that person.

He is right. Tarsis has nothing more to show you. The lintel will hold.

---

### AO — Anthos *(the base city; the new name; the commissioning)*

The community meets above a grain merchant. The room is large and costs nothing — the merchant is a member, and the arrangement is practical. Everyone has stopped noticing the smell of grain from below. This is how long they have been meeting here.

Someone outside the community named them first. This is how names given from the outside work — they arrive with the bluntness of someone who doesn't have a stake in the precision, and they stick. The community here was the first to be named. The name travels.

The fire runs early. The meeting runs long. You break bread together after the argument. No one leaves before the breaking.

The day they set you apart is a day like the others. There is fasting. There is the laying on of hands. Then you leave.

---

### CI2 — The Copper Isle *(Ezzir; the governor; the contest)*

The island makes copper. Everything on it reflects this: the soil, the light, the specific orange-red of the buildings on the west side. The harbor runs on goods going in being worth more than goods going out, which is the correct harbor economy.

Ezzir is at the governor's residence. He has been there long enough to have a seat and a title. His work is specific: he tells people with authority what they want to hear, in a form they can use. He is very good at this. You will meet him in a room with the governor present, which means the room will decide something before it empties.

The governor watches the way a man watches who has already decided to act on the outcome but does not want to be seen deciding. You have been in rooms like this before. The difference is which side of the room you are on.

Ezzir will not give up the seat voluntarily. This is accurate. He will also not win this room. By the time the meeting ends, both of these facts will be established.

---

### LT — Lythros *(the lame man; the crowd; the road northeast)*

The city has a main square. Markets on the south side, administrative buildings on the north. The square fills quickly — this is a market city, and a market square is already half-full at any given hour.

The man who cannot walk has sat near the south entrance since before anyone currently alive can remember sitting there. He is part of the market's furniture. The market has stopped seeing him the way it stops seeing furniture. You see him.

After, the crowd is confused about what to do with what it has just witnessed. Market crowds are used to seeing things for sale. They are not used to seeing this. They take a moment to decide, and then they decide all at once.

The road out of Lythros runs northeast. You take it. You get up from the square first, then you take it. The order matters.

---

### PL — Phillam *(Lyra; the river meeting; the prison; the stairs)*

The community meets on the riverbank outside the city walls. There is no building. This is not an oversight — there is no building yet. They meet under open sky because that is what there is.

Lyra buys purple cloth from the eastern mills and sells it to households that can afford it. She keeps a precise ledger. When she makes a decision, it is made. Her household becomes the community's base for the duration of your stay in Phillam. The guest room is on the second floor. The stairs are reliable.

The prison is a room below the city hall. You are beaten before you are put in it. This is the first time, documented. Silar is there. You sing, which is unusual behavior for a prison. Silar notes this. An earthquake occurs at midnight. The doors open. The chains come loose. The jailer wakes in a dark room where the doors should not be open.

You stay. The jailer brings you upstairs to his house. His household eats together in the middle of the night. After, you invoke your citizenship for the record — not for release, which has already happened, but to establish that you were beaten uncondemned and that the magistrates should know it. The magistrates are alarmed and come personally. You make them walk you out. This is for the church you are leaving behind.

You remember the reliable stairs at Lyra's house.

---

### AE — Aethon *(the altars; the unknown altar; the speech)*

The city is full of altars. This is not rhetorical — there are altars on corner lots, in garden spaces visible from the street, in the market squares. The Aethonans are methodical about gods: they accommodate all of them, including ones they haven't encountered yet.

The altar to the Unknown God is in the north quarter. The inscription is administrative. Someone in the city's history was concerned about inadvertently omitting a god and built an insurance altar. The hedge covers everything unaccounted for. It is the most honest thing in the city.

You speak from the public hall steps. This is a city with a tradition of public argument, and the crowd has seen speakers before. They have calibrated opinions. They will tell you when they stop listening — not rudely, just by leaving. Some will stop before you finish. A few will not stop.

You start with the altar. You tell them who it is for. This is the entry point: you use what they already built.

---

### KR — Korath *(the workshop; the tentmaking; eighteen months)*

The tentmaking district is east of the harbor. Prisca and Akil have a workshop there: two looms, a cutting table, a bench along the south wall. The goat-hair cloth is the material — heavy, smells of the animal in wet weather, makes seams that hold in wind when done correctly. You work at the bench.

You have worked with your hands since Tarsis. The specific work is not a demotion. The seam has to hold in wind — this is a real problem with a correct answer, and working on it is a rest from the argument. Prisca sets the standard. She named the workshop. You work to her standard.

Eighteen months in one city. You eat at the same table. You argue in the meeting hall, and in the harbor market when people have questions, and in the workshop when someone comes in for an answer. The governor eventually hears the formal complaint. He reads the charges. He declines to rule on internal religious disputes. This is legally correct, and you make note of it.

---

### EF — Ephrath *(three years; the hall of Tyrannus; Demetrios; the north gate)*

The Hall of Tyrannus rents cheaply during the middle of the day — the fifth hour to the tenth, when the hall stands empty during the city's rest period. You teach there daily. Five hours. Two years. The heat in the hall at midday is specific: the stones hold the morning warmth and release it by noon. Everyone in the hall sweats equally. No one stops coming.

The temple of the Threaded Lady is the economic engine of the city. Everything in Ephrath orbits it at a distance calibrated by dependence. The silversmiths are close in. Demetrios has been in the silver trade thirty years. He is correct about what your presence costs him. He is very effective at translating a business problem into a street.

You leave through the north gate. This is not the gate you arrived through. It is practical.

---

### MT — Melta *(two hundred and seventy-six; the wood; the snake)*

The number is in the ship's log: two hundred and seventy-six people aboard. The mast goes first, then the hull planking, then there is a beach. The people on the beach build a fire, which is the correct response to two hundred and seventy-six cold, shipwrecked people arriving at once.

You gather wood. There is a snake in the wood — this is not unusual, wood piles on islands hold snakes. You shake it off into the fire.

The people on the beach have a theory about what the snake means. The theory requires a specific outcome: you swell up and die within a defined window. They watch the window. The window passes. You continue. The crowd revises its theory about who you are. This is the second theory they have held about you since the ship went down; the first was that you were a criminal being transported to execution, based on the ship's manifest. Both theories are wrong. The crowd adjusts.

---

### ST — The Seat *(house arrest; the letters; the open ending)*

The apartment is on the third floor. Two guards, rotating. They are bored. Over two years you have become a specific kind of familiar to the rotation — not exactly a prisoner, because you receive visitors, and not exactly free, because they are there.

You write letters. This is not a diminishment. The letters go where you cannot go, which means they extend farther than you could walk. You have been beaten with rods three times, stoned once, shipwrecked three times, left for dead in a market square, let down over a city wall in a basket in the middle of the night, and bitten by a snake that did nothing. The letters are the safest thing you do.

The visitors come often. More than expected. The city is large and indifferent in the way that large cities are indifferent — it does not especially care what you believe, which is easier to work with than hostility.

The record ends here. This is not an error. The record ends here because the person keeping it — Luke, the doctor, who has been keeping it since the ship left — has documented what he was present for and stops at the boundary of what he saw. The text does not close. The apartment door is open.

## III. Quest Descriptions

> Format: `desc` (2–4 sentences, object-named, no named emotions) + `hint` (directive, one sentence) + `disposition` (in-game flavor quote).

---

### `quest_road_kesra` — "The Light at Noon"

**desc:** The warrants are in the inner pocket — fourteen, correctly sealed, each authorizing the arrest of people you have never met but know by description. The road to Kesra runs straight through midday. You have carried authority before. You know the weight of it. The light changes quality at the midpoint of the road: not brighter. Different. The kind of different that comes before an event rather than during one.

**hint:** Walk the road to Kesra.

**disposition:** *"The voice was not loud. That was the first thing. You expected loud."*

---

### `quest_anath` — "The House on the Lower Road"

**desc:** Three days without sight. The bread on the table arrived on the first day; you know it is still there because you can smell it. The warrants are still in the inner pocket. You have not moved them. Someone has been told to come. You can hear him deciding on the landing, one floor below — he stops there, and the stopping is the decision.

**hint:** Wait. Anath will come on the third day.

**disposition:** *"Brother," he said. You had not been called that in this city.*

---

### `quest_barnach_vouches` — "Vouched For"

**desc:** They know what you were before you arrived. The room does not offer you a chair. Barnach stands between you and the exit and speaks for you in the specific way a man speaks for someone he is not entirely certain of — because the alternative is worse than the uncertainty.

**hint:** Reach Herath. Barnach's favor is required before the room will consider you.

**disposition:** *"He will stay with me. That is how I am saying it."* — Barnach

---

### `quest_ezzir` — "The Sorcerer's Opposition"

**desc:** Ezzir has been at the governor's residence long enough to have a seat and a title and the ear of someone important. He will not give these up voluntarily. The governor is watching the room the way a man watches who has already decided to act on the outcome but does not want to be seen deciding. Ezzir is very good at his work. This is noted before the contest begins.

**hint:** Face Ezzir at the Copper Isle governor's residence.

**disposition:** *"He has been here thirty years. You have been here three days."* — observation at the harbor, pre-meeting

---

### `quest_stoning_lythros` — "Left for Dead"

**desc:** The man who could not walk could walk. The whole market square witnessed this. The crowd's opinion reversed faster than you would have predicted. The road northeast runs out of Lythros past the south gate marker. You take it. You get up from the square first, and then you take it. The sequence is the documentation.

**hint:** Survive Lythros. Then take the northeast road.

**disposition:** *"He got up."* — eyewitness, market square, Lythros

---

### `quest_unknown_altar` — "The Unknown Altar"

**desc:** The altar is in the north quarter. The inscription is administrative — a hedge against omission, an insurance altar built by someone in the city's past who was concerned about accidentally leaving a god out. The inscription says: *To the Unknown God.* The public hall steps have been used for arguments before. The crowd has calibrated opinions. Some of them will stop listening before you finish. A few will not stop.

**hint:** Find the entry point. Start with what they already built.

**disposition:** *"To the Unknown God: I know who that is."* — Paul, public hall steps, Aethon

---

### `quest_temple_riot` — "Riot in the Marketplace"

**desc:** Demetrios has been in the silver trade thirty years. He is not wrong about the numbers — your presence in Ephrath changes the market for temple souvenirs in a specific and calculable direction. He is very effective at translating a business problem into a street. The north gate is not the gate you arrived through. Practical.

**hint:** Reach the north gate before the crowd closes it.

**disposition:** *"Great is the Threaded Lady!"* — Demetrios, approximately two minutes before the street becomes a problem

---

### `quest_shipwreck_melta` — "Two Hundred and Seventy-Six"

**desc:** The number is in the ship's log. The mast goes first. Then the hull planking. Then there is a beach, and people on it building a fire, which is the correct response. You gather wood because gathering wood is what there is to do.

**hint:** Survive the wreck. Reach Melta.

**disposition:** *"Everyone who can swim — go first. The rest, on planks."* — Paul, before the hull goes, Acts 27

---

### `quest_snake_melta` — "It Did Nothing"

**desc:** There is a snake in the wood. This is not unusual — wood piles on islands hold snakes. You shake it off into the fire. The crowd on the beach has a theory about what this means. The theory requires a specific outcome within a specific window. The window passes. You continue. The crowd revises.

**hint:** Nothing is required. Wait for the window to pass.

**disposition:** *"The crowd changed their minds about him."* — unnamed observer, Melta beach

## IV. NPC Voice Lines

One line each. Minimum words to establish the character.

---

**`anath`** — Healer. Reluctant obedience. He has been told to come and has come despite himself.
> *"I was told to come. I am here. That is the extent of what I can tell you about why I am here."*

---

**`barnach`** — Advocate. He vouches not from certainty but from necessity. The warmth is inside the argument, not around it.
> *"He will stay with me. I am not asking for a discussion about this."*

---

**`silar`** — Second journey co-traveler. Dry. Has been beaten and imprisoned and has opinions about all of it.
> *"You realize that singing in a prison cell is unusual behavior."*

---

**`timael`** — Youngest companion. Picked up at Lythros, which was the wrong city to pick someone up in, and he came anyway.
> *"I will go where you go. I have said this before and the situation has not changed my position."*

---

**`lyra`** — Purple cloth merchant. Organized. When she makes a decision, it is made. Her household operates correctly.
> *"The guest room is yours. The stairs are reliable. You will find the household runs on a schedule."*

---

**`prisca_akil`** — Tentmakers. Prisca runs the workshop. She is named first for a reason.
> *"We make tents. The argument can continue at dinner, where it belongs."* — Prisca

---

**`the_governors`** — The prosecution arc. Multiple faces, one function. This line is the Korath governor, whose correct ruling should be documented.
> *"I find nothing in this that falls under my jurisdiction. This matter is closed."*

## VI. The Thorn

The thorn (the recurring physical condition, most likely an eye condition) is a mechanical debuff that cannot be removed. He asks three times for its removal and is told no. The answer is: *"My strength is made perfect in weakness."*

In-game implementation: a permanent status effect visible in the character sheet — `Thorn (Permanent)`. No mechanical penalty (he functions at full capacity). One cosmetic effect: when the player examines their character at a rest node, the thorn is listed. No tooltip. No explanation. If the player has found the Fishing Guide, they will know how to read a stat line and will notice it.

This is not a puzzle. There is no quest to remove it. The answer has already been given.
