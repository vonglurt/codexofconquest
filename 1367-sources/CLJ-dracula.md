## Pass 1 — Overview

Dracula is an epistolary novel told entirely through assembled documents: Jonathan Harker's shorthand journal, Mina Murray's diary, Lucy Westenra's letters, Dr. Seward's phonograph records, newspaper clippings, and Van Helsing's correspondence. Stoker's preface declares: "There is throughout no statement of past things wherein memory may err, for all the records chosen are exactly contemporary, given from the standpoints and within the range of knowledge of those who made them." The assembled document is not merely the medium of the story — it is the argument. Without the compilation, each witness is alone and deniable.

The novel follows Jonathan Harker (a young English solicitor's clerk sent to Transylvania to assist Count Dracula in completing a London property purchase), his imprisonment in Dracula's castle, his discovery of what Dracula is, his escape, Dracula's arrival in England in a ship of dead men, the deaths of Lucy Westenra, the assembly of a team of investigators, and the final chase back across Europe to Transylvania.

For the pipeline, Dracula's most useful elements are:
- **The assembled dossier as proof**: Van Helsing's central argument — each account separately can be dismissed; together they are unassailable; the compiler who stitches them together creates the proof by assembling what no single mind could generate alone
- **The property transaction as enablement**: Dracula needed a solicitor to legally purchase Carfax Abbey; the legal documents of property transfer made his invasion possible; the law that was designed to protect enabled the danger
- **The locked room and the professional obligation**: Harker continues to do his job inside the castle even as he realizes he is a prisoner; professional duty and terror in simultaneous tension
- **The correspondence between women**: Mina's letters to Lucy; Lucy's diary; the women are the most careful observers, least believed, most endangered
- **The box of earth**: Dracula must sleep in Transylvanian earth; moving or consecrating his boxes is how to neutralize him; the object is the key to his power and his vulnerability simultaneously
- **Mina as compiler-archivist**: She typed up all the documents; the proof exists because she assembled it; the archivist creates the case

Unique features as quest material:
- The entire novel is about documents — keeping them, losing them, assembling them, being believed based on them
- The property deed as a weapon: the transfer of title to Carfax Abbey is the act that enables the invasion; the solicitor's clerk is unknowingly an instrument of the harm
- The Scholomance: a Romanian/Transylvanian legend Stoker uses — a school near Sibiu where ten scholars study dark knowledge; one in ten stays with the teacher permanently
- The novel is set contemporaneously with 1897 but the vampire folklore is exactly contemporary with 1367 Transylvania; Harker's descriptions of Wallachs, Saxons, Szekelys, and Magyars are accurate for the 14th century; Vlad I of Wallachia was ruling 1364–1377

## Pass 2 — Structure

### Part 1 Content (Chapters I–IV: Jonathan Harker's Journal)

**Chapter I:** Harker travels east — Munich, Vienna, Budapest, Klausenburg, Bistritz. At Bistritz he receives Dracula's letter directing him to the Golden Krone Hotel. The landlady begs him not to travel on St. George's Eve (the evil things in the world have full sway). She puts her crucifix around his neck. The coach passengers give him protective gifts. Wolves. The Borgo Pass. A driver with red eyes and a steel grip arrives. "For the dead travel fast." The castle: ruined, dark, no lights.

**Chapter II:** Dracula himself opens the door — old, white-mustached, entirely in black, silver lamp. Courtly. "Welcome to my house. Come freely. Go safely; and leave something of the happiness you bring." Harker observes him: aquiline nose, massive brows, very red lips, sharp white teeth, coarse hands with hair in the center of the palm. No reflection in mirrors. Does not eat. Explains that he is moving to England; needs Harker's professional assistance to complete the property transaction. By day Harker is locked in. He explores and finds only locked doors.

**Chapter III:** Harker discovers he cannot leave. The three women who sleep in a dusty room — they almost attack him; Dracula stops them. "This man belongs to me!" Harker realizes the driver and the Count were the same person. He sees Dracula crawling down the castle wall face-down. Harker finds Dracula sleeping in a box of earth in a vault.

**Chapter IV:** The boxes of earth — fifty of them, being loaded onto carts. Harker tries to get help from the Szekely gypsies who load them. He dispatches letters through them; the letters come back opened and returned by the Count. He writes in shorthand. Final entry: the three women approach his room at midnight. Then: a long gap.

### Part 2 Content (Chapters V–XI: England; Lucy's Decline)

Lucy Westenra receives three proposals on the same day (Seward, Morris, Godalming); accepts Godalming. Mina and Lucy's friendship in letters — the warmth and specificity of their correspondence. Lucy begins sleepwalking. Mina finds her in Whitby churchyard, leaning against a tombstone, with two red marks on her throat. The Demeter arrives at Whitby in a storm — the captain dead, lashed to the wheel, the log showing the crew disappeared one by one. The dog that leaps off the ship as it grounds. Lucy deteriorating — Seward calls Van Helsing. Transfusions. Lucy dies. Returns. The children she stalks. Staking Lucy: the physical reality of it; Holmwood's "unnatural" grief-release as he strikes.

### Part 3 Content (Chapters XII–XVIII: Van Helsing's Assembly)

Van Helsing explains to the men what Lucy was. He assembles the evidence — the documents, the wounds, the newspaper reports of "the bloofer lady." He persuades Seward. Mina has Jonathan's journal (he threw it into a drawer after his breakdown, recovered in Budapest). She types up everything. Van Helsing reads it. He tells her: "You are braver than any of us." The group forms: Van Helsing, Seward, Morris, Godalming, Jonathan, Mina. They begin locating Dracula's London houses and the boxes of earth. They consecrate boxes. Dracula retaliates: he enters Mina's room, forces her to drink his blood. The men find him gone. Mina is marked — a red scar on her forehead from the sacred wafer. She is connected to Dracula by the link he made. Van Helsing uses hypnosis to read what she senses. This becomes the tracking mechanism.

### Part 4 Content (Chapters XIX–XXVII: The Chase)

Dracula flees to Transylvania by ship. The party follows — Mina's hypnotic connection guides them (twice a day, at dawn and dusk, she reports what she senses: water, wolves, cold, motion). They split into groups. Race to reach the castle before Dracula's helpers can get his box inside before sunset. Mina's mark fades as they approach — it will disappear at sunset, signaling Dracula's full return to his power. Van Helsing consecrates the castle, entombs the three brides. Morris and Dracula die at the same moment as the sun sets. Mina's mark disappears — she is free.

## CLJ-01 Full Vignette Spec: "The Compiled Account"

### Source Hook

Van Helsing says: "We have all the evidence — Jonathan's journal, Mina's typescript, the phonograph records, the newspaper clippings, Lucy's diary. No court and no bishop and no lord would believe any of us telling it alone. But these documents — assembled, consistent, each derived without the others' knowledge — tell the same story. That is the only argument that cannot be accused of collusion."

The dossier is proof not because any single document is conclusive, but because independent witnesses who did not coordinate their observations agree on the sequence of events. The compiler who understood this — Mina, who typed the manuscripts and saw the agreement emerging — created the argument by doing the work of assembly.

### 1367 Setting

Three men in a village near Bistritz — Father Miklos the village priest, Heinz the Saxon merchant, and Captain Benedek the Hungarian officer quartered there through the winter — have each separately written accounts of a series of deaths over the previous eight weeks. The deaths share consistent features: the first victim wasted without evident cause; her body was found with marks on the throat; the second victim was found near the first grave; and so on through seven deaths. Each man wrote what he personally witnessed and recorded separately.

**Doktor Janos** — the court physician of Bistritz — has read all three accounts, found them independently consistent, and stitched them together into a single folio with a prefacing analysis. His argument: the agreement between three witnesses who did not speak to each other about their conclusions is the only form of proof that resists the charge of collective delusion.

He is sending the folio to the Bishop's archdeacon at Klausenburg before the visiting Dominican inquisitor (Fra Bonaventura) completes his own official investigation. Fra Bonaventura's preliminary finding is that the deaths resulted from a local groundwater contamination — his report will be officially filed in three days. Once filed, the official record will resist correction.

**TOKEN:** A stitched folio — three separately dated accounts in three different hands on three different kinds of paper (the priest's account on vellum, the merchant's on cheap German paper, the officer's on Hungarian military stock), prefaced by a physician's analytical note in a fourth hand. Sewn together with black thread, sealed with Doktor Janos's wax seal on the outside. The folio is thick and slightly damp from the cold house.

**TOKEN granted:** Act 1 (Doktor Janos releases the folio)
**TOKEN checked:** Acts 2, 3, 4
**TOKEN taken:** Act 5 (the archdeacon's secretary receives the folio)

**Commission city:** BIS — Bistritz (Bistrița), Saxon market town in eastern Transylvania, 1367
**Delivery city:** KLZ — Klausenburg (Cluj-Napoca), principal city of Transylvania under Hungarian crown

### Act 1 — Bistritz (Doktor Janos's study)
**DC 12 | Persuasion or Insight**

Janos has the folio ready and sealed — but he is not well. He has been sitting too long in his cold study, drinking brandy for warmth, convincing himself the folio is wrong or incomplete. He wants to add a fourth section: his own account. The problem: his own account would not be independently derived; it would be the compiler's account, which undermines the core argument that the three accounts are independent. He cannot see this clearly right now.

He also keeps returning to a variant reading of the officer's account — Captain Benedek described the first victim's wound as "two small marks as if from a pointed instrument, the smaller slightly lower." Janos keeps re-reading this and asking whether "slightly lower" means below the first mark or below the collarbone. He wants to write to Benedek for clarification.

**Skill check purpose:** Get the folio released without further additions.

- **Success (Persuasion DC 12):** The carrier makes the structural argument: adding the physician's own account changes the folio's nature from independent testimony to supervised testimony; the argument rests on the independence of the three accounts; the prefacing analysis is enough. Janos hears this, is quiet, and hands over the sealed folio. He says: "You are right. The argument is what it is, not what I wish it to be."
- **Success (Insight DC 12):** The carrier reads that Janos is frightened, not uncertain. He doesn't want more pages — he wants to not be alone with what he knows. The carrier acknowledges this directly, sits with it for a moment, then takes the folio. Janos says nothing.
- **Failure:** Janos adds four pages of his own account and a query letter to be forwarded to Benedek. The folio's structural argument is partially compromised. At Act 5, the archdeacon's secretary notes the folio contains a first-person account from the compiler (not independent testimony) and files it with a lower evidential classification.

**Flavor:** The study is cold. There are three candles on the desk and they are all burning short. The folio sits in the center of the desk as if Janos has been staring at it for hours. He is wearing his coat inside the house. When he finally hands it over, he does not look at it. He looks at the door.

### Act 2 — Mountain Road (Bistritz Toward the Western Pass)
**DC 12 | Animal Handling or Athletics**

The road from Bistritz toward Klausenburg passes through a wooded section where the road narrows. The coachman hired for the journey is increasingly uneasy — the horses have been pulling against him for twenty minutes, and just before the carrier's position on the road, a wolf has been following the coach at the tree line. Not threatening, just following. The horses know.

The coachman wants to stop at the next village inn and wait until morning — the wolf may attract others. The delay would cost eight hours.

**Skill check purpose:** Keep the journey moving.

- **Animal Handling DC 12:** The carrier can calm the lead horse enough that the coachman regains confidence. Horses that are calm enough to move are horses the coachman will drive. The wolf watches from the trees, then disappears.
- **Athletics DC 12:** The wolf has shifted to block the road ahead — it is standing in the wheel-tracks, watching. The carrier can move forward on foot and drive it off without showing the horses the confrontation. It goes.
- **Failure:** The coachman stops at the village inn. Eight hours lost. At Act 5, the carrier arrives at Klausenburg the morning after Fra Bonaventura submits his report, not the morning before. The archdeacon's secretary has already received the official report; he files the folio as supplementary material rather than prior submission.

**Flavor:** The wolf is not aggressive. That is the disturbing part. It stays twenty yards behind the coach in the wheel-track and does not hurry to close the gap. The coachman has taken his crucifix out and is holding it above the reins. The night is clear and very cold.

---

### Act 3 — Junction Village (Road to Klausenburg, Midway Point)
**DC 12 | Deception or Persuasion**

Fra Bonaventura's assistant — a young Dominican brother named Fra Clement — is at the same junction village, returning to Klausenburg from a different direction. He has been interviewing survivors in the western villages and is carrying his own interview notes to the inquisitor. He sees the physician's seal on the folio the carrier is carrying. He knows Doktor Janos's seal — he visited his house two days ago to ask about the deaths.

Fra Clement is not hostile and not corrupt. He is young, confident, and completely certain that groundwater contamination is the explanation. He wants to read the folio, not to suppress it, but to confirm that Janos's evidence is consistent with the contamination theory. He believes it will be.

**Skill check purpose:** Get through the junction without surrendering the folio for inspection.

- **Deception DC 12:** The carrier claims the folio is a personal medical correspondence — private patient records, not the vampire investigation document at all. Fra Clement has no authority to demand private medical records. He accepts this with visible skepticism.
- **Persuasion DC 12:** The carrier argues that Janos's folio will be submitted to the archdeacon, who will review it alongside the inquisitor's report; Fra Clement will have full opportunity to see the folio's argument when both documents are before the archdeacon simultaneously. Fra Clement considers this. He accepts it as procedurally correct and lets them pass.
- **Failure:** Fra Clement reads the folio's prefacing note (not the full accounts — just the note). He finds the argument about independent witnesses "interesting" and asks if he can have Janos's contact information for follow-up. He also notes in his own documents that the physician has submitted a counter-thesis. This gives Fra Bonaventura advance notice and time to address the folio's argument in his report.

**Flavor:** Fra Clement is twenty-three years old and has never seen anyone die of anything other than war or ordinary illness. He is absolutely certain about what happened to the village. He looks at the folio with the detached professional interest of a man who expects to be confirmed.

---

### Act 4 — Village One Hour From Klausenburg
**DC 12 | Medicine or Persuasion**

One of the original seven victims — a woman named Erzsebet — did not die. She recovered, weakened, with no clear explanation. She has since recanted her initial statement to Captain Benedek, saying she remembered nothing unusual and that she simply had a fever. She is traveling to Klausenburg to submit her recantation in person to the archdeacon's court.

She is not lying. She genuinely does not remember clearly what happened to her. She is frightened. She has been visited by someone in the past two weeks who suggested, firmly, that her recantation would be better for her family.

The problem: her recantation, if filed first, changes the evidentiary picture. The folio's argument rests on seven consistent deaths; if the one surviving victim denies the consistent features, the three accounts have an inconsistency.

**Skill check purpose:** Either treat her fear directly (making her reconsider) or understand her situation well enough to anticipate the archdeacon's question.

- **Medicine DC 12:** The carrier sits with her, examines the scar at her throat (visible under her scarf), and says quietly that this is not the mark of a fever. She already knows this. She breaks down and says a man came to her house and said her children would be protected if she did not make trouble. She will still go to Klausenburg, but now the carrier knows to warn the archdeacon.
- **Persuasion DC 12:** The carrier argues that the archdeacon's court is the only place where she will actually be safe — once the official investigation closes, whoever visited her no longer needs her cooperation. She hears this. She is not convinced, but she is no longer certain her recantation is the safe choice.
- **Failure:** She recants in person before the folio is delivered. The archdeacon's secretary notes the inconsistency when the folio arrives.

**Flavor:** Erzsebet is perhaps thirty-five and looks sixty. She is traveling with a younger sister and they both look at the carrier with a particular expression — not of fear exactly, but of the specific hope that someone else will make a decision they cannot make themselves.

---

### Act 5 — Klausenburg (The Archdeacon's Court)
**DC 12 | Persuasion or Investigation**

The archdeacon's court offices are in the cathedral precinct. Fra Bonaventura submits his official report at midday; if the folio arrives before midday, it will be received as a prior submission and the archdeacon must review both before issuing a finding. If it arrives after midday, it is supplementary.

The archdeacon's secretary — a meticulous Hungarian administrator named Mihaly — controls the submission log. He is scrupulously fair. He has one concern: the folio was sealed by a physician in Bistritz, not a church authority or legal officer. He needs to classify it as either a legal submission (which requires a notary's certification) or a pastoral submission (which requires a priest's countersignature). Doktor Janos provided neither. The folio is a physician's document, which falls between classifications.

**Skill check purpose:** Get the folio logged under a classification that gives it evidential status before midday.

- **Persuasion DC 12:** The carrier argues that a physician's sworn testimony about deaths in his community is equivalent to a pastoral submission in matters of supernatural harm — it is a witness statement from a professional whose observations bear on spiritual as well as physical events. Mihaly checks the classification manual, finds an ambiguous provision, and accepts this argument. He logs the folio as a pastoral submission.
- **Investigation DC 12:** The carrier looks at the classification manual (available at the secretary's desk) and finds the category "documentary evidence, unclassified, pending archdeacon's review" — which requires no countersignature. The folio can be entered as evidence pending review. Mihaly agrees that this is technically correct.
- **Failure (past midday arrival):** The folio is received, logged, and classified as supplementary to the inquisitor's already-submitted report.

**onPass:** The archdeacon reads the prefacing note before Fra Bonaventura's report arrives. He holds both documents for three days. Fra Bonaventura protests; the archdeacon says he requires time to consider independent testimony. Three days is enough for the village to be examined by the archdeacon's own investigators.

---

### onPass

The archdeacon reads the prefacing note and says: "Three accounts, independently derived, agreeing in sequence." He places the folio beside Fra Bonaventura's report. Both documents sit on his desk for three days. His investigators return to the village. The dead stay in the ground after that. Fra Bonaventura's groundwater-contamination report is filed with a note: "findings disputed; archdeacon's review ongoing."

---

### onFail

Fra Bonaventura's report is the official record. "Poisoned groundwater; community advised to boil water from the eastern well." The folio is received but classified as supplementary material to the official report. The village continues to lose people through the following winter. Doktor Janos's seal appears on two more documents the following spring — both requests for transfer to another posting.

## Seeds CLJ-02 through CLJ-06

### CLJ-02 — "The Property Deed"
**Source hook:** Harker's professional obligation was to complete the purchase of Carfax Abbey on Dracula's behalf. ## Cycle 2 — Angle: *The Last Letter — Written in the Interval Before Self-Loss*

**What this angle reveals that cycle 1 missed**: Cycle 1 showed the compiled dossier — independent witnesses assembled so that no single account could be dismissed; the proof that exists only in compilation. Cycle 2 shows the document that cannot be compiled: a letter written from inside the transformation, in the interval when the writer is still herself but knows she may not be when it is read. The compiled dossier is strong because it cannot be accused of collaboration. The Interval Letter is strong for the opposite reason: it has only one voice, fully present, writing from the moment of maximum honesty, when there is nothing left to protect.

### 5-Act Vignette Play: *The Last Letter*

#### The Letter

*The Interval Letter — a single folded sheet, Mina's hand, ring impression in soft dark wax — written in the hour before the men returned, addressed "to whoever reads this after" — dated, timed (she writes the hour), unsigned except with a name; the letter describes what she knows and does not know about her own state; it contains no plea and no reassurance; it is the record of a woman writing from the exact threshold between being herself and not knowing whether she will remain herself; the wax impression is a ring she was still wearing when she pressed it.*

---

**ACT I**

*The Interval Letter — sealed, ring impression intact — Klausenburg archdeacon's court, morning.*

The archdeacon's clerk has the letter as part of a bundle of materials received from the Van Helsing group's contact in the city. The bundle was logged as "medical case materials — Seward group, Transylvania inquiry." The letter is among them — folded, sealed, clearly different in quality from the assembled notes around it. The clerk has been treating it as a cover document or administrative letter.

He does not understand what it is.

The court archivist, a careful woman named Petronela, has noticed that the letter is sealed with a personal ring impression, not a clerical seal. She has been waiting to find out if anyone else notices.

*— The letter has been misclassified by accident, not malice. The Fighter must tell Petronela what it is before someone files it under the wrong category permanently.*

> **Dialogue DC 12.** Tell Petronela the letter's category: not a cover document, not medical case material, not testimony — it is a document written from the interval before possible transformation, when the writer has full knowledge that she may not be herself when it is read; this is neither victim record nor compiled evidence; it is a primary source of a different order, whose truth depends on its being written at exactly that moment and no other.

**Fail:** Petronela nods slowly but keeps the letter on the "pending medical case materials" pile. "I take your point but I need authorization to reclassify." She requests the authorization; it arrives by evening. Try again at the evening hour.

**Pass:** Petronela sets the letter on a separate section of the desk. "I will need documentation that this was received from a primary carrier, not as part of the dossier assembly." She writes a court receipt: *received independently, Interval Letter — Mina Murray's hand, personal seal, separate from dossier materials; Petronela Toth, archdeacon's court Klausenburg.* She releases it to the Fighter with the receipt attached.

**TOKEN GRANTED: The Interval Letter** — single folded sheet, ring impression seal, Petronela's receipt attached; "to whoever reads this after."

---

**ACT II**

*The Interval Letter — with court receipt — Klausenburg, the main square, midday.*

A young man is waiting outside the archdeacon's court. His name is Edgar; he represents the Seward family's English estate interests in Transylvania. He heard the letter was being separated from the dossier materials and has come to object.

"The letter was submitted as part of the Van Helsing compilation. It belongs with the other compiled documents. Separating it damages the coherence of the case record." He is speaking in the register of a man who has rehearsed this argument but does not fully believe it. He has a document: a letter from the Seward estate solicitor claiming the right to decide how the compiled materials are organized.

*— He is wrong about the letter's category. The Van Helsing compilation derives its strength from being assembled from independent sources. The Interval Letter is valuable precisely because it is not part of the compilation — it was written before the compilation existed. Filing it with the compiled dossier would subordinate the primary source to the secondary assembly.*

> **Skill Check: History DC 12.** Establish that the Interval Letter predates the compilation — it was written before Van Helsing assembled the dossier; filing it with the compilation would treat the primary source as one item in a secondary assembly, which is the opposite of what the compilation's evidentiary logic requires.

**Fail:** Edgar's solicitor's letter creates a forty-eight hour hold pending estate review. The estate review confirms the Fighter's argument by the second morning.

**Pass:** Edgar pauses. The argument is new to him. He consults his own notes. "Before the compilation." "Yes." He folds the solicitor's letter back. He adds a notation to his own file: *independent document, pre-compilation; Seward estate claim withdrawn — letter does not belong to dossier; Edgar R., Seward estate representative.* He gives the Fighter a copy.

**TOKEN STATE 2**: Seward estate claim withdrawn — pre-compilation independent document notation added to the receipt.

---

**ACT III**

*The Interval Letter — receipt with two notations — Sibiu, Romanian Orthodox church scholar's study, evening.*

Father Vasile received word of the document from a colleague at the Klausenburg archdeacon's court. He is a compassionate man and a careful canon lawyer. He has been thinking about this document for three days.

"A letter written by a baptized Christian woman in the knowledge that she might lose herself constitutes a testament in the canonical sense — a statement made in extremis, before expected death or transformation. The church has jurisdiction over testaments of the baptized, and this letter meets the formal requirements." He opens his hands. "I am not seeking to suppress it. I am seeking to receive it correctly."

*— He is not wrong about the canonical definition. He is wrong about whether a testament must be filed under ecclesiastical jurisdiction merely because it meets the canonical definition; a letter written to "whoever reads this after" is not a testament in the sense of disposing of property or making spiritual provision — it is a record of a state of mind and a moment in time.*

> **Skill Check: Persuasion DC 13.** Distinguish between a canonical testament (disposing of property and soul) and a document written in the knowledge of possible loss — the Interval Letter makes no spiritual provision, disposes of nothing, and asks for nothing; it records; a document that records a state of mind is a witness statement, not a testament, even if written at a moment of extremis.

**Fail:** Father Vasile files a preliminary testament classification with the diocesan office. The diocesan canon lawyer reviews it and responds within two days: the letter does not meet the property/spiritual-provision test for a canonical testament. The classification is rescinded.

**Pass:** Father Vasile thinks for a long moment. He is working through his own canon law from memory. "A testament disposes. This letter records." A pause. "You are right." He writes his own endorsement: *reviewed — not subject to canonical testament classification; document type: state-of-mind record, witness statement in extremis; no ecclesiastical jurisdiction claim; Father Vasile Gheorghe, Romanian Orthodox, Sibiu.* He hands it to the Fighter.

**TOKEN STATE 3**: Orthodox canon law endorsement added — *not a testament, no ecclesiastical jurisdiction; state-of-mind record at moment of extremis.*

**ACT IV**

*The Interval Letter — receipt with three notations — road from Sibiu northwest toward the Transylvanian road, night.*

Two men intercept the Fighter at the mountain road. They are employed by a Transylvanian legal firm representing the descendants of the Carfax property transaction — the English solicitor's family whose papers completed the deed. They are not here because of the letter's content; they are here because the letter, if it establishes that Dracula existed and acted with full intention, retroactively makes the property transfer voidable as fraud. They want it gone.

*— They are not wrong about the legal implication. They are very wrong about their approach.*

> **Combat: 2 opponents — AC 12, HP 20 each.** They want the satchel, not blood. They break off at half health.

**Fail:** They take the letter. The legal firm's office is in Sibiu; Father Vasile's endorsement naming the incident is the beginning of the recovery chain.

**Pass:** Both down or broken. Road northwest clear. Three notations intact.

**TOKEN STATE 4**: Carried through property-transaction agents' ambush. All notations undamaged.

**ACT V**

*The Interval Letter — receipt with three notations — Weimar archive, the desk of Archivus Sweelinck, morning.*

Sweelinck reads the letter. He reads it twice — not for the content (he processes the content quickly) but for the quality of attention it requires. He sets it down.

"She wrote this not knowing if she would be herself when it was read." He is quiet for a moment. "She is fully herself when she writes it. That is — visible. The handwriting is steady. The thought is clear. She knows exactly what she is saying." He looks up. "What does an archive call a document written by a person who is still themselves, but knows they may not be when it arrives?"

*— He has the category question exactly right. The Fighter needs to give him the name.*

> **Skill Check: History DC 11.** Tell Sweelinck the category: Documents of the Final Interval — written in the knowledge of possible self-loss; the value is not testimony or evidence or testament but the record of a person's voice at the moment when it is most fully itself, because there is nothing left to protect. The archive's function here is to preserve the interval, not to adjudicate what followed.

**Fail:** Sweelinck files it under "Personal Testimonials — Extraordinary Circumstances" and marks it for further classification review. He revisits the category after a conversation with his senior colleague; the correct name is created an hour later.

**Pass:** Sweelinck writes: *Documents of the Final Interval — written in the knowledge of possible self-loss; voice at its most unguarded; the archive receives the interval, not the judgment of what followed.* He files the letter in its own section.

He says:

"She was still herself. That's what this is."

**TOKEN TAKEN: The Interval Letter**

The folded sheet enters the Weimar archive under Documents of the Final Interval. Petronela's court receipt, Edgar's pre-compilation notation, and Father Vasile's canon law endorsement are indexed separately. The ring impression in dark wax is preserved intact. Mina Murray is the first entry in her category.
