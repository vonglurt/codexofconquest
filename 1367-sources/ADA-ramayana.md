## Pass 1 — Overview

The Ramayana is one of the two great Hindu epics (the other being the Mahabharata). Valmiki's Sanskrit original comprises approximately 24,000 couplets in seven books (kandas). Griffith's 1870 verse translation renders the first six books in English rhymed couplets, preserving the epic's ceremonial register.

The story follows Rama, prince of Ayodhya and avatar of Vishnu, from birth through his exile, the abduction of his wife Sita by the demon-king Ravana, the war to recover her, and his eventual return to rule. The epic is simultaneously a narrative of dharma (right action), devotion, kingship, and loyalty — with Rama as the ideal man and Sita as the ideal wife, both tested to the limits of endurance.

The Ramayana contains several features with no parallel in other sources processed: the origin myth of poetry itself (the first sloka composed from grief), the trial-by-bow bride-price, Kaikeyi's deferred boon as a political weapon, the leap of Hanuman across the sea, and Sita's fire ordeal as proof of chastity that nonetheless fails to protect her.

Part 1 of 12 covers: the epic's frame (Valmiki's invocation, Brahma's commission, Narad's synopsis), the mythology of the setting (Dasharatha's horse sacrifice, Vishnu's incarnation plan, Ganga's descent), and the beginning of Book I proper (Rama's birth, Vishwamitra's visit, the killing of Tadaka, celestial weapons given to Rama, the march toward Mithila). The full story arc is recovered from Canto I's complete synopsis.

## Pass 2 — Structure

### The Six Books (Kandas) — Story Arc

**Book I: Bala Kanda (Book of Youth)**
Valmiki composes the epic after Brahma's commission. Narad summarizes the whole story. Ayodhya described: a gleaming city, King Dasharatha childless and grieving. He performs the Asvamedha (horse sacrifice); the sage Rishyasring officiates; a divine being emerges from the sacrificial fire bearing nectar for the three queens. Each drinks; Kaushalya bears Rama (Vishnu's avatar, half his essence), Kaikeyi bears Bharat (one quarter), Sumitra bears twins Lakshmana and Shatrughna (one quarter between them).

The sage Vishwamitra arrives requesting Rama to guard his sacrifice from demons. Dasharatha is terrified, tries to refuse. Vasishtha persuades him. Rama and Lakshmana depart with Vishwamitra: they receive the spells Bala and Atibala. They kill the demoness Tadaka in the Forest of Tadaka. Vishwamitra gives Rama 36 named celestial weapons. They travel toward Mithila, hearing long mythological stories en route (Ganga's descent, Sagara's 60,000 sons, Bhagiratha's penance).

At Mithila, King Janak displays the bow of Shiva: an iron bow so heavy it cannot be moved by demons, gods, or ordinary men. Janak has vowed his daughter Sita to the man who can string it. Rama lifts, strings, and breaks it. The earth shakes. Sita is given to Rama in marriage. Lakshmana weds Urmila. Rama's other brothers also marry Janak's nieces. The weddings are celebrated. On the road home, Rama defeats Parashurama (a different Rama, bearer of Vishnu's bow), who tests him; Rama proves himself the true avatar.

**Book II: Ayodhya Kanda (Book of Ayodhya)**
Dasharatha prepares Rama's coronation. The minister Manthara poisons Kaikeyi's mind: if Bharat is king, Kaikeyi's son rules; if Rama is king, Kaushalya's line holds power. Kaikeyi uses two boons promised by Dasharatha long ago on a battlefield where she saved his life: first, Bharat on the throne; second, Rama exiled 14 years into the forest. Dasharatha is shattered but cannot refuse — the boons are sacred debts. Rama accepts the exile without bitterness, departs with Sita and Lakshmana. Dasharatha dies of grief. Bharat returns, is horrified, pursues Rama to the forest to beg him to return. Rama refuses — the king's word is sacred. Bharat takes Rama's sandals and places them on the throne, ruling as regent in Rama's name, living austerely in a small village outside Ayodhya, refusing to sit on the throne himself.

**Book III: Aranya Kanda (Book of the Forest)**
Fourteen years in the Dandaka Forest. Rama and Lakshmana visit hermits, kill demons. The demoness Surpanakha falls in love with Rama, is disfigured by Lakshmana. Her brothers Khara and Dushana attack with armies; Rama kills 14,000 demons. Ravana, hearing of this, is enraged. He traps Rama and Lakshmana by sending the magic deer Maricha to lure them away, then abducts Sita in their absence. The vulture Jatayu tries to stop Ravana and is mortally wounded. Rama finds Jatayu dying and learns the direction of Sita's abduction. Rama and Lakshmana, lost and grieving, encounter the demon Kabandha, kill him, and release his trapped spirit — Kabandha counsels Rama to seek alliance with Sugriva the monkey-chief.

**Book IV: Kishkindha Kanda (Book of Kishkindha)**
Rama meets Hanuman on the way to Kishkindha. Alliance with Sugriva: Rama kills Bali (Sugriva's brother who took his wife and throne) with an arrow from ambush. Sugriva is restored to his kingdom and his wife. The four quarters of the earth are searched for Sita. The bird Sampati (Jatayu's brother) reports Sita is in Lanka across the sea. Hanuman is identified as the one who can leap the sea — son of the Wind God, unrestricted in size and speed.

**Book V: Sundara Kanda (Book of Beauty)**
Hanuman leaps 200 leagues to Lanka. He finds Sita under an Ashoka tree, tormented but refusing to give up. He gives her Rama's ring; she gives him a gem from her hair. He destroys the Ashoka grove and kills five of Ravana's captains. He allows himself to be captured so he can confront Ravana; his tail is set on fire; he burns the city and escapes. He returns to Rama with Sita's token.

**Book VI: Lanka Kanda (Book of Lanka) / Yuddha Kanda**
Rama approaches the sea. The Ocean God appears and agrees to hold still if a bridge can be built. Nala (son of the divine craftsman Vishvakarma) builds a stone bridge. The monkey army crosses. Ravana's brother Vibhishana defects to Rama. Battle: Kumbhakarna (Ravana's giant brother) is killed; Meghanada (Ravana's son, who earlier had defeated Indra himself) is killed by Lakshmana. Ravana comes to battle; after a long duel Rama kills him with the divine arrow of Brahma. Vibhishana is crowned king of Lanka. Sita is brought out — Rama, publicly, under pressure to demonstrate she is not compromised, asks her to prove her purity. Sita walks into fire. The Fire God Agni brings her out unharmed and certifies her chastity. Rama reclaims her, returns to Ayodhya in the flying chariot Pushpaka, meets Bharat, is crowned king. The Golden Age begins.

### Part 1 Detailed Content (Lines 1–597)

**Cantos I–IV (Lines 62–144):** Invocation to Valmiki. Narad's complete synopsis of the entire story. Brahma's commission of the poem. The rhapsodists Kusha and Lava, Valmiki's twin sons, perform the epic in Rama's own court.

**Cantos V–VII (Lines 149–178):** Ayodhya described. King Dasharatha and his court.

**Cantos VIII–X (Lines 179–210):** Sumantra's speech about the prophecy. Rishyasring the forest-hermit lured to civilization by dancing girls; he marries Shanta; officiates at Dasharatha's sacrifice.

**Cantos XI–XIII (Lines 213–234):** The horse sacrifice fully described. 21 stakes, 300 victims, three queens touch the horse. Rishyasring promises Dasharatha four sons.

**Cantos XIV–XV (Lines 239–270):** The gods petition Brahma to stop Ravana. Vishnu agrees to incarnate. The divine nectar descends from the fire; the queens drink. Each queen conceives.

**Canto XVI (Lines 275–280):** The Vanaras (monkey warriors) created by the gods to serve Vishnu-Rama in battle — each born of a god, each possessing divine power.

**Cantos XVII–XIX (Lines 285–322):** Rishyasring departs. The four princes are born. Rama described: lotus-eyed, dark, with all auspicious marks. He and Lakshmana are inseparable from childhood.

**Cantos XX–XXIV (Lines 327–366):** Vishwamitra arrives. Demands Rama to guard his sacrifice. Dasharatha's terror; Vasishtha persuades him. Vishwamitra reveals the demons are servants of Ravana.

**Cantos XXIV–XXXII (Lines 371–462):** Rama and Lakshmana depart. Bala and Atibala spells given. The Forest of Love (Anga). The Forest of Tadaka — the demoness's origin story. Tadaka killed by Rama. The celestial arms (36 named weapons) given by Vishwamitra to Rama. The Retreat of Vishnu (Perfect Hermitage). Vishwamitra's sacrifice: six days of watching; Marica and Subahu attack; Marica thrown 100 leagues out to sea; Subahu killed.

**Cantos XXXIII–XLIV (Lines 467–596):** March toward Mithila. The river Sona. Vishwamitra's lineage told. The birth of Ganga: Himavat's daughter given to the gods, then made to descend to earth through Bhagiratha's penance. The legend of Sagara's 60,000 sons (they dig through the earth, are burned by Kapila, their ashes must be washed by Ganga). Bhagiratha's thousand-year penance. Shiva agrees to hold Ganga in his hair.

## ADA-01 Full Vignette Spec — "The Curlew's Grief"

### Source Hook

In the Ramayana's second canto, the sage Valmiki walks to the river Tamasa to bathe. He sees two curlews (krauncha birds) mating by the water. A hunter shoots the male. The female cries out. The sage is seized by sudden grief and, involuntarily, four lines of verse form in his mouth — the first sloka ever spoken. He is amazed: the word sloka (verse) and the word shoka (grief) share a root. Brahma confirms this was intentional: the first poem in the world was born from witnessed suffering.

A medieval Persian scholar, reading a summary of this story, writes a commentary arguing that the same impulse underlies the origin myths of Persian ghazal (which the poet Rudaki describes as emerging from loss), Arabic elegy (marthiya), and the Psalms of David. The commentary is a philosophical treatise, not a religious claim. He wants to deliver it to a poet in Tabriz who is compiling exactly this kind of cross-tradition study.

---

### Logistics

**Year:** 1367
**Territory:** Jalayirid Sultanate (northwest Persia / Azeri highlands)
**Commission city:** Sultaniya (a former Ilkhanid capital, still a center of Persian intellectual life in 1367; about 200 km southwest of Tabriz)
**Delivery city:** Tabriz (the Jalayirid capital; home of Persian poets, astronomers, and manuscript scholars)
**Road distance:** ~200 km, 5–6 days by horse; passes through Qazvin-area hill country, then into the Azeri plain
**Dangers:** Jalayirid bandit activity on the Qazvin road; Chagatai raider incursion from the northeast; competing scholarly factions (another scholar wants the text discredited before it reaches Tabriz)

---

### Cast

**Commissioning party — Scholar Musa of Sultaniya:** A Persian-Armenian scholar, aged 60, who has spent 30 years studying Indian manuscripts that reached Persia through trade routes. He does not travel; he sends. He is known and respected in Sultaniya but has a rival in Tabriz (see below). He uses a reed-pen seal. He is precise, slightly anxious, very proud of the work.

**Delivery recipient — Poet Kamal of Tabriz:** A Persian master poet, 45 years old, compiling a multi-tradition treatise on grief as the source of lyric poetry. He has been waiting for exactly this kind of cross-cultural argument. He is well-connected at the Jalayirid court. He will authenticate the delivery by returning the TOKEN.

**Obstacle 1 — Qazvin tollman Farhad:** Controls a mountain road junction. He has been bribed by Musa's rival to delay all messengers from Sultaniya by 3 days (claiming customs inspection). The delay would cause the manuscript to arrive after a court session where Musa's rival can establish prior authorship of the same argument.

**Obstacle 2 — Scholar Rumi of Tabriz (the rival):** Not named in game text, referred to only as "the other man." He has his own partial version of the cross-tradition argument and will reach Kamal before the manuscript does if Farhad succeeds in causing the delay. He has agents watching the road.

**Obstacle 3 (Act 4) — Chagatai raider outrider:** A lone scout from a raiding party that hit a village east of Tabriz two days ago. He is wounded, disoriented, dangerous. He interprets the carrier's approach as a threat.

**Obstacle 4 (Act 5) — Kamal's gate-keeper at the poet's house in Tabriz:** Kamal is at a court recitation and not home. The gate-keeper has orders to receive no documents without the poet's personal presence. The delay is genuine — the gate-keeper is not corrupt — but the rival's agent is now also at the gate, watching. The carrier must wait, act, or find Kamal at the court.

---

### TOKEN

**Object:** A small cedarwood writing case, sealed with red wax and a reed-pen impression (Musa's personal seal). Inside: the rolled manuscript. The wax seal must be intact when delivered. If broken in transit, Kamal cannot authenticate it as Musa's original submission.

**Granted:** Act 1 — Musa places the sealed case in the carrier's hands at his house in Sultaniya.
**Checked:** Acts 2, 3, 4 — the seal is intact and the case is present.
**Taken:** Act 5 — Kamal breaks the seal in the carrier's presence, reads the first paragraph aloud, nods, and sets the case down. The cedarwood case stays; the carrier is dismissed with thanks and a coin.

### Five-Act Structure

#### Act 1 — Commission (Sultaniya, Scholar Musa's house)

**Scene:** Musa's study, morning. Maps, Indian manuscript fragments, a smell of camphor. Musa is agitated — he has just heard his rival in Tabriz has been working on a similar argument and is close to completing it. Musa's commentary is finished; he needs it in Tabriz before the next court session (four days hence).

**Skill check (DC 12 — Insight or Persuasion):** Musa is trying to send the carrier immediately but is also trying to give three different sets of instructions at once and contradicting himself. The carrier must get a single clear directive from him: which gate at Tabriz, what name to ask for, what phrase proves the package is from Musa (the phrase is the opening line of the manuscript — "Grief made the word, and the word was grief").

**Pass:** The carrier leaves with the sealed case, the delivery phrase, and a clear road description. Musa gives a small advance on the fee.
**Fail:** The carrier leaves without the delivery phrase. When they reach Tabriz, Kamal's gate-keeper will not accept the package from an unknown messenger with no authentication. The carrier must improvise in Act 5.

**TOKEN granted:** The cedarwood case is placed in the carrier's hands.

---

#### Act 2 — The Qazvin Junction (hill road, day 2)

**Scene:** A narrow road junction in the foothills. Farhad the tollman has a painted wooden post and a canvas shelter. He is professionally friendly, regretful, produces documents. A three-day customs inspection is required for "manuscripts of foreign origin." He cannot be bribed directly — he has already been paid and is afraid of who paid him.

**Skill check (DC 12 — Deception or Persuasion):**
- Deception: The manuscript is not a "manuscript of foreign origin" — it is a Persian text written by a Persian scholar who was born in Sultaniya. It has no foreign words in it. (True. The commentary is in Persian.) If the carrier argues this persuasively, Farhad has no grounds.
- Persuasion: Ask to see the customs regulation that defines "foreign origin manuscript." Farhad does not have one — he has a bribe and a story. Patient insistence breaks it.
- Athletics or Intimidation (alternative): Do not engage. The road is narrow but there is a goat trail around the post visible from here. Farhad is a bureaucrat, not a fighter.

**Pass:** The carrier is waved through immediately or after 2 hours, not 3 days. Full fee waived.
**Fail:** Three-day delay begins. The carrier must find another route (goat trail — harder travel but only adds 6 hours) or accept the delay and know the rival will arrive first.

---

#### Act 3 — The Rival's Outrider (plain below Tabriz, day 3)

**Scene:** A man on a mule, riding fast, passes the carrier going the same direction. He looks back, recognizes the Sultaniya road, slows. He is the rival scholar's messenger — sent to carry a rumor, not a document: a rumor that Musa of Sultaniya plagiarized the cross-tradition argument from an older manuscript (false, but damaging if it reaches Kamal's ears first).

**Skill check (DC 12 — Perception or Insight):** Notice the outrider's saddlebag is empty (he has no manuscript; he is carrying only a spoken message) and that he is looking at the carrier's case with specific attention.

**Pass:** The carrier understands what is happening. The outrider can be outpaced, diverted (sent to the wrong gate in Tabriz), or confronted. If confronted, the outrider folds quickly — he is not important, just paid.
**Fail:** The outrider reaches Kamal's house first and delivers the rumor. In Act 5, Kamal will have heard it and will be colder at reception. The authentication phrase (from Act 1) now matters more — it proves Musa's text is original.

---

#### Act 4 — The Chagatai Scout (eastern approach to Tabriz, late day 3)

**Scene:** A wounded man in partial riding gear, sitting against a wall at the edge of a village east of Tabriz that was hit by a raiding party two days ago. He is Chagatai, disoriented from blood loss, and has a knife out. He has been waiting because he cannot ride yet and fears capture. He sees the carrier approaching and reads it as a threat.

**Skill check (DC 12 — Medicine or Animal Handling or Persuasion):**
- Medicine: The wound is infected; he will die in 2 days without treatment. If the carrier acknowledges this calmly and offers to help (bandaging, water), the situation de-escalates.
- Animal Handling: His horse is tethered 20 meters away; the horse is calm. Approaching the horse first, slowly, signals non-aggression.
- Persuasion: The carrier does not speak Chagatai, but the man speaks some Persian. Simple words, open hands.

**Pass:** The scout stands down. He asks the carrier which road avoids the garrison (he wants to get out of Jalayirid territory). The carrier points east. The man limps to his horse and goes.
**Fail:** Combat or flight. The TOKEN is not damaged — the case is sealed and sturdy — but the carrier arrives in Tabriz shaken and late. Act 5 begins after dark rather than at the afternoon court session.

---

#### Act 5 — Delivery (Tabriz, Kamal's house or the court)

**Scene:** Kamal is not home. His gate-keeper is polite, firm, and has standing orders: no documents accepted without the poet's personal presence. The rival's outrider (whether or not the carrier succeeded in Act 3) is sitting across the street.

**Approach A — Find Kamal at the court recitation:** Kamal is performing at a Jalayirid court session in the palace district. The carrier must get through the outer court gate (a guard who wants a name on the access list, which the carrier is not on) and into the recitation hall.

**Skill check (DC 12 — Persuasion or Deception):**
- Persuasion: State clearly that the carrier is a private messenger from Scholar Musa of Sultaniya with a time-critical scholarly document for the poet Kamal, and that it concerns the treatise on poetic origins that Kamal has been expecting. This is all true. The guard has seen couriers before.
- Deception: Present as a servant of a court official (Musa has one connection at court, mentioned in passing in Act 1).

**Approach B — Wait at the house:** If the carrier waits, Kamal returns by evening. But the rival's outrider has now also been waiting and will attempt to speak to Kamal first.

**Pass (either approach):** Kamal receives the case, breaks the seal, reads the opening paragraph, hears the authentication phrase if the carrier uses it. He nods. He dismisses the outrider with a single word. He asks the carrier to wait while he writes a receipt for Musa.

**Fail:** Kamal hears the plagiarism rumor from the outrider and is cautious. The carrier must produce the authentication phrase (if obtained in Act 1) or describe the specific argument of the manuscript (requiring the carrier to have learned it in Act 1). Without these, Kamal accepts the manuscript but files it provisionally, pending his own investigation. Musa gets paid, but with a delay.

**TOKEN taken:** Kamal breaks the seal in the carrier's presence. He does not return the case — he keeps it as the authenticated container. The carrier is paid.
