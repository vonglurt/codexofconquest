Pass 4 on KYA-shah-nameh added five source summaries and five new vignette cycles (26–30) to KYA-shah-nameh.md. Cycle 26 — "The Portrait in the Gallery" (Part 1: Jemshíd's flight to Zábulistán) uses the gallery portrait the princess of Gúreng's court fetched to verify the stranger's identity, routing through Herat, Merv, Nishapur, Trebizond, and Weimar; the archive opens the category Identification Records — Images Used in Acts of Recognition That Did Not Resolve. Cycle 27 — "Gúrd-afríd's Broken Lance" (Part 2: the Sohráb campaign) uses the lance fragment Gúrd-afríd cut from her own girdle belt after Sohráb's spear pierced it, routing through Sistan, Yazd, Nishapur, Tabriz, and Trebizond; the archive opens Combat Records — Weapons Recovered After Inconclusive Engagements, with the oral-tradition detail that she laughed when she got inside the walls. Cycle 28 — "The Khakán's Collar" (Part 3: the great wars against Túrán) uses the golden torque Rustem demanded as tribute from the Khakán of Chín, routing through Samarkand, Merv, Constantinople, and Trebizond; the archive opens Tribute Records and Their Displacements — Objects Claimed by Epic Tradition That Predate the Claim. Cycle 29 — "The Merchant Kherád" (Part 4: the Brazen Fortress infiltration) uses the false clay trade permit Isfendiyár created for his merchant disguise, preserved by the gate-keeper Barzin whom it deceived, routing through Herat, Maragha, Tabriz, and Trebizond; the archive opens False Documents That Enabled Entry — Forgeries Preserved by Their Victims. Cycle 30 — "The Cup of Three Kings" (Part 5: the Divan of Hafiz) uses a folio by the Tabriz scholar Nasir al-Din Kashani who noticed that the Hafiz verse "Sikander's mirror, once so famed, is the wine-filled cup" connects to the Cup of Jemshíd and Alexander's four goblets from Dárá's table — all three in the same KYA volume — and wrote the chain in a single hand; routing through Tabriz, Nishapur, Baghdad, Constantinople, and Weimar; the archive opens Acts of Reading That Produced the Archive — Connections Made by a Single Reader Across Multiple Works; questComplete: true on Act 5.
