# Quest Design: CLR-01 — "I Just Know This" (The Claircognizance Quest)

**Source:** Video transcript — *How to Identify Which Clair is Your Strongest*
**Quest prefix:** `clr_` | **Quest ID:** `clr_01_act1` through `clr_01_act5` | **questComplete:** `clrForestKnowing1Complete`
**Fighter role:** Token courier — carries the woman's collected testimonies from a forest clearing to the Weimar archive; tested three times along the route by situations that require knowing rather than seeing, hearing, or feeling
**Fighter approach:** Low-combat; all five acts are social or navigational — the challenge is recognizing and trusting the claircognizant sense rather than suppressing it in favor of investigation or perception

## TOKEN

**The Knowing-Without-Looking Testimonies** — a small leather satchel containing eleven accounts from travelers, merchants, a soldier, and one confessor priest, each recording a moment of certainty that preceded any visible evidence. The woman's preface note: *"Pay attention to the language. The word each person chooses — know, feel, see, hear — is the evidence. These eleven all used the same word."*

---

## Route

**GVW → road → road → WM**

---

## Theme

The knowing-sense is identified by language, not by content. *I just know this* marks claircognizance; *I feel this* marks clairsentience; *I see it this way* marks clairvoyance; *I heard something was wrong* marks clairaudience. The woman's project is a natural history: evidence that human cognition operates in a mode that requires no visible input. The archive receives it not as mystical testimony but as a document of language patterns — eleven people from different countries, professions, and centuries, each independently choosing the same word for the same experience.

The quest's meta-structure mirrors its source: the Fighter identifies their own strongest clair by paying attention to the word they use when they cannot explain how they know something. The three road tests are each designed to make claircognizance the only tool that works.

---

## Skill Sequence

- Investigation DC 12 — GVW: The woman asks how the Fighter found the clearing. The Fighter recalls the precise moment they stopped following the map and followed something else. The answer she is listening for: *"I knew."*
- Perception DC 11 — road: Walking with the woman to the hidden document cache; the forest path shifts direction without warning; the Fighter must stop checking and let their feet follow without verifying
- Insight DC 13 — road: Three-way crossroads, no signs, no tracks to read; the Fighter must wait for the knowing-sense to arrive rather than reason or investigate their way through
- Persuasion DC 12 — road: A merchant asks which road leads to market; the Fighter knows but cannot explain; must convince with conviction rather than argument
- History DC 12 — WM: Propose archive category — **Knowing Records: Cognition Without Visible Input**; the archivist asks how the Fighter categorized the collection without reading all eleven testimonies; the answer is: *"I paid attention to the language."*

## 5-Act Vignette Spec

### Act 1 — The Clearing (GVW) `[skill_check · Investigation DC 12]`

The Fighter arrives at a clearing that was not on any map. There is a house. Smoke from a chimney. A woman in the doorway who says: *"I wondered which word you would use."*

She has not been introduced to the Fighter. She does not ask their name or their purpose. She asks only: *"How did you find this place?"*

Four honest responses — the Fighter can answer or attempt a cover story:
- *"I followed the map."* — She says: *"The map was blank past the third oak."* She waits.
- *"I felt something pulling me here."* — She says: *"That is clairsentience. Useful. Not what I am testing for."*
- *"I saw a light between the trees."* — She says: *"There was no light. Try again."*
- *"I just knew."* (any variation) — She says: *"Yes. That one."* She goes inside to make tea.

Investigation DC 12: the Fighter accurately remembers the moment the map stopped working and something else took over — and recognizes that what took over was not a feeling, a vision, or a sound. It was a knowing. **Success:** she hands over the satchel immediately and says: *"Eleven accounts. Each from a different person. Each uses the same word. Deliver them to the archive at Weimar. Tell the archivist to pay attention to the language."* **Failure:** she says *"You are not ready yet — but you will be. Walk with me first."* She walks the Fighter to the document cache before departing. The satchel transfers regardless; the DC determines whether the Fighter already understands what they are carrying.

---

### Act 2 — The Cache Walk (road) `[skill_check · Perception DC 11]`

She walks the Fighter partway into the forest, pointing them toward the main road. The path changes direction three times. She does not look at the ground. She does not use landmarks.

She says: *"The ones who get lost in this forest are the ones who keep checking."*

- **Check the map** (Investigation): the path is not on it.
- **Watch her feet** (Perception DC 11): she moves without hesitation, following no visible trail. The Fighter sees that she is not navigating — she is knowing. **Success:** the Fighter follows without checking and arrives at the main road cleanly. They open the satchel on the road and read the first testimony: a merchant who knew his ship had sunk six days before the news arrived. His word: *"I simply knew."* **Failure:** the Fighter checks the map, loses the woman's position, and has to find the main road alone. No mechanical penalty — the Fighter arrives, and reads the same testimony. They just didn't see her walk.

---

### Act 3 — The Crossroads (road) `[skill_check · Insight DC 13]`

A three-way crossroads. No road signs. No other travelers. One set of tracks leading left — made a day ago, moving in the wrong direction. Whoever made them was also lost.

Three approaches:
- **Investigate** (read tracks, terrain, sun angle): leads to the wrong fork — the tracks were left by a disoriented traveler; the terrain and sun are ambiguous.
- **Wait** (Insight DC 13): stop all active cognition; let the knowing-sense arrive. **Success:** the Fighter takes the left fork without knowing why. Within a hundred paces, a familiar milestone from the original planned route confirms it. **Failure on wait:** the Fighter chooses incorrectly and must backtrack 20 minutes. The knowing was there; the Fighter talked themselves out of it. No permanent penalty — the correct road still waits.
- **Guess**: 1-in-3 chance of correct fork.

This act is designed so that Investigation actively misleads. The only reliable tool is stopping and waiting.

---

### Act 4 — The Merchant (road) `[skill_check · Persuasion DC 12]`

A merchant traveling the opposite direction stops the Fighter. He is looking for the market at the nearest town. He has tried both other forks and returned each time.

The Fighter knows which road leads to the market — the same knowing-sense that navigated the crossroads. But they have no visible reason to offer. No map. No prior experience of this road. No credible account.

The merchant asks: *"How do you know?"*

Persuasion DC 12: convince with conviction rather than argument. The Fighter must not claim a map or prior travel — the woman's lesson is that the knowing stands without justification. **Success:** the merchant pauses, then says: *"I don't know why I believe you."* He takes the road the Fighter indicated. **Failure:** the merchant tries the third fork anyway — but arrives at the market via the long way around, an hour late. A later traveler tells the Fighter. The knowing was correct. Only the argument failed.

---

### Act 5 — The Archive (WM) `[delivery · History DC 12]` `questComplete: clrForestKnowing1Complete`

The archivist at Weimar opens the leather satchel. He reads the first three testimonies silently. Then a fourth. He looks up.

*"These are from completely different people. Different centuries, different countries, different situations. What is the category?"*

History DC 12: the Fighter proposes — **Knowing Records: Cognition Without Visible Input.**

The archivist writes it. He pauses. *"How did you settle on 'knowing' rather than 'sensing' or 'feeling'?"*

The Fighter says: *"She told me to pay attention to the language."*

He opens the satchel fully and reads the preface note. He is quiet. Then he stamps it:

*RECEIVED. KNOWING RECORDS — COGNITION WITHOUT VISIBLE INPUT. ELEVEN TESTIMONIES. PROVENANCE: GREENWOOD WATCHER'S CLEARING, 1367.*

He asks: *"Do you know her name?"*

The Fighter thinks. She never said it. And they knew, at the time, that she wouldn't.

## Other Story Ideas — What We Could Do Next

These are the other angles the video opens up, each its own quest:

**CLR-02 — "The Four Languages"**
A scholar linguist has been traveling trade routes collecting traveler reports and sorting them by which word the person used — *know, feel, see, hear.* She has four stacks. She wants the Fighter to carry all four to Weimar separately, because the archive must receive them as distinct categories or the distinction is lost. A logistics quest: four routes, four drops, four different archivists who each argue their category is the most important. The mechanic: the Fighter must not mix the stacks even when it would be easier to deliver all four to one desk.

**CLR-03 — "The Silence Score"**
Clairaudience. A musician in a tavern can hear things others can't — not supernatural sounds, but the subtle harmonics in a room that tell him when someone is lying or when the weather is about to turn. He has notated "silence scores" — pieces that are mostly rests, with annotations about what the silences contain. He wants to donate them to Weimar under: *Auditory Records — The Information Content of Silence.* The road test: the Fighter must navigate a mountain pass by listening for what isn't there.

**CLR-04 — "The Unfinished Portrait"**
Clairvoyance. A painter in Florence finishes commissioned portraits before she meets the subjects — she sees them first. Eighteen completed. One of them was hanged six months after the portrait was finished. The nineteenth is unfinished: she stopped mid-face because she didn't like what she saw. The Fighter must decide whether to deliver the unfinished nineteenth to Weimar before the subject is located — or wait. A morality beat about whether the archive should receive a document that may be a warning.

**CLR-05 — "The Comparison Problem"**
Two scholars have been arguing for a decade about whose abilities are stronger. The woman from the clearing (she appears again) has written a mediating document: comparison is categorically invalid — each clair pattern is uniquely configured; the only valid comparison is each person against their own prior state. TOKEN: *The Non-Comparison Argument* — her formal position that the question "whose is strongest" is the wrong question, and that what makes each person's configuration valuable is precisely its difference from everyone else's. Archive category: *Clair Development Records — The Invalidity of Cross-Person Comparison.*

**CLR-06 — "The Changing Levels"**
A scholar has been logging their own four clairs daily for a full year — which was strongest on which day, what they were doing, what changed. The journal shows shifts: clairsentience peaked during grief, claircognizance during travel, clairaudience during music study, clairvoyance during illness. TOKEN: *The Development Journal — Clair Shift Patterns, 1366–1367.* The fighter delivers it. The archivist's question: *"What does this prove?"* The answer: *"That the levels are always changing. What is strongest now may not be strongest in six months. The journal is not a ranking. It is a map of movement."*
