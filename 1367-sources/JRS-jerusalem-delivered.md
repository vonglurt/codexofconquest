# JRS — Jerusalem Delivered (Gerusalemme Liberata)
**Author:** Torquato Tasso (1544–1595)
**Translator:** Edward Fairfax (1560–1635); translation first published London 1600
**Source file:** JRS-jerusalem-delivered.txt (Gutenberg #392, 237 KB — read in full per ≤400 KB rule)
**Written:** c. 1575, published Parma 1581
**Category:** Renaissance Christian Epic, 20 Books in ottava rima

---

## Pass 1 — Full Telling

Tasso wrote *Jerusalem Delivered* during one of the most troubled creative lives in literary history. He completed the poem in draft around 1575 but spent the next years revising it under pressure from ecclesiastical censors who worried about its orthodoxy — its demons, enchantresses, and the ambiguous piety of its most passionate characters. In 1579, under circumstances still disputed, he was imprisoned in the asylum of Sant'Anna in Ferrara, where he remained for seven years. The poem that obsessed him had always been about what faith costs: not just blood, but the full inventory of the self.

The story begins in the sixth year of the First Crusade. God looks down from heaven, sees that the holy city remains untaken while his chosen commanders have dispersed into comfortable regional kingdoms, and sends the angel Gabriel to wake Godfrey of Bouillon. The message is simple: the siege has stalled; you are the commander; finish it. Godfrey calls a council, is unanimously elected leader, and the march on Jerusalem begins.

From the first pages, the poem runs on two registers simultaneously. The divine and infernal manage events from above and below: God assigns angels; Satan dispatches demons; the sorcerer Ismen is the devil's agent among the Saracens; Peter the Hermit is God's voice among the Christians. Godfrey is the instrument of Providence — a man of such consistent virtue that Armida's enchantments cannot touch him. Against him stands Aladine, the aged tyrant of Jerusalem, governed by fear and spite; advised by Ismen; defended by the great Pagan warriors Argantes and Solyman. The tension between the poem's earthly and supernatural registers is unresolved to the last page. Providence is clearly operative. The humans it operates through are just as clearly broken, passionate, and magnificent.

**Sophronia and Olindo (Book II)**: Ismen steals the Virgin's icon from the Christian church and hides it in a mosque, hoping its magic will protect the city. When the icon disappears from the mosque, Aladine accuses the Christians of reclaiming it and orders a massacre. A young woman named Sophronia — admired by everyone, desired by no one more than Olindo — goes to the king and falsely confesses to stealing the icon herself, to save the community. Olindo, who has never told her he loves her, immediately runs forward and claims he did it, trying to take her death. The king orders both burned. They stand at the stake — Sophronia composed, Olindo weeping not for himself but that she refused him even in dying — when Clorinda rides by and stops the execution. She wins their pardon by promising the king her military service. Sophronia and Olindo marry and vanish from the poem. The lie saved everyone. Olindo declared his love in the only grammar the situation offered: claiming her crime. She heard it, and lived.

**Tancred and Clorinda (Books III–XII)**: Tancred, the finest Christian knight after Rinaldo, falls in love with Clorinda when he first sees her resting by a stream, helmet off, golden hair loose. He sees only a face before she puts the helmet back. He spends the next nine books loving a woman he knows only in armor, who fights him three times and escapes twice. The third fight is in darkness. Clorinda has disguised herself in black armor to burn Godfrey's great siege tower. She succeeds — the tower falls burning — but she is locked out of Jerusalem when the gates close too soon. Tancred pursues her through the dark, thinking her a Pagan warrior. They fight to exhaustion. He drives his sword into her breast. She asks, dying, for baptism — "a spirit new did her those prayers teach, spirit of hope, of charity, and faith." He fills his helmet from a stream, baptizes her, and removes her visor. He sees her face. He all but dies of grief. She smiles: "I die in peace."

Before she went out that last night, her old eunuch guardian Arsetes had told her the truth of her birth for the first time: that she was the white-born daughter of the Black Christian queen of Ethiopia, who had prayed to St. George for a child and prayed for his protection; that she had been given to Arsetes as an infant to be carried away and baptized; that Arsetes had carried her through wars and wildernesses for thirty years and never delivered her to the font; that on the night of her birth a champion in shining armor had appeared in a dream and charged him to baptize the child — and he had deferred and deferred. Clorinda received this news. She had dreamed twice that night of dying. She went out anyway. What the eunuch had deferred thirty years, Tancred performed in a stream with his helmet. The vow was kept — by the worst possible hand, at the worst possible hour, and she died his handmaid.

**Erminia and Tancred (Books III, VI–VII, XIX)**: Erminia, princess of Antioch, fell in love with Tancred when he was her captor in the aftermath of Antioch's fall. He treated her with such honor that she loved him without hope of return. She escaped with the Crusader army because she could not leave him. She watches him from Jerusalem's walls, pointing him out to the king, her speech disguising love as hate: "Tancred he hight — would Macon grant that I might hold him thrall." When Tancred is badly wounded by Argantes, Erminia steals Clorinda's silver armor from the armory — everyone who sees it in the moonlight believes it's Clorinda and clears a path — and rides through the Christian camp at night to heal him. A patrol gives chase. She is lost. She ends up among shepherds in a green valley, carves her love into the bark of trees, and lives there months in pastoral grief. A spy named Vafrine eventually finds her. The poem ends with Erminia at Tancred's side, healing his wounds from the final battle: the love deferred through the whole poem arriving in the last canto, quietly, without ceremony.

**Rinaldo and Armida (Books IV, XIV–XVI, XX)**: Armida is a sorceress sent by the infernal powers to seduce the Christian army. She arrives at the camp with a false story of persecution by a usurper uncle, requests ten knights as an escort, and makes every knight in the army desperate to be chosen. She enchants ten, then draws others who follow unbidden. She eventually captures Rinaldo — the poem's strongest warrior — and keeps him in a garden island of enchanted bliss: music, fruit, water, Armida's arms. Two knights, Ubaldo and Carlo, are sent to retrieve him. They carry an enchanted shield, clear as a mirror, and when Rinaldo is alone in the garden they hold it before him. He sees himself: his sword wreathed in flowers, his armor neglected, his face idle and soft. He wakes in shame and leaves without a word. Armida pursues him crying, offering to be his slave, his servant, his prisoner. He pities her but does not turn back. At the poem's final battle, Armida arrives on the field to kill him with arrows. The shield deflects them. She falls from her horse. He catches her. She says: "You have conquered." She converts to Christianity. Whether this is love or defeat or both, the poem does not say.

**The Enchanted Forest and the Final Siege (Books XIII, XVIII–XX)**: Ismen enchants the forest from which the Crusaders need timber for siege engines. Every knight who enters hears his worst fear given a voice and a body. Tancred enters and sees Clorinda's blood pouring from every tree he cuts; he cannot continue. Rinaldo alone breaks through: he walks in, hears Armida's voice from the trees begging him to stop, and keeps cutting. The illusion dissolves when he refuses it. In the final assault, after weeks of battle and supernatural intervention — divine wind driving back the incendiary fires; angel Michael visible to Godfrey alone — Rinaldo scales the wall, Godfrey plants the cross on the battlements, and Jerusalem falls. Ismen is killed by a stone from the siege engine. Argantes dies at Tancred's hands in a private duel in a small valley outside the city — both men exhausted, the Saracen refusing to yield, receiving the killing blow while still saying the battle was not over. Godfrey enters the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in full armor and kneels at the tomb, weaponed, weeping.

The poem's abiding subject is love's collision with duty — and the specific tragedy that the most loving people in it (Tancred, Erminia, Armida, Sophronia, Arsetes) cannot time their love correctly. They are always too early, or too late, or in the wrong armor, or carrying the wrong thing. The divine machinery runs perfectly. The human machinery fails at the joints where feeling lives.

## Pass 2 — Character Deep-Dives

**Godfrey of Bouillon**: The instrument of Providence. His virtue is so consistent that enchantment cannot grip him — not from coldness but from fullness; he has no unfulfilled desire that can be leveraged. He manages the army's grief and faction with patient competence. When Rinaldo kills Gernando and must leave, Godfrey chooses political pragmatism over justice, which is not heroic but is wise. He kneels at the end in full battle armor — no time to remove it, too much still to do — and that image condenses him exactly.

**Tancred**: The poem's emotional center. He is the best of the Christian knights in character as well as arms — honorable, generous, gentle — and the poem repays him by making him kill what he loves. His grief after Clorinda is total and permanent. He fights Argantes at the end not to win but to find a frame large enough to hold what he has done. He does win. It gives him nothing. Erminia arrives and heals him and perhaps that is enough; the poem doesn't say.

**Clorinda**: Born to a Christian queen who prayed for a miracle; protected through thirty years of war by a saint whose name she never heard; baptized in the moment of her death by the man who inflicted the death. She goes out knowing the warning dream and disregards it. Her final words — "I die in peace" — are the poem's most earned line. She has been given what she was always owed at the worst possible moment and receives it as grace.

**Arsetes**: The old eunuch who carried Clorinda from infancy. He had one charge: baptize the child. He deferred it for thirty years. He followed her through every war. He told her the truth on the last night, which she received calmly, and then watched her walk out to die the death the dream had promised. He has also been carrying something else — the Ethiopian queen's sealed letter of dedication to the St. George shrine, the mother's half of the vow — which he has never delivered, because to deliver it would mean putting Clorinda down, ending the commission, and he could not do it while she lived. Now she is dead. The letter is still undelivered.

**Rinaldo**: The poem's strongest warrior and its most spectacular case of redeemed failure. He is fifteen when he arrives; kills a man in a camp argument; is sent away in disgrace; is enchanted by Armida and kept in a garden for months. The shield that shows him his own reflection is the poem's most efficient act of mercy — one image, one moment of recognition, and he is restored. He breaks the enchanted forest alone because there is nothing in it he is more afraid of than the mirror already showed him.

**Armida**: The poem's most alive figure. She arrives as a weapon — beautiful, skillful, equipped with an infernal mandate — and becomes human through being refused. Godfrey doesn't want her; Tancred's love is already spoken; Rinaldo is enchanted but not in love. Her conversion at the end is not clearly spiritual — it may be the final act of a woman who has no other strategy left. Or it may be genuine. The poem refuses to adjudicate.

**Erminia**: Patient and unrequited through twenty books. She loves Tancred in the only ways available to her: by describing him to his enemies, by stealing Clorinda's armor to go to him, by living among shepherds carving his name in trees. She arrives at his side at the end in total silence. She is simply there, with her healing instruments, at the moment they are finally needed. The poem earns her.

## Pass 3 — Pivotal Scene: Book XII, Stanzas LXIV–LXIX

*The death of Clorinda.*

They have fought for hours in darkness. She is dying from a sword thrust to the breast. Both of them are exhausted past the point of speech. Then:

"Friend, thou hast won, I pardon thee, nor save / This body, that all torments can endure, / But save my soul, baptism I dying crave, / Come wash away my sins with waters pure."

He does not know who she is yet. He hears the request and his anger collapses. He runs to the mountain stream nearby, fills his helmet, returns, and baptizes her — speaking the formula over a stranger whose name he doesn't know. Then he removes her visor and sees her face.

He does not fall. He is capable of remaining upright only because grief has numbed the mechanism that causes falling. He keeps performing the rite he came back to perform: the sacred words, the water, the sign. He finishes baptizing the woman he has just killed and whom he loves.

She says: "I die in peace." She smiles. She gives him her naked hand. She dies.

Tasso's description of her death is as careful as an inventory: "As violets blue mongst lilies pure men throw, / So paleness midst her native white begun." This is the precision grief uses when it cannot be said directly. The color draining from a face described as the specific geometry of blue flowers in white ones.

Tancred then nearly dies. The Christian squadron that finds him assumes he is dead. He survives — barely — because his task is not finished. Clorinda is in the ground. The letter in Arsetes's pouch is not yet delivered. The thirty-year chain of deferral extends one more link past the grave.

## Seven Storylines

1. **The Eunuch's Charge** — Arsetes, dying in a Christian hospice, has carried an undelivered letter from the Ethiopian queen to the St. George shrine for thirty years; Clorinda is dead; the vow has been kept on the saint's side; the Fighter carries the payment to the shrine. *(Selected)*

2. **Sophronia's Witness** — After the fall of Jerusalem, a notary seeks to amend the official Saracen record listing Sophronia as a convicted thief; Olindo's deposition naming the real sequence must reach the archive before the record is permanently fixed.

3. **Tancred's Grant** — Tancred has vowed to build a chapel at the site where Clorinda died; the Crusade council's land allocation closes in two days; the Fighter carries the petition before the land is reallocated.

4. **Ismen's Notebooks** — The sorcerer's recovered spellbooks document how the enchanted forest was constructed; the Crusade council wants them burned; one scholar argues they are the only evidence that black magic operates through architectural principles, not divine power; the Fighter carries the books to a safe archive against the destruction order.

5. **Erminia's Safe Passage** — Erminia, now a Christian ally, carries papers granting her father's Antioch estate back, but the garrison commander disputes her identity (she has been living as a shepherd for months); the Fighter carries her credentials from the camp to the city land registry.

6. **Armida's Conversion** — Armida has declared herself a Christian and Rinaldo's ward; her Damascus properties are now legally contested; the Fighter carries her formal declaration to the ecclesiastical authority who must ratify Christian protection for a converted sorceress.

7. **The Godfrey Dispatch** — In the last hours before the final assault, Godfrey wrote three letters; the courier carrying the letter to Rome was wounded; the letter contains the official account of the Crusade's conclusion, which differs from Raymond's in ways that will matter to history; the Fighter carries it to the ship before it sails.

## 5-Act Vignette Play: "The Eunuch's Charge" (JRS_001)

*French noir register. The Fighter is a carrier. The TOKEN is the grammar.*

---

**TOKEN**: The Sealed Letter of the Queen

A cloth pouch, grey linen worn to near-transparency with age. On one face, pressed into the weave in the first weeks of its existence: the impression of a tigress's paw — not stamped deliberately but preserved when the great cat lay down beside the infant Clorinda in a wilderness and the cloth was pressed against the paw. No one made this mark. It preserved itself.

Inside: a folded document sealed with Ethiopian royal wax bearing the double-headed eagle device. Inside that, the letter in two hands — Ge'ez script from the queen's own hand, Latin translation appended by a clerk thirty years dead. The queen's promise to the shrine of St. George: *I vow gifts and prayer for the remainder of my life if thou keep my white child safe from harm and bring her to thy service.* Below the Latin, a date.

The child lived thirty years. She was kept safe from harm by a saint whose name she never heard. She was brought to the saint's service in the worst possible way, by the worst possible hands, at the worst possible hour. She died his. The letter has been in the pouch since she was sixteen months old. The pouch has been in Arsetes's hands since the same day.

**TOKEN DOCTRINE**: TOKEN granted Act 1 (Arsetes's hands relinquish it). TOKEN checked Acts 2–4. TOKEN delivered Act 5 (into the sealed vault of the shrine, which the Fighter will never see opened again).

### Act 1 — The Pilgrim Keeper's Quarter (PKR)
*Node: PKR (new). Skill: Insight DC 12. TOKEN GRANTED.*

The hospice in the pilgrim district smells of dried sage and old leather and something specific to men who have outlived their purpose. Arsetes is in the third room, the one with a window onto the wall. He was old when Clorinda was young; now he is very old and diminishing rapidly. He has sent for someone to carry a thing. The Fighter is the person who arrived.

He cannot explain the full story. He tries three times and stops. The pouch is on the blanket beside his hand. He has been sleeping with one hand on it for thirty years. He picks it up and holds it out.

Insight DC 12: The Fighter understands that Arsetes is not asking for a promise. He is not asking the Fighter to understand the Ge'ez, or the Latin, or the date. He is asking for the transfer of custody to proceed without ceremony, because ceremony would require him to say that Clorinda is dead and that the letter was deferred too long and that he failed the only thing her mother asked. He cannot say those things. The Transfer must happen in silence.

*Success*: The Fighter picks it up. Arsetes nods once. His hand stops shaking.
*Failure (Insight missed)*: The Fighter reaches for it with a question. Arsetes pulls back. After a long silence, he pushes it forward again without looking. TOKEN granted — but Arsetes does not nod. He turns his face to the wall.

**TOKEN GRANTED.** (The pouch with the tigress paw in the Fighter's hands.)

### Act 2 — The Hospice Gate Road (PKR outskirts)
*Node: PKR (road departing). Skill: Persuasion DC 13. TOKEN CHECKED.*

A Crusade records officer has been tasked with inventorying property belonging to the wounded and dying at the hospice. He has a list. He intercepts the Fighter at the hospice gate. He has noted "one cloth pouch, foreign materials, sealed document within" in the property register for the hospice. He believes this is an unclaimed Saracen dispatch — the cloth looks foreign, the seal is unfamiliar and does not match any known crusading house.

He wants the pouch returned for cataloguing and possible military intelligence review.

*The argument*: The pouch is a votive document in transit to a shrine — not a Saracen military dispatch. The Latin translation appended to the interior document establishes a Christian religious purpose; the double-headed eagle is Ethiopian royal, not Saracen military. The hospice keeper can confirm that Arsetes commissioned a carrier for this object and that the transfer of custody was voluntary.

Persuasion DC 13.
*Success*: The officer notes the object in the register as "votive document, released to authorized carrier, destination: shrine." He initials it. The Fighter walks through the gate.
*Failure*: The officer keeps the pouch for forty-eight hours while an interpreter is found. The Fighter waits, then argues again (DC now 11 — the interpreter's Latin translation already performed the work). Delay: half a day.

---

### Act 3 — Limestone Road to SGA (Approach, midpoint)
*Node: limestone road between PKR and SGA. Skill: Persuasion DC 14 + possible combat. TOKEN CHECKED.*

Two armed servants are waiting at a road-fork. Their lord — a Crusade knight who collects religious curiosities and relics from the eastern campaign — has heard that an Ethiopian queen's sealed letter is traveling this road toward the shrine. He has sent his servants to acquire it.

Their argument: the letter is a relic. An Ethiopian queen who prayed to a saint and bore a miracle child; the tigress paw on the cloth; the thirty-year history. Their lord will pay a significant price. The letter would be cared for.

*The Fighter's argument*: A votive letter is not a relic. Its spiritual content — if it has any — derives from being delivered, not from being possessed. A letter of donation to a saint's shrine belongs to the shrine by the terms of the donation; intercepting it is not acquiring a relic but stealing a payment. The lord's collection cannot house it correctly because it was not made to be housed. It was made to be put down.

Persuasion DC 14.
*Success*: The servants stand aside.
*Failure*: Combat: 2× soldiers, AC 12, HP 14 each. After combat, the pouch is intact — the servants were not authorized to damage it.

---

### Act 4 — The Shrine Town Gate (SGA outskirts)
*Node: SGA approach. Skill: Athletics DC 13. TOKEN CHECKED.*

The shrine town's outer gate has a toll. The gate-keeper is operating under a new town-council regulation: all sealed documents entering the town pay a transit charge, to fund repairs to the shrine roof. The charge is posted on a board. It applies to merchants, couriers, and pilgrims with sealed materials.

The counter-argument is obvious but must be made: the regulation was designed for commercial documents; a votive letter directed to the shrine as a donation is not cargo in transit through the town but cargo addressed to it; the donation fund is the shrine; taxing the letter on arrival is taxing the thing that funds the tax. The gate-keeper has heard similar arguments before and has a counter of his own: the council wrote "sealed documents" with no exceptions.

This is not a Persuasion check. The gate-keeper is not persuadable — the council wrote the rule. This is an Athletics check (DC 13): a secondary entrance to the shrine district is known to local pilgrims — a low wall in the eastern quarter, no gate, no toll register — but it requires a controlled descent and a short climb. The pouch cannot be dropped. The old cloth cannot survive a fall onto limestone.

*Success*: The Fighter reaches the shrine precincts without engaging the tollgate.
*Failure*: The toll is paid. No delay; minor resource cost. But the Fighter arrives at the shrine from the main entrance, where a different obstacle applies (see Act 5 — the Insight DC is not raised, but the mood of the arrival changes).

### Act 5 — The Shrine of St. George (SGA)
*Node: SGA. Skill: Insight DC 12. TOKEN DELIVERED (TAKEN).*

The shrine's keeper of records, Brother Matthias, is a careful man. He sits at a desk in the donation office, a small room off the main nave with stone shelves holding sealed votive boxes and wax-tablet records going back two centuries. He receives visitors who bring offerings. He reads the accompanying documents. He files them in the vault.

He takes the pouch. He reads the Latin translation. He reads the date. He looks at the pouch.

"Thirty years ago," he says. This is not an accusation. It is the start of a calculation.

He opens the register. There is an entry from thirty years past in a different hand: a projected donation from an Ethiopian royal family, to be delivered by a representative named Arsetes. The entry has a note: *awaited.* Brother Matthias draws a line through *awaited* and writes the date and the Fighter's description in the margin.

Then he opens the sealed vault. He places the letter inside. He closes the vault.

He does not say the debt is discharged. He says: "She was kept."

Insight DC 12: The Fighter understands what this means — not only that the letter has been received, but that the saint's side of this contract was performed completely: across thirty years of war, the child was kept; she lived her full life; when she died, she died in the name of the one who had always protected her. The contract was not interrupted by her death. It was completed by it. The Ethiopian queen's letter of intention and the stream in the dark and Tancred's helmet full of water are all the same event at different distances.

TOKEN TAKEN. The Fighter is left holding only the empty cloth pouch with the tigress paw. Brother Matthias does not ask for this. It was never part of the letter.

*What the Fighter carries out*: The empty pouch. The tigress paw pressed into old linen by a day in a wilderness thirty years before the Fighter was born. Nothing else.

*What is spoken at the end (the token's essence, given to the player)*: **"She was kept."** — The saint fulfilled the contract. The payment was late but not refused. A debt to the dead is still a debt, and it was owed not to the dead but to the one who had already performed.

## Cycle 2 · Sophronia's Witness

**New angle:** Cycle 1 was about fulfilling a contract — carrying what had been carried too long and finally putting it down. Cycle 2 is about correcting a record that misrepresents heroism as crime. Sophronia's false confession saved the entire Christian community of Jerusalem from massacre; the Saracen court record logs the act as theft and conviction, not sacrifice. Now the record is being transferred to permanent archive status under the new Christian administration. What cycle 1 missed: the specific complication of an act of love that can only be vindicated by exposing the lie at its center — and the person whose signature the deposition needs is the person whose lie it is.

---

### Token Doctrine

A sealed document naming the full sequence: Ismen removed the icon himself before the accusation; Aladine's massacre-threat was the reason for Sophronia's false confession; the confession was made with no expectation of survival; Clorinda's intervention stopped the execution. Olindo's signature is on it. The deposition is single-signature — insufficient to override a dual-signed conviction record. The required second witness is Sophronia. She has not signed. The token's state changes at Act IV when Sophronia adds her signature.

### 5-Act Vignette Play: "Sophronia's Witness"

*A Fighter has been asked by Olindo to carry his deposition from their Jerusalem quarter to the city administrative archive before the old Saracen conviction record is permanently filed. The TOKEN ITEM is Olindo's Deposition: a sealed document naming the sequence of Sophronia's false confession, its intent, and its outcome. It needs a second signature before it can supplement the conviction record. The Fighter carries it to the archive, discovers the signature problem, returns to Sophronia, and brings the dual-signed document back.*

*The Thing · Olindo's Deposition · sealed folio · scribe's desk → Fighter*

---

**ACT I — Olindo and Sophronia's Quarter, Jerusalem**

*Morning. A small room near the old Christian quarter. Olindo at a scribe's desk, the scribe across from him with his stylus paused.*

*The Thing · Olindo's Deposition · unsigned · on the scribe's desk*

*The Fighter observes: the scribe has written the sequence accurately. His stylus is not moving. He is deciding something.*

The scribe's problem is specific: certifying a document that describes a false confession made to a king is certifying perjury. The document is accurate — Sophronia lied to Aladine — but calling the lie heroic and certifying it as such requires the scribe to make a moral judgment he is not sure is his to make. Olindo cannot explain why this matters enough to override the scribe's hesitation. He is not a lawyer.

---

**ACT II — The Archive District Road**

*The road to the Jerusalem administrative archive. A Saracen legal representative, now operating under the new administration's procedural agreements, has an expedited filing order.*

*The Thing · Olindo's Deposition · sealed · Fighter's pack*

*The Fighter observes: the representative is not hostile. He is completing a job. The job has a deadline: all unresolved Saracen civil conviction records become permanent at the third bell.*

The expedited filing order allows records to be closed without waiting for challenge filings. It was written for commercial disputes, not criminal convictions with living parties. The Fighter must identify the procedural ground that distinguishes a civil commercial record (which the expedited order covers) from a criminal conviction record with named living defendants (which it does not).

---

**ACT III — The Jerusalem Administrative Archive**

*The archive keeper's office. A Greek clerk named Alexios, who served the Saracen administration and now serves the Christian one.*

*The Thing · Olindo's Deposition · sealed · Fighter's pack · about to be revealed as insufficient*

*The Fighter observes: Alexios is precise and fair. He reads the deposition. He reads the conviction record. He sets them side by side.*

"The conviction record carries two signatures," he says. "Your deposition carries one. Under the archive's supplementation protocol, a single-signature deposition can be appended as commentary but cannot be filed as a contradicting record. To formally supplement the conviction, you need a second witness signature. The obvious second witness —" He looks at the Fighter. "— has not signed it."

---

**ACT IV — Return to Sophronia**

*The small room again. Sophronia is at the window. Olindo is not in the room.*

*The Thing · Olindo's Deposition · unsealed now, page open · in the Fighter's hands*

*The Fighter observes: she has read the deposition before the Fighter arrived. Olindo told her. She is not distressed. She is considering something with complete composure.*

She does not ask what signing will do to the record. She knows: the conviction disappears as a stand-alone entry and is supplemented by a documented act of self-sacrifice. She is thinking about something else — whether writing her own lie's name in her own hand is a different thing from having lived it. The Fighter must understand what she needs to hear in order to sign: not justification, not praise, not legal reasoning. What she needs to hear is that the deposition closes what the lie opened, rather than re-opening it.

---

**ACT V — The Archive, Second Visit**

*Alexios's office. The third bell is two hours away.*

*The Thing · Olindo's Deposition · dual-signed · Sophronia's signature on the witness line · Fighter's hands*

*The Fighter observes: Alexios reads both signatures. He reads the deposition again. He opens the permanent record ledger.*

He could enter the conviction record as originally filed, with the deposition appended as contradicting commentary. Or he can enter both together — conviction record and deposition — with a notation that the conviction record reflects the official Saracen court finding and the supplementing deposition reflects a subsequent sworn account of intent and context by both parties named in the original conviction. The notation does not say who was right. It says both exist in the permanent record and that anyone consulting it will find both. Alexios picks up his stylus.

### 5-Act Vignette Play: *Tancred's Grant*

**THE TOKEN: TANCRED'S PETITION TABLET** — A rectangular olive-wood tablet, twice the length of a hand. On one face, carved in a soldier's functional Latin: the boundary marks of a small valley northwest of the city wall, the request to allocate it in perpetuity as a chapel site, the legal basis, and Tancred's name. On the reverse face, a line scratched in haste the night before: *this is where she died*. No other annotation. The five boundary marks are precise enough to exclude the possibility of confusion with any other valley. Tancred himself drew them.

---

**ACT I — "The Allocation Window"**
*Node: JAR — Jerusalem Archive and Administration Registry.*

The Crusade land council is closing its allocation session in one hour. After that, unclaimed valley parcels revert to the administrative pool and a new session will not be called for months. The tablet is already written. Tancred gave it to you at dawn. His exact words: "File it before the session ends."

The land allocation desk is at the far end of the registry hall. Between you and it: the queue from this morning's petitions, a side-corridor argument between a Crusade quartermaster and a pilgrim hostel proprietor over stable rights, and the desk clerk who has been processing documents since before first light.

> **Persuasion DC 12.** The clerk has fifteen documents ahead of yours. The session window is real. Ask him to log the petition now, before the session ends, on the basis that it has already been reviewed — Tancred's name is on it, the boundaries are specified, the legal basis is written. He does not need to make a decision. He only needs to log that a petition has been received.

**Fail:** He logs it as received but marks it as pending session review. The session ends. The tablet is in the queue but unclosed. You need to return tomorrow.

**Pass:** He reads the name. He reads the boundary marks. He reads the line on the reverse. He logs it in the current session without comment.

TOKEN GRANTED: Tancred's Petition Tablet.

---

**ACT II — "The Orchard Interest"**
*Node: JAR road departing.*

A Crusade knight named Bernard is waiting outside the registry. He has been watching the session door. His steward holds a draft petition of his own — for the same valley, framed as an agricultural allocation for an orchard his family will plant. He speaks to you pleasantly. He knows who Tancred is. He is not hostile. He says: Tancred can build a chapel somewhere else.

> **History DC 11.** The valley is the site of a specific combat — the night fight in which Tancred killed Clorinda. That combat is already in the Crusade's military record. A site where a named engagement in the official record occurred is not available for agricultural allocation under the campaign's own land-use provisions. Bernard's petition would have failed on those grounds before it reached a decision. Tancred did not know this when he filed his petition. It doesn't change what he asked for. It changes what Bernard can legally do.

**Fail:** Bernard's steward argues the military record provision is informal. You file a counter-argument with the clerk and the question will take weeks.

**Pass:** Bernard receives the information. He reads his steward's face. He withdraws the petition without speaking further about it.

TOKEN check Act II.

---

**ACT III — "The Relic Offer"**
*Node: CON — Constantinople archive district.*

A Byzantine collector — not hostile, cultured, with a good library — finds you in the archive waiting room. He wants the olive-wood tablet. His argument is careful: the line on the back (*this is where she died*) is a relic notation; the object has intrinsic significance independent of what it files; he can offer a price proportional to the significance. His offer is genuinely proportional.

> **Insight DC 12.** The tablet's significance is relational, not intrinsic. The line on the back means what it means because Tancred wrote it about a specific valley, and the object's function is to identify that valley in a land registry. Removed from the registry function and placed in a collection, it becomes an object about something that cannot be explained from the object. The line *this is where she died* requires the five boundary marks to be meaningful; without the context of the allocation petition, the words become grief-decoration. The collector would possess an object he cannot interpret correctly.

**Fail:** He makes a second offer. You hold.

**Pass:** You give him the distinction between relic and working document. He thinks about it. He says he will commission a copy of the valley's description from a geographer instead.

TOKEN check Act III.

---

**ACT IV — "The Byzantine Transit Check"**
*Node: CON road toward WM.*

A Byzantine customs official stops you at the city gate. Carved objects are subject to documentation — the empire has been losing architectural records and inscriptions to informal export. He reads the Latin. He reads the boundary marks. He cannot parse the boundary references — they describe a valley northwest of Jerusalem, which is outside Byzantine administrative jurisdiction.

> **Athletics DC 12.** This is not a Persuasion check. The official's procedure requires a written authorization for any carved object that cannot be assessed by his office. The authorization office is twenty minutes back in the customs district. The road ahead will be passable for four more hours before the evening gate closes. You need to get the written authorization, return, and reach the gate before the evening closure. This requires moving fast through a busy customs district and back without damaging the tablet.

**Fail:** You miss the gate. One day's delay at the customs hostel.

**Pass:** You make both trips and pass through the gate with the authorization stamp before the evening closure.

TOKEN check Act IV.

---

**ACT V — "The Archivist's Note"**
*Node: WM — Weimar Archive.*

Sweelinck reads the petition. He reads the boundary marks. He reads the line on the reverse. He sets the tablet down carefully with the reverse facing up.

"He filed this himself?"

You explain: he gave it to you at dawn and asked you to file it before the allocation session closed.

Sweelinck looks at the line again. He turns it over and reads the boundary marks again.

"The site where a man killed what he loved and baptized what he killed cannot be given to an orchard," he says, and this is not poetic. It is the legal basis he will cite in the archive note.

> **Investigation DC 11.** The archive needs to classify the petition under a category that preserves both the land grant function and the personal record it carries. Standard land grant records do not cover sites whose significance is simultaneous with the event that created them. Sweelinck needs you to read the existing category list and confirm that no existing category covers this — that the archive must create a new one.

**Fail:** Sweelinck selects an existing category that partially fits. The tablet is filed but the category is approximate.

**Pass:** You confirm the gap. Sweelinck creates: *Council Land Grants — Sites Marked by Irresolvable Personal History, First Entry. The valley northwest of Jerusalem where Tancred killed Clorinda and baptized her: the five boundary marks are not a memorial notation but a legal description; the chapel Tancred will build is not a monument but a location; this is where the event happened and this document marks it as that place permanently.*

TOKEN TAKEN: Tancred's Petition Tablet.

### 5-Act Vignette Play: *Ismen's Notebooks*

**THE TOKEN: ISMEN'S ARCHITECTURAL NOTEBOOK** — A small iron-clasped notebook, octavo size, in black-dyed calfskin. The clasp is iron because the contents require a material that will not respond to the enchantment the book documents. Inside: seventy pages of architectural diagrams, measurement tables, and margin notes in the sorcerer Ismen's hand — detailed records of how the enchanted forest was constructed layer by layer, which trees were modified first, the harmonic sequence of the enchantments, the specific psychological material each zone was keyed to. On the cover, fastened with a wax seal: the Crusade council's destruction order, issued three days after Jerusalem fell, classifying the notebooks as "materials employed in black art, to be burned." The seal has not been broken. The order is still valid.

---

**ACT I — "The Scholar's Argument"**
*Node: PKR — Pilgrim's Quarter.*

The scholar Magister Aldo found the notebook in Ismen's quarters after the sorcerer was killed. He brought it to the pilgrims' district rather than the council because he had read enough of it to know what the council would do. He wants you to carry it to an archive where it will not be burned.

His argument: the notebook is not a spell. It is a record of architectural method. Ismen documented how enchantment works the way an engineer documents how a vault is built — materials, sequence, load-bearing relationships. The destruction order covers "materials employed in black art." The notebook documents; it does not employ.

> **History DC 11.** The distinction is not merely semantic. Architectural records of construction are in a different legal and documentary category from the construction itself. Magister Aldo's argument holds under standard archive law: a document that describes a method does not instantiate the method. The destruction order's language cannot be read to cover documentation without also covering every other technical manual the council would prefer to keep.

**Fail:** You carry the notebook without internalizing the distinction. You will need to make the argument under pressure in Act II without preparation.

**Pass:** You have the argument before anyone asks for it.

TOKEN GRANTED: Ismen's Architectural Notebook.

---

**ACT II — "The Destruction Order"**
*Node: PKR gate.*

A Crusade captain at the quarter gate has a copy of the destruction order. He has been watching for exactly this notebook — someone told the council that Magister Aldo had it. The captain is professional. He is not cruel. He is enforcing a valid legal order.

> **Persuasion DC 13.** The destruction order reads "materials employed in black art." The notebook documents the architecture of enchantment; it does not employ enchantment. The distinction is legible in the order's own language: "employed" requires active use; documentation is passive. Carrying an architectural record of what the enchanted forest was is not carrying the enchanted forest. The captain must either accept the distinction or challenge it in writing — and a written challenge will require him to argue that documentation of a thing is the same as the thing, which will not survive review.

**Fail:** He detains the notebook for twenty-four hours pending council review. You wait.

**Pass:** He reads the order again. He reads the cover notation. He accepts the distinction and lets you pass with a warning.

TOKEN check Act II.

---

**ACT III — "The Venetian Factor"**
*Node: VEN — Venice, Merchant Republic Archive District.*

A Venetian factor who handles acquisition commissions for a Levantine collector meets you in the archive district. He has heard about the notebook through a ship manifest and wants to purchase it for his client. His offer is generous. His framing is part commercial, part genuinely scholarly — he notes that the Levantine collector has a library that includes three other works on enchantment methodology and would treat the notebook as a research document.

> **Insight DC 12.** The factor's interest is genuinely divided: the commercial commission is driving the inquiry, but there is authentic scholarly motivation behind it. The distinction matters because the correct response is not to reject him entirely but to give the scholarly interest its correct answer: archive records are accessible to researchers; the notebook will be reachable at Weimar through standard scholarly access. The archive provides what the collector actually needs — legibility of the record — without transferring custody to a private collection where the destruction order's legal status remains unresolved.

**Fail:** He makes a second, higher offer. You decline again. He follows you to the harbor.

**Pass:** He receives the distinction. He notes Weimar's access protocols and says his client will send a letter of inquiry. This is an acceptable outcome.

TOKEN check Act III.

---

**ACT IV — "The Dominican's Authorization"**
*Node: VEN harbor road.*

A Dominican friar intercepts you on the harbor road. He carries a letter from a Papal legate authorizing him to collect and remand to Rome any materials related to black magic found in the eastern theater. The letter is genuine and broad. He is polite and unhurrying. He believes he is performing a service.

> **Deception DC 13.** His authorization covers "materials related to black magic." The notebook documents architectural methodology; its subject is enchantment, but its character is analytical and archival. Tell him honestly that the notebook is an architectural record of method — not a spellbook, not an active magical instrument, not a scroll of incantations. The friar's authorization cannot logically cover a technical manual without also covering every alchemical notebook in Venice. You are not deceiving him about what the notebook is; you are preventing him from misclassifying it under a category it does not occupy.

**Fail:** He flags the notebook for forwarding to the Papal legate for assessment. A two-week delay while the legate's office in Avignon reads a letter.

**Pass:** He accepts the classification. He notes the Weimar destination in his record. He lets you pass.

TOKEN check Act IV.

---

**ACT V — "The Destruction Order in the Record"**
*Node: WM — Weimar Archive.*

Sweelinck reads the architectural diagrams. He reads the harmonic sequence tables. He reads the margin notes. He puts the notebook down and looks at the destruction order on the cover.

"The council wanted this burned. It was not burned. Both facts are part of the record."

He begins to remove the wax seal from the cover.

> **Investigation DC 11.** Stop him. The destruction order is not incidental to the notebook's provenance — it is the central fact of the notebook's existence as a document. The notebook survived because the distinction between "materials employed in black art" and "architectural records of method" was maintained. Removing the order would remove the evidence of the challenge that defines the notebook's significance. The archive should receive the notebook and the order together, with both intact.

**Fail:** Sweelinck removes the seal before you speak. The destruction order is filed separately. The notebook is separated from the evidence of its own survival.

**Pass:** Sweelinck stops. He reads your argument. He sets down the wax tool. He files them together: notebook and destruction order, intact. His notation: *Architectural Records of Supernatural Intervention — The Construction Behind the Illusion, First Entry. Ismen's notebook documenting the enchanted forest's construction layer by layer: this record survived because it is a document about enchantment, not a material of enchantment; the destruction order on the cover is part of the provenance chain and will remain attached.*

TOKEN TAKEN: Ismen's Architectural Notebook.

## Cycle 8 — The Desertion Record

**What this angle reveals that prior cycles missed:** Every prior cycle treats documents that were created with intent — commissions, confessions, conversion records, dispatches. This cycle takes the opposite: a record that nobody wanted to exist, created not by authority but by absence, which becomes the only honest account of what actually happened to the men who left.

---

**THE TOKEN: `jrs_cycle_8_token`** — A muster-roll sheet, folded three times, smeared at one corner with camp grease. Beside each name: rank, unit, and a single column that reads either *returned* or *not returned*. Twelve names have *returned* written in a different hand, later. Thirty-one have nothing. At the bottom, in Godfrey's own script: "Let this remain unburned. They were not cowards." The sheet was never formally entered into the campaign archive. It traveled in a clerk's satchel for fourteen months before anyone thought to file it.
