## Cycle 1 — Angle: Diana Vernon and the education of a gentleman

---

### Pass 1 — Quick

Rob Roy (1817) is a coming-of-age novel disguised as a Highland adventure: Francis
Osbaldistone, a young London merchant's son who would rather write poetry, is
banished to his uncle's dark manor in Northumberland, falls for the impossible
Diana Vernon, follows a trail of treachery into the Scottish Highlands on the eve
of the 1715 Jacobite Rising, and is educated by the outlaw Rob Roy MacGregor in
what honor actually costs. The novel is not about Rob Roy — he is the gravitational
center the plot orbits — it is about Frank learning that his father's world of
commerce and his uncle's world of blood-honor are both insufficient, and that the
only people who live whole lives are those, like Diana and the Bailie, who contain
contradictions without resolving them.

---

### Pass 2 — Characters, Choices, Tone

**Francis Osbaldistone (Frank):** The narrator. Twentyish, well-read, romantically
inclined, more at home with Ariosto than a ledger. His defining characteristic is
that he is wrong about almost everything he thinks he knows — about Scotland,
about honor, about Diana — and the novel is the process of his correction. He is
not a hero; he is a witness who gradually learns what he is witnessing. By the end
he has become capable of the world he lives in without being consumed by it.

**Diana Vernon:** The novel's moral center. Sharp, educated, Catholic, Jacobite by
obligation if not entirely by conviction, she is under her father's wardship and
cannot choose her own fate. She loves Frank plainly and openly — she says so, or
nearly — but she cannot act on it, and she refuses to deceive him about why. Her
famous line: *"I will be frank with you, since Frank you are. I will not say, Do
not love me — I will say, Do not love me with a love that expects a return, for I
am not free to give one."* She exits Frank's life twice, each time closing a door
behind her with perfect composure. The novel is largely her loss — told by the man
who was left.

**Rob Roy MacGregor:** The historical figure transformed into a legendary type —
the outlaw who is more honorable than the law, the criminal who is more principled
than the merchants and nobles around him. He appears first as "Mr. Campbell" at
a roadside inn, unremarkable until he is remarkable. He operates entirely by his
own code: clans are family; debts are sacred; betrayal is answered. He helps Frank
because Frank is owed help; he never explains himself further than that. His
relationship to the Jacobite cause is pragmatic — he uses what he can, disclaims
what he cannot. He is the most self-possessed character in the novel.

**Bailie Nicol Jarvie:** Glasgow magistrate, Rob Roy's kinsman (his mother was a
MacGregor), and the novel's comic-heroic soul. He represents Scottish Lowland
sense — commerce, practicality, loyalty to law — but he cannot help being loyal
to Rob Roy despite all of it. His greatest moment is the brawl at the Clachan of
Aberfoil, where the fat magistrate fights off Highlanders with a red-hot coulter
he pulls from the fire, his magisterial dignity entirely intact. He is Scott's
argument that contradictions can coexist in a person without destroying them.

**Rashleigh Osbaldistone:** The villain. Clever, Jesuit-educated, working as
agent for both Jacobites and Hanoverians simultaneously, loyal to nothing but
advantage. He steals the Osbaldistone commercial papers to ruin Frank's father
and force Frank into dependence. He dies at Rob Roy's hands in the novel's climax
— a death that is also justice, though of the Highland kind.

**Helen MacGregor:** Rob Roy's wife. Fiercer than he is, less forgiving. She
orders the government spy Morris bound and thrown into Loch Lomond. She watches
him drown without expression. She is the novel's hardest moral fact — that the
Highland world contains a justice the English world cannot accommodate, and that
accommodation would be a form of erasure.

**The world's tone:** Scotland in 1715 is a country in the act of being absorbed
by a larger power that does not understand it. The Highlands and Lowlands already
speak different languages, economically and culturally. The '15 Rising — the first
Jacobite attempt to restore the Stuarts — is the historical backdrop, but Scott
uses it to explore how individuals navigate between loyalties when the structure
around them is fracturing. The novel is autumn-lit: everything beautiful in it is
either departing or already gone.

---

### Pass 3 — The Scene in Full: Diana at the Library Window

The night before Frank leaves Osbaldistone Hall for the last time to pursue
Rashleigh into Scotland, Diana comes to him. She cannot be seen; she comes to the
library window from the garden side, and she speaks through the half-open casement
into the dark room where Frank stands.

She is not distraught. That is the first thing. She has been distraught before —
or near it — but not tonight. Tonight she has the composure of someone who has
already made the hardest decision and is now executing it. She gives him a sealed
letter. She tells him it must reach Glasgow, a specific name, a specific counting-
house. She does not tell him what is in it. She says: *"It will serve your father.
That is all you need know."*

Frank presses her. He asks if they will see each other again. She says: *"We are
both of us in other people's hands, Frank. What we want cannot be arranged by
wanting it."*

He reaches through the window. He means to take her hand. She gives him the letter
instead. Her fingers close around his for a moment — not long. Then she is gone
back into the dark garden, and he can hear her footsteps on the gravel, quieter,
quieter, gone.

He stands at the window with the letter and knows that she has just told him
everything. That this is it. That the letter is the last thing she can give him
and she has given it.

He does not go after her. He closes the window. He sits down.

The letter is sealed with her cipher — a small heron, her emblem, pressed into
red wax. He will not break it. He will carry it to Glasgow. He will deliver it.
It is all he can do for her now.

## Seven Storyline Ideas

1. **The Bailie's Coulter** — A bar-fight breaks out at the Clachan of Aberfoil
   when English soldiers attempt to arrest a Highland chief. The Bailie Nicol
   Jarvie pulls a red-hot coulter from the fire and defends the group; the Fighter
   must hold the door. Unarmed tavern combat, protecting an improvised hero.

2. **Diana's Letter** — Diana gives the Fighter a sealed letter at Osbaldistone
   Hall to carry to a Glasgow counting-house. Rashleigh's agents are watching
   every road south. The Fighter must move through contested territory keeping
   the letter unread and unbroken. Pure escort with concealment and deception.

3. **Helen's Justice** — The Fighter witnesses Helen MacGregor condemn a government
   spy to death by drowning in Loch Lomond. They can intervene — and trigger a
   Highland blood-feud — or hold their tongue and carry that silence back to
   Glasgow. A moral weight quest with no clean answer.

4. **Rob Roy's Word** — Rob Roy gives the Fighter a verbal token — a phrase, a
   specific greeting — to carry to a clan chief who will then provide safe passage.
   The token is memory; the quest is not losing it while being interrogated,
   threatened, and delayed. Perception and Persuasion checks across a three-day
   journey.

5. **Rashleigh's Double Game** — The Fighter discovers that Rashleigh is carrying
   two sets of papers — one for the Jacobites, one for the Hanoverians. Recovering
   the right one without letting Rashleigh know he's been discovered requires a
   long deception chain. Stealth and Deception quest.

6. **The Highland Muster** — The clans are assembling for the '15 Rising on a
   loch-side field at dawn. Frank is in the middle of it. The Fighter must get
   him out before the English dragoons arrive, without drawing weapon against
   either side. Escort through a crowd; every wrong move fans the spark.

7. **The Ford at Aberfoil** — Rob Roy's escape: he is taken by English soldiers
   on the road, his hands bound. He dives into the river, swims under fire,
   reaches the far bank and vanishes. The Fighter holds the ford against the
   soldiers' pursuit long enough for him to be gone. Rear-guard combat quest
   that ends when the outlaw disappears into the Highland dark.

## 5-Act Vignette Play: *Diana's Letter*

*Storyline 2 — Escort Quest. The Fighter carries Diana Vernon's sealed letter
from Osbaldistone Hall through contested country to a Glasgow counting-house,
where it will discharge a debt that saves the Osbaldistone firm. Diana cannot
deliver it herself — she is watched. The Fighter is her hand.*

**THE TOKEN: Diana's Sealed Letter** — heavy cream paper folded three times,
sealed with red wax and Diana's cipher: a small heron, wings folded, pressed
deep. It smells faintly of her work-room — ink, beeswax, a dried sprig of
heather pressed flat inside the fold. It has not been opened. It must not be.

---

### ACT I — *The Library Window, Dusk*
*The letter: sealed, warm, just pressed into the Fighter's hands through a
casement window. Location: Osbaldistone Hall, Northumberland — east library,
garden-facing.*

The house is quiet with the particular quiet of a house being watched. Two men
have been on the road outside since morning — mounted, patient, not talking.
Everyone inside knows they are there.

Diana comes to the window from the garden side. No candle. She does not enter.
She speaks through the half-open glass and her voice is steady in the way that
costs something to make steady.

**Diana:** *"A name. A counting-house in the Gallowgate. You will know the door —
there is a painted heron above it. Give him this. Tell him it comes from the
woman who knows what the heron means. Do not break the seal."*

She gives the letter. Her fingers close around the Fighter's for a moment. Then
she is back in the dark garden, her footsteps quieter, gone.

*The Fighter thinks: she did not ask if they would succeed. She already decided
they would.*

---

### ACT II — *The North Road, Third Hour Before Dawn*
*The letter: sealed, tucked inside the Fighter's coat against the chest. Warm.
Location: the Redesdale road, Northumberland — fog, loose stone, no torches.*

The two riders from the road outside the hall are ahead somewhere. The Fighter
has taken the sheep-track instead of the post-road, which adds three miles but
loses two followers — or should. The fog is useful and not useful. A horse moves
in it without sound until it is close.

At a stone bridge over a bog-stream, a checkpoint: two of Rashleigh's men with
a lantern, searching everyone moving south toward the border. They are looking
for papers, they say. A ledger, specifically. Or letters.

*The Fighter thinks: they do not know the shape of what they are looking for.
That is still an advantage.*

---

### ACT III — *Glasgow — The Gallowgate, Midmorning*
*The letter: sealed, inside the coat, dampened slightly by two days of road-fog
but unbroken. Location: the Gallowgate, Glasgow — narrow stone, fish-smell,
the crier going at the far end.*

Glasgow is tighter than London but more awake. Everyone is moving, everyone has
a destination, and a stranger with a coat that is buttoned too deliberately is
not invisible here. The painted heron is on a door in the third close off the
Gallowgate — black paint, wings folded, exactly as described.

But there are soldiers at the close entrance. Two redcoats checking papers —
writs, bills of passage — looking for Jacobite correspondence, they say, though
what they're really doing is making commerce expensive enough to coerce it.

The counting-house man — Jarvie's factor — is visible through the window, ten
feet away, behind a grate. He sees the Fighter. He does not move.

*The Fighter thinks: he will not come to the door while the soldiers are there.
He is waiting to see if I can solve this myself.*

---

### ACT IV — *The Bailie's Parlor, Late Afternoon*
*The letter: sealed, laid on a table between the Fighter and Bailie Nicol Jarvie.
Location: Jarvie's house, off the Saltmarket — good fire, ledgers, candles.*

The factor's message: Bailie Nicol Jarvie must countersign the delivery before
the letter can be received. The Bailie is Rob Roy's kinsman and Diana's sometime
protector; he knew this letter was coming. But he will not countersign without
hearing the story from the Fighter's own mouth — who sent it, how it traveled,
whether the seal is intact.

He sits across the table, his hands wrapped around a cup he is not drinking from,
and he listens with his eyes. He is a magistrate. He has heard a hundred stories
from a hundred travellers and he knows the weight of a true one.

**Jarvie:** *"You carried it three days without breaking it. Why?"*

He already knows the answer. He is asking to see if the Fighter does.

*The Fighter thinks: because she asked me not to. That is the whole answer. It is
enough.*

---

### ACT V — *The Factor's Counting-House, Dusk*
*The letter: broken open at last — by the factor's hands, not the Fighter's.
Location: the counting-house at the heron door — one candle, a scale, a pressed-
tin ceiling.*

The Bailie's countersignature is on a slip of paper, folded inside Jarvie's coat
and handed to the Fighter without ceremony. With it, the factor opens his door.

He reads the letter once. Twice. He looks up.

**Factor:** *"She has released the bond. Entirely. The father's debt to this house
is discharged."*

He writes out a receipt. He stamps it with the counting-house seal — the heron
again, mirror of Diana's cipher. He hands it to the Fighter.

The original letter he folds carefully and burns in the candle. The wax seal
goes last — the small heron, wings folded — melting until the shape is gone.

*The Fighter thinks: the letter came from her hands through mine into fire.
That is the whole distance it needed to travel.*

## Cycle 2: Helen's Justice

### Pass 1 — Plot Précis (Helen's Justice)

At the shore of Loch Lomond, Helen MacGregor — Rob Roy's wife, the most dangerous person in this novel — conducts the execution of Morris, a government spy whose testimony was going to destroy the MacGregor clan and the Osbaldistone credit both. She reads his papers before giving the order. He is thrown into the loch. Helen hands the Fighter the government warrant from Morris's bag — "Take this to my husband. He should know what his enemies were carrying" — and returns to the Highland trees. The Fighter is the only person present who did not participate and will not be presumed to have participated.

### Pass 2 — Fighter's Role (Helen's Justice)

The Fighter arrives at the loch before the execution. Helen allows their presence because she needs a neutral carrier — someone who can bring the warrant to Rob Roy without being a witness anyone could use against him. Commission: witness without intervening, receive the warrant, carry it out of MacGregor territory through the government patrol searching for Morris's horse, and deliver it to Rob Roy with an honest account of what happened.

### Pass 3 — What This Angle Reveals That Cycle 1 Missed

Cycle 1 was Diana Vernon — the moral center of the novel, operating through indirection and sealed paper because the world will not give her any other instrument. *Helen's Justice* reveals the other form of moral authority in the book: direct, physical, final, not subject to appeal. Diana works through letters and codes because she cannot act in the open. Helen acts in the open because she has decided the law is not the appropriate jurisdiction. Both women are right. Both women operate at the outer edge of what the world allows. What cycle 1 missed: the scene at the loch is the only moment in the novel where an execution is presented without authorial condemnation and without authorial approval — Scott gives you Helen's reasons, the horror, and then declines to adjudicate. The Fighter's role is the role Scott assigns the reader: present, unable to stop it, required to carry the evidence.

### 5-Act Vignette Play: *Helen's Justice*

*Storyline 3 — Witness and Delivery. Helen MacGregor executes a government spy at Loch Lomond. TOKEN: Morris's Commission — the government warrant from the spy's satchel, given to the Fighter by Helen, carried to Rob Roy.*

**THE TOKEN: MORRIS'S COMMISSION** — a government warrant, folded three times, the seal broken where Helen opened it, bearing authority to arrest the MacGregor family on charges of debt and sedition. Helen hands it to the Fighter without a word after reading it. In Act V, Rob Roy reads what his enemies were prepared to use.

---

#### ACT I — *The Loch Shore*
*The warrant: in Helen's hands, not yet the Fighter's. Location: LLM — Loch Lomond shore, morning.*

The shore of Loch Lomond is grey and cold. The loch is wide and still. Morris — the government spy, the informer — is sitting on a rock at the water's edge with his hands bound behind him. Two MacGregor men stand behind him. He is not injured. He is afraid.

Helen MacGregor stands between the treeline and the water with Morris's satchel already open in her hand. She looks at the Fighter.

**Helen:** "You did not come with them. You are not one of theirs." A statement, not a question. "Then you are here as a witness. That is well. My husband should know what was done here and why, and the telling should come from someone with no stake in either side."

She holds up the warrant.

**Helen:** "After this is done, you will take what is in this bag to Rob Roy. You will tell him what you saw. You will not add to it or take from it. Can you do that?"

*Insight check DC 12: understand the commission — neutral carriage, honest testimony; neither praise nor condemnation; the account of a witness, not a partisan.*

**Failure:** You begin to say whether what is about to happen is right or wrong. Helen stops you. "I did not ask your judgment. I asked your service." She hands you the satchel anyway, with less confidence than she intended to give it. "Stand there. Do not speak."

**Success:** "I will carry what you give me and say what I saw." Helen holds the satchel out. You take it. She turns back toward Morris without another word. You receive Morris's Commission.

---

#### ACT II — *The Execution*
*The warrant: in the satchel in the Fighter's hands. Location: LLM.*

It happens quickly. The loch is cold. There is the sound of water and then the sound of nothing.

Helen walks back to the treeline without looking at the water. One of the MacGregor men picks up Morris's hat from the rock and throws it in after him.

The Fighter stands at the water's edge with the satchel.

*Composure check DC 12: hold the position of witness — still, present, not moving toward the water, not speaking — through the execution and the silence that follows.*

**Failure:** You move — a single step toward the water. One of Helen's men puts a hand on your arm and holds you still. You are not in danger. But Helen sees it from the treeline, and files it, and the account that reaches Rob Roy may arrive with something missing.

**Success:** You hold still. The water settles. Helen stops at the treeline, looks back once in final assessment, nods, and is gone. You are alone at the loch with the satchel and the sound of the water settling back to itself.

---

#### ACT III — *Out of MacGregor Country*
*The warrant: in the satchel on the Fighter's back. Location: LLM → Highland road.*

The road out of MacGregor territory crosses the government patrol zone. An English dragoon company is searching for Morris's route — they want his horse, and they want Morris. They will stop anyone on this road and search their satchel. The warrant is, specifically, proof that the government had standing arrest plans for a prominent Highland family. In English hands, it becomes a diplomatic problem. In the Fighter's hands, it is evidence of a crime.

*Stealth check DC 13: take the high ridge path above the patrol road and cross the exposed section before the dragoons complete their sweep.*

**Failure:** A scout on the ridge sees movement. You hide the warrant under a flat stone, clear the patrol, retrieve it. But you are now an hour behind Rob Roy's messenger, who is already warning him that strangers are in the glen.

**Success:** You cross at the exact pace the patrol's pattern requires and descend to the far side with the warrant intact and the patrol none the wiser.

---

#### ACT IV — *The Highland Road*
*The warrant: in the satchel, past the patrol zone. Location: Highland road, LLM toward GLN.*

Beyond the patrol zone a MacGregor outrider intercepts you on the track. He knows where you are going. He offers the short way to the glen — a ford route government horses cannot follow — on the condition that the satchel stays on your person and dry.

*Athletics check DC 13: cross the Highland ford in full gear with the satchel kept dry.*

**Failure:** The satchel takes water. The warrant is legible but the government seal is partially dissolved — its provenance is harder to assert with certainty when Rob Roy reads it.

**Success:** You cross clean. The outrider brings you to the glen entrance in half the expected time with the satchel dry and the seal undamaged.

---

#### ACT V — *Rob Roy's Glen*
*The warrant: ready to deliver. Location: GLN — glen shelter, Highland interior.*

Rob Roy reads the warrant without expression and sets it on the rock beside him. He looks at you.

**Rob Roy:** "You were at the loch. Tell me what happened."

He wants to know what his wife did — not softened, not defended, not condemned. The exact sequence. He has been married to Helen MacGregor for twenty years. He does not need the event translated for him.

*Persuasion check DC 12: give a true account without opinion attached — the shore, her words, the commission, the execution, the man's hat thrown in after him, the silence, the satchel. Let him hear it whole.*

**Failure:** You add a qualification — something that softens one moment. Rob Roy hears the gap. "You are being kind to me about it. I do not need that." He takes the warrant anyway. The commission is complete but the account is not entirely what Helen sent you to deliver.

**Success:** You say it exactly. Rob Roy is quiet for a long time. Then he folds the warrant twice and puts it inside his coat.

**Rob Roy:** "That is my wife. That is exactly my wife."

He pulls the ring from his finger — not a valuable ring, just the one he always wears — and holds it out.

**Rob Roy:** "You carried this clean. Go back to Osbaldistone. Tell Frank that Rob Roy still has an account to settle, and what I just read makes the balance clearer."

You receive Rob Roy's Ring.

*The Fighter thinks: I was asked to carry an honest account of an irreversible act to the person who authorized it, without opinion. I carried it exactly. Helen and Rob Roy and the man in the loch are the parties to this. I was the distance between what happened at the shore and what he now knows in the glen.*

## §SDQ-03 — "The Bailie's Coulter"

**Seed:** Storyline 1 — Bailie Nicol Jarvie defends the company at the Clachan of Aberfoyle with a red-hot coulter pulled from the fire  
**Route:** ABF → GLA → WM  
**New nodes:** ABF (Aberfoyle — Highland Gateway Inn)

---

**ACT I — "The Inn at Aberfoyle" (Aberfoyle)**

The brawl at the Clachan of Aberfoil begins without announcement. Two English soldiers have followed the party in from the road and one has laid hands on a Highland drover. The room is full of men who will fight before they think.

Bailie Nicol Jarvie is at the fireplace. He seizes the red-hot coulter from the grate with both hands — a plow-blade left by the farrier that morning — and holds it before him. His coat is already catching. He does not notice. The soldiers notice.

"In the name of the king and the law," he says. Which is entirely true, and also perfectly ridiculous, and also completely effective.

TOKEN: `jarvie_coulter_attestation` — a sworn notarial note in the Bailie's own hand, written that night: jurisdiction exercised beyond his formal territory, in defense of a party under his protection; the argument that authority travels with the person who holds it.

*Skill check: Perception, DC 11 — see that the soldier to the Bailie's left is not watching the coulter; he is about to go wide; clear the angle before he reaches the Bailie's side.*

*On success:* You step into the gap. The soldier pulls up short. The Bailie carries the coulter out the door with his dignity and his coat both on fire.
*On fail:* The soldier reaches the Bailie's shoulder. You haul him back by the collar. The Bailie escapes; your coat takes the burn.

*TOKEN granted Act I.*

---

**ACT II — "The Road to Glasgow" (road ABF→GLA)**

South of Aberfoyle a Highland clansman blocks the track — one of the Bailie's kin, sent with a complaint: Jarvie overstepped his territory and his office. "He has no authority above the Endrick. What he did was a fat man waving a hot blade."

*Skill check: Persuasion, DC 12 — the law extends as far as the person who holds it carries it; argue this without condescension.*

*On success:* The clansman listens. He does not agree. He steps aside.
*On fail:* He demands a written acknowledgment. You write it. It will travel to Weimar alongside the attestation — the document and its challenge, both preserved.

*TOKEN check Act II.*

---

**ACT III — "The Gallowgate" (Glasgow)**

At the Bailie's counting-house a rival magistrate has already filed a formal complaint: Jarvie's attestation is legally invalid because he exceeded his jurisdiction. If upheld, the complaint invalidates the document before it travels.

*Skill check: Investigation, DC 12 — the complaint is filed in the wrong court; a magistrate cannot invalidate another magistrate's jurisdiction claim in the same court; find the procedural flaw.*

*On success:* The flaw is clear. The complaint exceeds its own authority. The attestation stands.
*On fail:* You miss it. The Bailie's clerk finds it two hours later and adds a covering note explaining the defect.

*TOKEN check Act III.*

---

**ACT IV — "Jarvie's Parlor" (Glasgow)**

A government lawyer arrives wanting the attestation as prosecution evidence against the MacGregor clan: it places MacGregor men at the Clachan during a civil disturbance. Surrendering it converts proof of personal jurisdiction into evidence of clansmen's presence.

*Skill check: Insight, DC 11 — see the inversion before you hand anything over.*

*On success:* You see the reversal in time. The attestation stays in the Fighter's coat.
*On fail:* You begin to hand it over. The Bailie's clerk intercepts the transfer with an expression of patient disappointment and recovers it.

*TOKEN check Act IV.*

---

**ACT V — "The Archive" (Weimar)**

Sweelinck reads the attestation.

"He pulled a hot blade from a fire to defend a room full of people who would not have defended him. He wrote this document afterward to explain why he was legally entitled to do it."

He writes: *Jurisdiction in Contested Territory, First Entry. The Jarvie Attestation: jurisdiction is not bounded by the map of lawful territory alone; it travels with the person willing to carry it beyond the boundary; the magistrate who uses his body as the instrument of law has extended it precisely as far as he stood. Cross-file: Acts of Completion (willingness was the extension); Physical Courage as Legal Instrument, first entry.*

*TOKEN taken Act V.*

---

### Theme

**"Bailie Nicol Jarvie held a red-hot coulter and the law traveled with him to the Highland inn; jurisdiction is bounded not by the map but by the person willing to carry it beyond the boundary; the archive names this Jurisdiction in Contested Territory, first entry."**

## §SDQ-04 — "Rob Roy's Word"

**Seed:** Storyline 4 — Rob Roy gives the Fighter a verbal passage-token to carry to a clan sentinel; the token is memory; the quest is not losing it  
**Route:** GLN → ABF → EDI → WM  
**New nodes:** GLN (Highland Glen, Rob Roy's territory); EDI (Edinburgh, Scottish capital)

---

**ACT I — "The Glen" (Highland Glen)**

Rob Roy tears a strip of oilcloth from the satchel lining and writes three words in Scots Gaelic with a charred stick — a phrase that is both a greeting and a credential, understood by every MacGregor sentinel from Aberfoyle to Rannoch.

"Say this exactly at the Aberfoyle crossroads. Not before. Not after. Not approximately." He folds the oilcloth in four. "You will be asked where you got it. Tell them a man in a glen gave it to you. That is entirely true."

TOKEN: `macgregor_passage_note` — three Gaelic words on oilcloth, charred-stick ink; the written record of an oral credential; a phrase that works only when spoken aloud in the right place.

*Skill check: Perception or Insight, DC 12 — understand what the note is: not a letter but a proof of relationship that requires voice, not paper.*

*On success:* You see it. You put the oilcloth inside your boot, not your coat. Rob Roy nods.
*On fail:* You put it in your coat. Rob Roy says: "Boot. Not coat. They check the coat first." You move it.

*TOKEN granted Act I.*

---

**ACT II — "The Aberfoyle Crossroads" (Aberfoyle)**

A MacGregor sentinel in shepherd's clothes leans on a gate at the crossroads. He is waiting. Note first, phrase second, pause, wait for the response — in that order.

*Skill check: Persuasion, DC 13 — speak the phrase with the exact register Rob Roy used: not a password recited but a greeting made to someone you know, without having met.*

*On success:* The sentinel responds with a single word and opens the gate.
*On fail:* Your register is too formal. He stares a long moment, then gestures you through. "Rob Roy's man," he tells the man behind the wall. "Aye, but soft."

*TOKEN check Act II.*

---

**ACT III — "The Capital" (Edinburgh)**

At the Edinburgh customs house a government lawyer stops you. He has seen the oilcloth and wants to know what it says. "Jacobite correspondence is prosecutable. If that is a cipher, you are in difficulty."

*Skill check: Deception, DC 12 — establish the note as a personal memorandum: a phrase from a Highland song, written against forgetting; not a political document.*

*On success:* He accepts the framing with visible skepticism and lets you pass.
*On fail:* He holds the note one hour for translation. The translator finds it is a greeting, not a cipher. Released.

*TOKEN check Act III.*

---

**ACT IV — "The Royal Mile" (Edinburgh)**

A Jacobite agent recognizes the oilcloth format at a printer's stall on the Royal Mile. He knows what notes written on torn oilcloth in charred stick mean. He wants it — not to use it, but because possessing it proves something about the clan network.

*Skill check: Persuasion or Deception, DC 11 — decline without naming what you know about the note's function.*

*On success:* You tell him it is a personal keepsake. He does not believe you and lets you go.
*On fail:* He follows you for an hour through the Old Town closes. You lose him below the castle.

*TOKEN check Act IV.*

---

**ACT V — "The Archive" (Weimar)**

Sweelinck reads the oilcloth.

"Three words. A greeting that is also a credential. It was oral until someone wrote it down, and now it is a document — but only if the voice is added. The paper enables the voice. It does not replace it."

He writes: *Oral Traditions — Written Record, First Entry. The MacGregor Passage-Note: a verbal safe-conduct reduced to ink; the writing converts oral function to archival form without replacing it; the note retains its oral requirement; the paper enables the voice it cannot substitute.*

*TOKEN taken Act V.*

---

### Theme

**"Three Gaelic words on oilcloth; spoken at a crossroads they open a gate; written and filed at Weimar they open a category; the archive notes that the paper enables the voice and cannot replace it."**

## §SDQ-05 — "Rashleigh's Double Game"

Rashleigh's traveling case is open on the hall table. Two bundles of paper, each tied with red ribbon. He has prepared the same transaction for two incompatible masters.

TOKEN: `rashleigh_double_papers` — both bundles; proof that the identical commercial instrument can simultaneously bind two opposed parties; valid to both, honor-able to neither.

*Skill check: Investigation, DC 12 — identify which is which without disturbing the case's arrangement.*

*On success:* The Edinburgh bundle is on top. You take it, replace it with a folded blank sheet. Rashleigh will not notice until London.
*On fail:* You take both bundles. The case is obviously lighter. You hear Rashleigh's step on the gravel and move fast for the servants' passage.

*TOKEN granted Act I.*

---

**ACT II — "The Border Road" (road OBH→LON)**

At Carter Bar a government inspection post is checking documents moving south for Jacobite fund transfers. The Edinburgh bundle describes a commercial transaction that is also a fund transfer.

*Skill check: Deception, DC 13 — the commercial language is dry enough to bore an inspector; present it as routine trade correspondence.*

*On success:* He reads the top sheet and loses interest.
*On fail:* He copies the Edinburgh counterparty name into his register. The name will eventually attract attention. You are past the border.

*TOKEN check Act II.*

---

**ACT III — "The City" (London)**

A Hanoverian intelligence agent has been following the case since Northumberland. He wants the London bundle privately — he has been paid by the Hanoverian factor to suppress any competing commercial claim.

*Skill check: Persuasion, DC 12 — both bundles are evidence of a category of fraud, not of a specific political allegiance; surrendering one destroys what proves the double-dealing.*

*On success:* The agent accepts the distinction grudgingly. Both bundles travel.
*On fail:* He takes the London bundle. You are left with the Edinburgh bundle and a seizure receipt — which is itself evidence of the same fraud from a different angle.

*TOKEN check Act III.*

---

**ACT IV — "The Courier's Inn" (London)**

A Jacobite lawyer locates you near Cheapside. He argues the Edinburgh bundle was fabricated by the government to entrap Jacobite agents — the document is false.

*Skill check: Insight, DC 11 — both bundles are genuine; neither party is being framed; Rashleigh created them both and sold both contracts.*

*On success:* The standard fabrication defense. You see it clearly. Both documents travel.
*On fail:* The lawyer plants doubt that lingers through the archive delivery.

*TOKEN check Act IV.*

---

**ACT V — "The Archive" (Weimar)**

Sweelinck reads both bundles side by side.

"One transaction. Two counterparties. Both genuine. Neither cancels the other. What was promised was impossible to honor simultaneously — which is a different problem from fraud."

The Rashleigh Double Papers: the instrument simultaneously genuine to two opposed parties; validity to one does not invalidate the other; the category is not fraud — both parties received exactly what was promised; what was promised could not both be kept.*

*TOKEN taken Act V.*

## §SDQ-07 — "The Ford at Aberfoil"

**Seed:** Storyline 7 — Rob Roy, bound, dives into the Forth and swims under fire to the far bank; the Fighter holds the position of neutral witness  
**Route:** ABF → GLA → WM  
**New nodes:** none

---

**ACT I — "The Ford" (Aberfoyle)**

The ford at Aberfoil on the River Forth above the village. Rob Roy MacGregor stands in the road with his hands bound behind him. Six soldiers, a sergeant, a horse. He has finally been taken — or thinks they have him.

He looks at the Fighter. It is a specific look: a man who has already made his decision and needs one thing from the person he is looking at. Not rescue. Witness. An honest account of what happens next.

TOKEN: `rob_roy_escape_deposition` — a sworn deposition in the Fighter's hand: a true account of the ford, the jump, the swim, the shots; the neutral record of a politically inconvenient event.

*Skill check: Insight, DC 11 — understand the look; not help, witness; the account that every party will try to revise.*

*On success:* You take the witness position — clear view, not moving. Rob Roy nods once.
*On fail:* You step toward him. He shakes his head slightly. You step back.

*TOKEN granted Act I.*

---

**ACT II — "The River" (Aberfoyle)**

Rob Roy dives sideways off the bank. The soldiers fire. He swims under water, surfacing twice. He reaches the far bank and is gone into the alders before they can reload. The ford is quiet.

The soldier next to you asks what you saw.

*Skill check: Persuasion, DC 12 — give the account of what you witnessed without adding anything that could be called assistance: neither helping nor condemning; the witness account.*

*On success:* "He was bound. He jumped. He swam. He reached the other bank. The shots missed." The soldier accepts it, unsatisfied.
*On fail:* You add a detail that implies you knew where the bank was shallowest. The soldier marks you as a party.

*TOKEN check Act II.*

---

**ACT III — "The Sergeant's Report" (road ABF→GLA)**

On the road back toward Glasgow the sergeant stops you. He is writing his report and wants your signature on a version that describes the escape as "assisted by an unknown civilian."

"Sign it. You were there. You could have raised a cry."

*Skill check: Persuasion, DC 13 — give the exact sequence twice; it cannot be revised if it is precise enough.*

*On success:* He abandons "assisted" after you recite the sequence twice. He files "escaped during river crossing."
*On fail:* He files his version. You file a counter-deposition at the nearest justice's office. Both versions travel.

*TOKEN check Act III.*

---

**ACT IV — "Glasgow Justice" (Glasgow)**

In Glasgow the government account already circulates: Rob Roy "escaped with assistance." A magistrate wants a sworn counter-deposition naming the sequence in full.

*Skill check: History, DC 11 — reconstruct the exact sequence from memory with enough precision that the deposition is unimpeachable; the order of events is the argument.*

*On success:* Every second accounted for. The magistrate reads it and stamps it received.
*On fail:* One moment is uncertain — the second surfacing. The deposition notes the uncertainty. It is weaker but still honest.

*TOKEN check Act IV.*

---

**ACT V — "The Archive" (Weimar)**

Sweelinck reads the deposition.

"He was bound. He jumped into a river. He swam under fire. He reached the far bank. No one assisted him. This is what happened. The gap in a sequence is where the revision lives."

He writes: *Politically Inconvenient Events — Neutral Deposition, First Entry. The Ford at Aberfoil: the escape witnessed and recorded; the neutral account that resists institutional revision; the complete sequence as the argument; the archive holds what no official account will hold.*

*TOKEN taken Act V.*
*questComplete: true*

## Cycle 8 — Angle: *Morris's Bill of Exchange — The Commercial Instrument That Became a Political Target*

*All prior cycles reviewed. Source knowledge used directly.*

**What prior cycles missed:** Morris carries a bill of exchange for a large sum when he meets Frank Osbaldistone at the outset. The bill of exchange becomes the target of multiple parties — the MacGregors want it, the Jacobite plotters want it, the government wants it back. A bill of exchange is a commercial document: it authorizes payment from one party to another. In the middle of the Scottish Highlands in 1715, it is also political currency. Its commercial validity is irrelevant to everyone who is chasing it.

**This cycle introduces:** *The commercial instrument that became a political target — a bill of exchange whose monetary value became irrelevant when it entered a zone of conflict where its possession was the point.*
