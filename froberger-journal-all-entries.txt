FROBERGER'S JOURNAL — ALL 41 ENTRIES
======================================


ENTRY 1  [City Gate — CI]
The gate officer reviewed my papers three times. The third time was identical to the first — same questions, same scrutiny, same hands turning the pages. When I mentioned this, he looked surprised, then irritated. He approved my entry immediately.

I have since asked myself: did he approve me because my papers were correct, or because I named the pattern and he needed to end it? I cannot determine which. I'm not sure the distinction matters at the gate. It might matter later.

I am going to spend a great deal of time in this world being approved by people who cannot explain why. I think I should decide now whether that bothers me. I have decided: it does not. This is the first decision I have made with my eyes open.


ENTRY 2  [Tavern — TV]  ★ READ ALOUD
The bard at the tavern played four songs. After each one, he looked up before the last note finished — scanning the room for the response before the sound had settled. The room was full of people who needed to be seen needing something.

My host was the barkeep. He told me about his father's tavern, and his father's father's tavern, and the recipe for the house sauce that had not changed in ninety years. He was not telling me about the recipe. He was telling me that he comes from people who mattered.

I come from people who mattered too. I stopped talking about it somewhere before this entry. I am trying to remember when. I am writing this in my room, in the dark, trying to determine whether the silence about it is wisdom or loneliness.

I think it is both. I think most things are.


ENTRY 3  [Crypt — CR]
The Crypt Key was in a box inside a box inside a locked drawer. Someone hid it carefully. I spent time wondering who, and why, and what they were protecting from what. I spent longer than the route requires. I'm not yet moving at the pace the quest demands, and I think that is fine. I think I should write that down before I start moving faster, so I have a record of thinking it.


ENTRY 4  [Bazaar — BA]
The fence gave me three prices: one when he thought I was a pilgrim, one when I said I was a courier, and one when I put the manifest on the table. The third price was the real one. He didn't apologize for the other two. The performance was the negotiation.

I have been wearing a kind of mask since I started this journey. It is not a dishonest mask — it is the mask of someone who knows what they are doing. I put it on when I stopped explaining the routes to strangers and started just walking them. It fits better now than it did. I am not sure when I stopped noticing it.

He packed my commission in oilcloth and did not watch me go. He had already moved on to the next version of himself.


ENTRY 5  [Sewers — SE]
The sewer-keeper had worked the pumps for thirty-one years. He told me the overflow valves should be replaced every six years; his predecessor replaced them every six years; he replaced them every eight years because the budget required it; and every eight years, for thirty-one years, there had been a minor flood, and every time he wrote in the maintenance log: valves due for replacement.

He showed me the log. Thirty-one years of the same notation. Each one in his careful handwriting. Each one noting the same cause. He was proud of the record.

I asked whether the budget could be adjusted. He said that was not his department. He wrote a very accurate entry in the log. I watched him do it.


ENTRY 6  [Slums — SL]
The district is called many names, none of them its own. The people who live there name it after what it lacks — the market it isn't, the quarter it borders, the prosperity that arrives at its edge and stops.

My host's daughter spent the evening mending a coat she described, in three separate sentences, as belonging to the merchant's wife on the hill. She was not mending a coat. She was practicing something.

There is a type of wanting that becomes a shape of mind. It is not greed. Greed wants to have. This is different — it wants to be. I have felt it twice in my life. I know the texture of it. I did not say anything. She already knew what she was practicing. She didn't need me to name it.


ENTRY 7  [Inn — IN]  ★ READ ALOUD
The innkeeper's daughter is learning to read. She showed me the letters — she wrote them on a scrap of flour-sack paper with a stub of charcoal, pressing hard, the way children do when they haven't learned to trust that soft marks still count. She showed me three letters and said their sounds.

I showed her how to write expedition. She asked what it meant. I said: going somewhere you haven't been before, on purpose. She wrote it back, larger than I wrote it, because she was still pressing hard.

I am writing this in a room that smells of old hay and soap. The hay is from a bed that has had forty years of travelers in it and the soap is from someone who cares about forty years of travelers. I am not usually aware of soap.

I think this is the last night I will be unaware of soap. The route gets harder from here.


ENTRY 8  [Alley — AL]
The alley runs between the money-changer and the apothecary. My host, the money-changer's assistant, let me sleep on a pallet behind the counter because the inns were full. He told me about a deal he had made that morning — favorable terms, immediate return, a counterparty who offered more than the rate. He was pleased.

I asked about the counterparty's business. He didn't know. I asked about their route. He didn't know. I asked how they had found him specifically, at this specific alley, at this specific hour. He paused for the first time.

I didn't say anything else. The deal was already done. The pleasure of the morning was the most real thing about it. Concrete beats abstract every time, right up until the moment it doesn't.


ENTRY 9  [Blacksmith Quarter — BQ]
The swordsmith had been making blades in this quarter for twenty-two years. When I asked about his process, he gave me a demonstration — unhurried, confident, beautiful to watch. When I asked why he quenched in brine rather than oil, he stopped.

He looked at me for three seconds in a way that told me someone had raised this before, probably more than once, and he had spent years becoming correct and the correctness had not fully healed the question. He gave me the answer: precise, accurate, and slightly too long.

I do not know enough about metallurgy to have been a real threat. He knew that. He answered the ghost of someone else. I received the answer anyway. It was the right answer. It was also an old wound with a good explanation built over it, and from the outside you can only tell the difference by the length of the reply.


ENTRY 10  [Merchant Ship — MS]
The merchant ship had a library. Three shelves, bolted to the wall, with ropes across the books so they don't fall in heavy weather. I read for two hours before remembering I was supposed to be looking for the Shard. The Proprietor Dusk found me. She said: "You read like someone who still thinks they have time."

I think she was right. I think I did. I think that is the last time I did.


ENTRY 11  [Forest — FO]
The ranger had mapped every viable route through the forest before her twenty-fifth year. I was told this by two different people before I reached her camp — they each said it the same way, which meant it was a story the region had already finished writing about her.

She had not updated the maps since her twenty-fifth year.

I asked. She said the forest didn't change. I had walked three hours through the forest and it had changed considerably — blowdown in the northern passage, a new stream course in the lowland. She knew about both. She mentioned them after I described them. She said she was planning to update the maps.

The maps on her table were clean. They had been clean for a long time.


ENTRY 12  [Mountain — MT]
My guide through the pass was efficient and quiet and knew the mountain the way you know something you have stopped needing to look at. On the second night, at a fire, he talked for two hours about an avalanche that had happened fourteen years ago — with the precision of someone who had not talked about it in a long time and had therefore had fourteen years to organize it.

He was not organizing grief. He was organizing the facts of it: snow depth, slope angle, party composition, the decisions made and their order. The grief was in the cracks between the facts. He had sealed the cracks with accuracy.

I said: that must have been a hard day.

He said: yes. He did not speak again until morning. But something in his posture changed. I think the cracks let some air in. I don't know if that was the right thing. I am not a guide. I am someone who passes through.


ENTRY 13  [Catacombs — CA]  ★ READ ALOUD
The catacombs are tended by a keeper named Bors. He brought a lamp and said very little. At the lower level he showed me the tombs of three magisters whose names appear in every legal document the city has produced for a hundred years. He showed them with no expression. On the way back up, passing the tombs of lesser figures, he paused at one and said: this one was better, actually. The others got the recognition. This one did the work.

He said it quietly. He had clearly said it many times, always quietly.

I asked about the lesser figure. Bors talked for twenty minutes. He knew the name, the dates, the body of work, the reason for the obscurity. He had made himself the world's authority on a wrong that no one else was tracking.

There is a form of devotion that looks like advocacy but is actually suffering. I left a coin at the lesser tomb. Bors watched me do it. It was not enough. It was the only thing I had to give.


ENTRY 14  [Dockmaster's — DK]  ★ READ ALOUD
Magistra Muffat said: He was careful. Couriers who are careful last three years on this route. He lasted thirty-eight months.

She said it matter-of-factly, the way a person says a fact they have already grieved. Thirty-eight months is not an abstraction to her. It is a number she arrived at by subtraction, on a specific day, in a specific office, with a ledger open in front of her.

I have been calculating things since I started this journey. Distances, encounter probabilities, optimal approach vectors for the terrain types I've crossed. I am not sure when I started. I think it was before the inn with the daughter who presses hard.

Muffat gave me the Shard without ceremony. I received it the same way. I don't know if that was correct. I don't know what the correct way to receive a surrender document is.


ENTRY 15  [Sea Cavern — SC]
The captain of the cavern barge — it is not properly a barge, but he calls it that — has navigated the underground sea for sixteen years. He told me this in the first two minutes. He told me he was the only person in the region who knew the tidal patterns beneath the limestone shelf. He told me his father had been a fisherman, which is relevant because he is not a fisherman, he is something his father's world had no category for.

He navigated the passage with real skill. The sixteen years were genuine. The tidal knowledge was genuine.

None of this required the telling. The cavern required only the doing. He told it anyway, once at the start and once in the middle and once again when I thanked him at the far end.

I have begun to notice when I do this. I have begun to notice that I almost never do. I am not sure which is the warning.


ENTRY 16  [Hag's Shire — HS]
The youngest Crone makes distinctions. The Primarch makes none. The difference between them is that one sees people as different kinds of useful and the other doesn't see them at all. I asked Wane which was better. She said: "You ask that like you've already decided."

I hadn't decided yet. I think I decided three conversations later and didn't notice until now, writing this, which is probably the right amount of lag. The decisions that matter don't announce themselves.


ENTRY 17  [Midlands — MI]
The midland family fed me and gave me the grain-store loft. The husband spoke to me about routes. The wife spoke to me about harvest yields and drainage problems and the price of oats at the market town, which she had calculated precisely — the way someone speaks to topics they have mastered quietly, without recognition, over a long period.

Her husband listened. He added, once, that she was the one who managed the accounts. He said it as information. She received it the same way. They had long since resolved between themselves what the world outside had not resolved: that the categories were invented, and the work was real, and the work was hers.

The arrangements people make in private, without witnesses — those are the real contracts. The ones outside are just paper.


ENTRY 18  [Highlands — HL]
My host in the highlands had a good house, a fire, and no discernible project. He had been here for four years, having left the city after a specific event he mentioned once and did not elaborate. He was waiting for something to become clear.

He was very comfortable. He had solved for comfort completely. He had not solved for direction.

I stayed two nights because the pass was iced. On the second night I asked what he was waiting for. He said he would know it when he saw it. I think he was right about that. I also think he was waiting very slowly and that slow waiting and not waiting are difficult to distinguish from inside.

I left while the ice was still soft. The pass was hard. I was glad to be back on it.


ENTRY 19  [Swamp — SW]
The herbalist's apprentice was twenty-two. His name was Luc. He decided he was ready for field work without asking his teacher whether she agreed. He went in and didn't come back.

I should have arrived three months ago. I didn't know the route. I know it now. I am going to fix this three months too late and the three months are not something that can be fixed.

I am writing this down because I want to be angry about it, and I think the anger is correct, and I think I should not let myself get used to this. The anger is the part of me that knows Luc's name.


ENTRY 20  [Ocean Crossing — OC]  ★ READ ALOUD
The ocean passage took six days. The crew of twenty-four had been together for three seasons. By the end of the first day I understood the hierarchy — not the official one, the real one. The real one was based on who laughed first when the bosun made a joke, and who waited, and who watched before deciding.

On day four, one of the inner four said something I privately assessed as wrong — a point of navigation, confidently stated. Six of the crew agreed. No one asked me. I did not volunteer.

On day five the wrong thing was quietly corrected by the bosun, attributed to wind shift. The man who had been wrong nodded. The six who had agreed nodded. No one named what had happened.

I have learned to carry my own assessment separately from my visible agreement. I do not know if this is wisdom or cowardice. I think it is both, weighted differently depending on the stakes.


ENTRY 21  [Pirate Cove — PC]
The crew was between engagements, which is the pirate term for broke. My host had sailed under three captains, two flags, and one period of honest employment she described as the worst year of her life.

She was loyal to her current captain with complete sincerity. She told me this. She also told me she had felt this way about all her captains, right up until the moment the moment changed. She said this without embarrassment — as a point of information about herself, the way a person names a weather pattern they've noticed.

I asked what made the moment change. She said: the story stops being worth the cost.

I have been thinking about this for three days. I believe in this mission more than I have believed in anything. But I am honest enough to remember that she believes in her current captain more than she has believed in anything, and she is already narrating the end.


ENTRY 22  [Floodgate — FL]
Aldous ordered six boats. He ordered them before knowing whether he needed them, because he was optimistic. I tried to remember the last time I ordered something before knowing whether I needed it. I cannot. Optimism requires believing the future will be different from the past. I know too much about the past now.

He inspected the boats while telling me about them. He was confident they were good boats. They are good boats. I could see why they were good boats in the first twenty seconds. He talked about them for six minutes. I did not interrupt him.

I want to record that: I did not interrupt him. I think that was the right thing. I think I will think about it later as something I was pleased about. I think later I will not remember to be pleased about it.


ENTRY 23  [Deep Sea — DS]  ★ READ ALOUD
The Charybdis is not a monster. It is a consequence. The thing in the trench that causes the water displacement that causes the whirlpool — that is a monster. What I fought today was the evidence of something I haven't reached yet.

I have been thinking about evidence. Muffat's thirty-eight months. The claw marks in the crypt. The satchel at the altar edge, still packed. The evidence is always the same shape: someone who got close, and then stopped, and left a mark that points toward what stopped them.

I am following a trail of absences.

The navigator has been on the deep routes for thirty years. He folded his charts the way you fold something you trust completely. I am going to have to come back to this trench. I can feel it. The True Leviathan is down there. I know how that sentence sounds. I am writing it anyway.


ENTRY 24  [Camelot — KT]
The knights are extraordinarily courteous. The courtesy is so practiced and total that it functions as armor — not metaphorical armor, actual armor, which they are also wearing. Every exchange is wrapped in form: the bow, the honorific, the specific protocol for sitting and standing and speaking and silence.

Under the courtesy: tournament grounds with fresh-churned earth from morning exercises, an equipment shed that smells of blood they have not fully cleaned from the training weapons, and the way the youngest knight watched the oldest one across the hall in a way that had nothing courteous in it at all.

I have spent months solving problems through knowledge and preparation. I have noticed that the people who solve problems with knowledge are treated as curiosities, and the people who solve them with force are treated as authorities.

I write this down because I want to remember that the courtesy was real. The aggression was also real. Both things were true at once, in the same hall, wearing the same armor.


ENTRY 25  [Oriental Palace — OP]
The palace is three hundred years old. The court does not know this — I do not mean they are ignorant of the date, they know the date, they celebrate it. I mean they experience the present court as unprecedented. The crises are unprecedented. The alliances are unprecedented. The genius of the current ministers is unprecedented.

My host, a court historian, had spent twenty years reading the palace's own records. She showed me three crises from the second dynasty structurally identical to the present one — with the tired patience of someone who has pointed at evidence so many times that the pointing has become the whole job.

I asked if anyone listened. She said: they listen, they understand, and then they proceed as though the information doesn't apply to them specifically.

I thought about all the times I have received wisdom from someone who walked a route before me and then proceeded as though it didn't apply to me specifically. I did not tell her. She was tired enough already.


ENTRY 26  [Ghost Crypt — GC]
The Void Shaman's journal mentions Birka forty times. The Master says. The Master believes. I read it twice. The second time I read it faster — I already knew the structure, I was looking for the data. The data was on page seven. I wish I had taken longer with the first read. Not for tactical reasons. Because the first time you read something you are still a person who doesn't know what it says. The second time you are looking for the parts that matter. Those are different experiences and you only get one of them.


ENTRY 27  [Vampire Castle — VC]  ★ READ ALOUD
The castle is maintained past its natural end. Everything in it is preserved — the portraits, the silverware, the ritual of the evening meal at the same hour in the same chairs with the same number of candles. My host has occupied this castle for an unspecified long time. The servants do not age in any visible way, which I note without elaboration.

Over dinner my host spoke at length about the improvements planned for the east wing. The roof of the east wing is partially collapsed. The improvements have been scheduled for spring for longer than I can estimate.

There is a variety of pretense that is not really pretense — where the person speaking knows the truth and the person listening knows the truth and both parties maintain the fiction because the fiction is the last livable thing. I ate the meal. I expressed appreciation for the candles. The east wing remained collapsed, behind us, in the dark.

I left before dawn. I did not say goodbye. I think that was correct.


ENTRY 28  [Market Crossroads — MC]
I am faster now. I want to write something true about that. The truth is: faster means finishing sooner, and finishing sooner means the person you helped has more of their life back, and that is the reason to be fast. I keep telling myself that. It is true. I am not sure it is the whole truth. I am not sure what the rest of the truth is yet. I will write it when I find it.

I have a feeling I will not find it.


ENTRY 29  [Island Settlement — IS]
The island has seven families and one boat. The decision of when to use the boat is made by consensus, which takes approximately four days per decision. I observed three decisions in twelve days. None of them were wrong. None of them were made for the reasons stated.

The stated reasons were practical: weather, season, inventory. The actual reasons were relational: which family's patriarch had spoken last, what old debt was being honored or avoided, whose daughter had recently done something that required the others to find a way to be generous. The practical reasons were the clothing. The relational reasons were the body.

The island has been stable for four generations. The relational reasoning works. It just does not know it is reasoning. It believes itself to be practical. This is probably why it works.


ENTRY 30  [Beach Resort — BE]
The beach resort serves the prosperous. I slept in a fisherman's shed at the far end of the strand because the resort was full. Through the evening I watched the guests perform rest — elaborate because the stakes of appearing rested are higher than the stakes of being rested, sincere because they had practiced so long they no longer knew the difference. They arranged themselves against the light. They selected companions who would serve as favorable contrast.

I have been resting badly for six months. My sleep is functional and brief and I wake already calculating the next leg.

I watched the resort guests for three hours and felt, once, a deep envy of their ability to be the main character of a pleasant story. Then I felt the envy pass.

I think what passed was not the envy but the belief that it was available to me. Those are different things. One of them leaves you lighter.


ENTRY 31  [Desert — DE]  ★ READ ALOUD
The caravan master cannot file an insurance claim for "mummified pharaoh destroyed six camels." She told me this with the focused anger of someone who has been told by every institutional structure available that their problem doesn't fit the categories the institution recognizes.

She is right. It doesn't. Three hundred years of scholarship created the pharaoh and the insurance adjusters can't touch either of them.

I fixed it in one afternoon. She paid me. She is already filling in the resolution field on the claim form. The adjuster can assume what he likes.

I wrote "one afternoon" and then sat with it. Three centuries. One afternoon. I am not sure what to do with that ratio. I used to be certain it was a good thing.


ENTRY 32  [Market Quarter — MQ]
The market quarter is where the city performs itself at maximum volume. Everyone plays the role that makes the transaction possible: the merchant performs certainty, the buyer performs indifference, the appraiser performs neutrality, the guard performs authority. None of these performances are dishonest. They are the mechanism by which value gets assigned and transferred.

I watched an appraiser assess a piece of metalwork for forty minutes. The assessment was genuine expertise. The forty minutes was a performance of forty minutes — not because the expertise required it, but because the buyer needed to see forty minutes in order to believe the price.

I have been thinking about whether the work I do is the performance or the function. I think it is the function. I think I have been performing the function for long enough that I have forgotten the performance. I think that is what efficiency costs.


ENTRY 33  [Junction — JU]
Mael held the formulary without opening it. Just held it. I recognized that. I have held things that way — objects that mean more than their contents, that you hold instead of opening because opening is the last thing you can do with them and you want to wait until you're ready.

I have fewer of those now. I don't know if that's growth or loss. When I try to name it I reach for the word efficiency and that is probably the answer.


ENTRY 34  [Yard/Camp — YC]
The trapper has worked this watershed for eleven years and is, by every account, the best reader of the terrain in the region. His equipment is old — not worn-out old, deliberately old. He replaces nothing before it fails completely. He showed me a snare repaired so many times the original material was a minority. He was proud of this. He called it good workmanship, which it was. It was also a way of never committing to new equipment, which would commit him to new routes, which would commit him to being a beginner again in terrain he hadn't mapped yet.

I think about the routes I haven't walked. I think about the specific feeling, early in this journey, of arriving at a node without data and having to read it from scratch. I think I liked that feeling once.

I think he liked it once too. I think the repaired snare is his way of still being the person who liked it without having to feel it again.


ENTRY 35  [Sweelinck's Quarter — SQ]
Sweelinck's handwriting gets smaller toward the end of each page, as though he's trying to fit more in before the space runs out. I recognize that. I did that in the early entries. I don't do it anymore. I use a second page.

I do not think this is better. I think it is just what you do when you stop believing that space is finite and you start believing there will always be another page. There will always be another page, until there isn't. Sweelinck knows that. His handwriting knows that.


ENTRY 36  [Desert Caravan — DC]  ★ READ ALOUD
The desert caravan moves because it is a caravan — the structure is movement, the revenue is movement, the identity of its forty people is that they are the ones who move. When I asked the caravan master about the destination, she named three: a market, a depot, a waypoint that led to another waypoint.

When I asked which was the real destination, she paused in a way that told me no one had asked that before, or had asked and been redirected so long ago the redirect had become the answer.

The caravan was efficient. The forty people knew their roles. And somewhere in the third day of crossing, I understood: the destination is the next departure. The caravan is the answer to the question of what to do when you can't stop long enough to answer the question of what to do.

I am not a caravan. I have a destination. Most days this feels like the important difference. Some days it just feels like the loneliest one.


ENTRY 37  [Agora — GA]
The Oracle said: "You have six. Faster than I anticipated." She said it as a compliment. I received it as a compliment. Later, walking back, I realized: I do not remember the walk to the Agora. I remember the route. I remember the distance and the terrain type and the time elapsed. I do not remember the walk.

I used to write about walks. The quality of light. The smell. I have not written about a walk in some time.


ENTRY 38  [City Yard — CY]
The streets move like water. Not the people individually — individually they are distinct, purposeful, strange. As a mass they are water: finding the path of least resistance, filling the available space, moving around obstacles without deciding to move around obstacles.

When a disturbance happened — a cart tipping, an argument — the water-behavior stopped. Individuals became visible. Each one decided independently whether to stop, to help, to avoid, to observe. For approximately four minutes the street was full of separate people making separate choices.

Then the disturbance resolved and the water-behavior returned. The ones who had helped, the ones who had watched — indistinguishable again. The current didn't care what they'd decided.

I stand outside currents now. I don't think I chose to. I think the route just took me out, over time, far enough that re-entering would require a decision I don't know how to make. The street looked like it would take me back without asking. That was the most frightening part.


ENTRY 39  [Archive — AR]
Sigrid paid me at a run. She didn't look back. She was already moving — there were posts waiting, sixty days of backlog, and she was not going to make them wait sixty-one.

I had forgotten that people do that. Pay you and go back to their lives, immediately, because their lives are right there waiting and they're glad to get back to them. I used to have a life that waited for me. I think I traded it for the knowledge of how to fix theirs.

I don't think Sigrid would understand that sentence. I don't think I fully understand it yet. I am writing it down so that whoever comes after me can read it and decide whether to disagree.


ENTRY 40  [Hermit Cave — HC]
Ithiel moves like someone who knows exactly how much strength he has left and is spending it carefully. I used to move that way. Before the route got clear. Before I stopped believing that strength was a finite resource.

The problem with believing that your strength is infinite is not that it makes you reckless. It makes you lonely. Finite strength is something you share with other people — everyone runs out, so everyone understands running out, so there is a kind of fellowship in the exhaustion. If you never run out, you are not part of that fellowship anymore.

Ithiel is part of it. I can tell by the way he walks. I am envious of him in a way I cannot explain to anyone who hasn't felt what I am feeling right now, which is most people, which is the whole problem.


ENTRY 41  [Cosmic Realm — CO]  ★ READ ALOUD
Your sibling will find seven. I know this the way I know everything now — by the shape of the absence. Someone made the covenant before me. I made it after. You are making it now. The pattern is very clear from here.

The curse is not gathering the Shards. The curse is knowing, every time, that you are the only one who will — and being right about that, and still not knowing whether being right about it is the same as it being true.

I tried to give this knowledge away. I wrote it in forty-one entries. I explained the terrain, the routes, the monsters, the people. I explained everything I knew. No one who read this before you could carry it — not because they weren't capable, but because knowledge that cost this much to earn cannot be received for free. It has to be walked.

You walked it. So you know now. I am sorry for that. I am also grateful. The covenant needed someone who knew.

Come back, when it's done. To the people. They need the person who knows, not just the knowledge. That is what I failed to understand. I kept the knowledge and gave them the outcomes. They needed the person who was willing to stay uncertain with them while the outcomes were still unknown.

Come back.
