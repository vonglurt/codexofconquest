[LJ0] THE LITTORAL PASSAGE

The sea runs west from here. The charts name this water the Littoral Passage —
the chain of coastal courts that controls the southern lanes. Four harbors, four
seals. The commission requires all four.

The captain of the crossing packet points south and says nothing. He has run this
route long enough to have no opinions about the courts at its end. The water is
deep and the current is not a current but a direction that wants to be followed.

The first harbor keeps its light on all night. It is visible from here.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[LC1] PORT AUREL — THE TIDE KEEP

The harbor is a working harbor. Cranes, cargo, the particular arithmetic of tide
windows and ship drafts that governs every decision made here.

Lady Aurel administers it from the harbormaster's tower. She is never not
working. The tide tables are open on her desk. She reads them while speaking. She
speaks while reading them.

She is glad you have come. She will see you at the next window. The next window
follows the current survey. The current survey began before dawn.

The warmth is genuine. The warmth is given in installments. The installments
arrive on her schedule, not yours — which is not a coincidence and is not
carelessness. It is a form of measurement. She is not measuring her schedule. She
is measuring you.

[LJ1] FIRST CROSSING

The passage between the first and second harbors runs through open water. The
charts mark it with the notation common to all deep crossings: here the current
changes.

The change is not weather. It is depth — two hundred fathoms, then three, then a
depth the charts stop measuring because the measurement costs ships.

The sea spawn come from below. They do not announce themselves.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[LC2] PORT CALICE — THE DRAWBRIDGE COURT

Port Calice is built on a headland with a drawbridge on the landward approach and
open water on three sides. The bridge is the only entrance. The bridge chain hangs
visible from the court window — thick iron links, well-maintained, operated by a
wheel mechanism in the courtyard below.

Lady Calice says: perhaps at the evening tide.

She says it with the warmth of someone who is genuinely uncertain. The evening
tide exists. The bridge will lower at some evening tide. Both statements are
accurate. Neither one names the wheel in the courtyard. The wheel is not locked.
The wheel has been there the entire time you have been waiting for the evening
tide.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[LJ2] SECOND CROSSING

The second crossing runs along the cliff face. The coast rises on the eastern side
and the water narrows. In the narrows, the depth-water creatures come up.

They are old things. They live where sunlight does not reach and have been there
longer than the courts above them have been standing. They have no opinion about
the courts. They have been here long before courts were a feature of the
coastline.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[LC3] PORT MIREILLE — THE CAPE COURT

The court of Mireille is the warmest of the four. The fire is large. The table is
set.

Lady Mireille rises when you enter and addresses the court before you can address
it yourself.

'My most trusted companion,' she says. She says it with evident affection. The
court receives this. The court nods. The frame is set before the herald can speak
your name and title.

Your name is still your name. Your title is still your title. Both remain. The
role, however, was not offered. It was assigned. The court will ask for your
counsel. The court will receive your counsel as the counsel of a companion — not
the counsel of a peer, not the counsel of a knight on commission. The distinction
is not declared. It does not need to be. The room already believes it.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[LJ3] THE SERPENT PASSAGE

The third crossing is the longest. The charts name this water la mer des serpents
— the sea of serpents — not as a warning but as a classification.

The serpent is a single creature. It is large enough that the passage narrows when
it enters the channel. It does not attack from below. It enters from the south,
which is the direction you need to travel. It does not move from the channel.

The crossing requires it to move.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[LC4] PORT SOLEN — THE FAR HARBOR

The harbor at Solen faces south. From the window of the court you can see open
water in three directions. The fourth direction is the road back.

Lady Solen stands at the window and points at a ship on the horizon. 'My captain,'
she says. 'He returns soon — he carries the letters of passage.' She names the
ship. She says it with the easy confidence of a woman who has given this
information many times and has never been asked to verify it.

The fishermen at the dock know that name. They have watched this harbor their
entire lives. They have been watching that specific point on the horizon for three
seasons. The ship has not moved. They have not been asked about this, because no
one who comes to Port Solen for the letters of passage goes to the dock first.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[LSO] THE FOG BANK — OPEN WATER

The fog bank sits four miles east of the serpent passage. It has no seasonal
explanation — the captains who know this water say it is always here, at this
depth, in this reach.

The navigator has been speaking in a different register since the serpent crossing.
Not often. Brief exchanges with the air — not muttering, not the wind, something
between. You have noticed it.

There is a voice in the fog that knows you.

It knows about Port Aurel and the tidal schedule. It knows about Port Calice and
the wheel. It knows about Lady Mireille's introduction and what it cost. It knows
because it has been in the water ahead of you the entire time — not as a threat,
as an administrator.

'You have one more court,' it says. Not through the navigator now. Directly. 'I
can arrange that differently. It would be simpler.'

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[LCA] THE SOUTHERN ANCHORAGE

The southern sea opens here.

The four seals are in your coat. The crossing is complete.

The water is still. The stillness is the first moment of stillness since Port
Aurel — the first moment in which no one is measuring anything, no word is being
used as a frame, no warmth is being given on a schedule that is not yours.

The sea does not require anything from you. It is simply water. This is,
unexpectedly, a relief.

[quest_aurel_tide] THE TIDAL SCHEDULE
Port Aurel — WIS Insight DC 12

PREMISE:
Lady Aurel administers the harbor from the harbormaster's tower. She is never not
working. The tide tables are open on her desk. She is glad you have come. She will
see you at the next window. The warmth is genuine. The warmth is given in
installments.

APPROACH:
The tide table is open on her desk. She reads it while speaking to you. She speaks
while reading it. This is the message: everything has prior claim on her
attention, and the space she gives you is borrowed from the schedule.

She is not describing her schedule. She is describing your position in it. The
warmth is real. The installments are also real. Both things are true
simultaneously, which is what makes the frame stable.

PASS:
You name the pattern without naming it as a pattern. You say: I will receive the
seal when it is convenient, not when it is next — send word when the window opens.
You make yourself the appointment, not the waiting.

She looks at you for a moment. She closes the tide table. She gives you the seal.

FAIL:
You wait for the next window. The window opens briefly, closes again. You wait for
the one after that. The seal comes eventually — she was always going to give it.
But she has learned something about you in the waiting that you did not mean to
teach.

DISPOSITION: "The tide keeps its own schedule. Everything else negotiates."
             — The harbormaster's tower, Port Aurel

[quest_calice_bridge] THE WHEEL IN THE COURTYARD
Port Calice — INT Investigation DC 13

PREMISE:
The drawbridge chain hangs visible from the court window. Lady Calice says:
perhaps at the evening tide. She says it the way someone says something true in a
technical sense. The evening tide exists. The wheel is in the courtyard. The wheel
is not locked.

APPROACH:
The bridge chain runs from the tower to the courtyard. The courtyard is accessible
from the gate that was left open when you arrived. The wheel is visible from the
gate.

She has said: perhaps at the evening tide. Perhaps is not no. Perhaps is not yes.
Perhaps is a holding pattern — the coin is still in the air and you are being
asked to wait for it to land.

The wheel has a handle.

PASS:
You go to the courtyard. You find the wheel. The mechanism is straightforward —
well-maintained, designed to be operated without assistance. You turn it. The
bridge lowers.

You cross before the evening tide.

Lady Calice, when she sees you on the other side, says nothing for a moment. Then
she gives you the seal. The perhaps is over.

FAIL:
You wait for the evening tide. The tide comes. She lowers the bridge. You cross.

The crossing happens, but you are no longer the person who initiated it. You were
waiting and she was watching the wait. The seal is given. You have taught her
something about your patience.

DISPOSITION: "The evening tide is reliable. The question is whether you need it
             to be." — Port Calice

[quest_mireille_ami] NAME YOUR STANDING
Port Mireille — CHA Persuasion DC 14

PREMISE:
Lady Mireille introduces you to her court as her most trusted companion before the
herald can speak your name and title. The court receives this. The frame is set.
Your name is still your name. The role, however, was assigned before you could
claim one.

APPROACH:
The introduction has already happened. The court already has an opinion of who you
are in relation to Lady Mireille. The opinion is warm and favorable and entirely
wrong about the category.

You have been placed in the last box — valued, present, available, not chosen. The
court will ask for your counsel. The court will receive it as a companion's
counsel, which is the counsel of someone who gives and is not owed a return.

The herald is still standing at the door with your name on his tongue.

PASS:
You address the court before they can address you through her.

You give your name. You give your commission. You give your title. You do it the
way a person gives these things when they have decided they are worth giving —
without softening them, without the apologetic parenthetical that invitation-only
rooms produce.

The court adjusts. Lady Mireille looks at you — a different look than the one that
accompanied the introduction. She gives you the seal.

FAIL:
You accept the introduction. You counsel the court through the evening. You give
what the role asks for — attention, reassurance, guidance. You are good at this.
You give it well.

You leave the court lighter of something you brought in and heavier of nothing you
were owed. The seal is in your coat. The evening was warm. The role stayed with
you.

DISPOSITION: "He addressed the court directly. It was unexpected. I found it
             clarifying." — Lady Mireille

[quest_solen_horizon] THE SHIP THAT DOES NOT COME
Port Solen — WIS Insight DC 13

PREMISE:
Lady Solen points at a ship on the horizon and names it. Her captain returns soon
with the letters of passage. The fishermen at the dock have watched that ship for
three seasons. It has not moved. No one who comes to Port Solen for the letters
goes to the dock first.

APPROACH:
The ship is on the horizon. She named it. The name is specific.

A future that is real has a date, a plan, a place, a time — something you can
point to and say: this exists. She has given you a name and a direction. She has
not given you a date.

The dock is forty paces from the court gate. The fishermen have been watching this
harbor since before there was a court to watch it from. They know every ship on
their water by the way it sits.

PASS:
You go to the dock before you go to the court.

The fishermen know the ship. They name the season they last saw it move — three
seasons ago, in the autumn, when the captain put in for water and departed south.
He has not returned. The ship on the horizon is anchored. It has been anchored
since the second season of waiting.

You bring this back to Lady Solen. You give her the name she gave you, and the
season the fishermen gave you, and you say: I require a date, or I require a
different carrier.

She pauses. She gives you the date. The carrier is different.

FAIL:
You wait. She says soon. The week passes. Another follows. The letters arrive
eventually — by a different courier, sent quietly, without explanation. The ship
on the horizon is still there.

You did not look at the horizon clearly. You believed the soon was a real soon
because you wanted it to be. The letters are in your coat. The season has changed.

DISPOSITION: "The fishermen remember. They always remember."
             — the dock at Port Solen

[quest_sea_overseer] THE VOICE IN THE FOG
The Fog Bank — WIS Insight DC 15

PREMISE:
The navigator has been speaking in a different register since the serpent crossing.
In the fog bank east of the passage, a voice speaks directly — no intermediary.
It knows about every court. It has been in the water ahead of you the entire time.
It offers to make the fourth court unnecessary.

APPROACH:
The voice is not hostile. This is the part you are meant to notice. A hostile
voice can be refused. A helpful voice requires a different kind of refusal.

It knows Port Aurel. It knows Port Calice. It knows Port Mireille. It has this
information because it has been present at each court — not physically, but
through the navigator, who has been walking around in their own body and having
their own thoughts while also, in a separate register, reporting to something in
the water.

The navigator does not know this. The navigator has simply been feeling unusually
clear-headed and well-directed since the first crossing.

"You have one more court," the voice says. "I can arrange the letters another way.
It would cost you nothing. It would simply require that you allow me to be useful
to you." It says this pleasantly. It is pleasant. This is exactly the problem.

PASS:
You name the structure aloud.

Not to accuse. Not dramatically. You simply say, in the fog, to the voice that has
been ahead of you the entire time: you arranged the four courts. The tidal schedule
was a test because you needed to know if I could read it. Port Calice, the same.
Port Mireille was a categorization test. Port Solen was temporal delay.

You name each one. Flat. Present-tense. Not angry.

"I understand you," you say. "I am going to the fourth court anyway."

The fog lifts. The navigator stops speaking in the second register. The navigator
does not know what just happened.

FAIL:
The offer sounds reasonable. It is reasonable. That is what the voice does — it
makes the reasonable offer at the moment when you have been working hard and the
end is close and the shortcuts look attractive.

You say: tell me what you need.

The voice tells you. It needs a small compliance — nothing significant, a minor
framing of how you will request the letters at Port Solen. A word. A specific
word.

The word is not wrong. The word simply gives the voice a small piece of the frame.
The letters come. The frame shifts a degree. You are not aware of the degree.

DISPOSITION: "The most effective intervention is the one that appears to be
             assistance." — the fog bank, open water

[LC4] LADY SOLEN — Port Solen

DEFAULT (quest not yet complete):
"My captain returns soon — he carries the letters of passage." She points at the
ship on the horizon. She names it with the easy confidence of someone who has named
it many times. "Soon. When the wind is right." The fishermen at the dock have three
seasons of watching that ship not move. No one has asked them.

AFTER PASS (solenSoonRead set):
"You went to the dock first." She says it quietly. "No one goes to the dock first."
She looks at the horizon. The ship is still there. "The letters will come from the
north courier. He leaves on the new moon. That is a date." She gives you the date.
It is real. "That is a date," she says again, as if it needed saying twice.

[LSO] THE OVERSEER — The Fog Bank

DEFAULT (first arrival):
"I have been watching since Port Aurel." The voice is in the air — not the
navigator, directly. "The tidal schedule test. The wheel in the courtyard. The
introduction at Port Mireille. You navigated them." A pause. "You have one more
court. I can arrange the letters another way. It would cost you nothing."

AFTER FAIL (seaOverseerMet set):
"A small compliance," it says. "A single word at Port Solen. You will not notice
the difference in how the letters read." The fog is still present. The voice is
still pleasant. It has been pleasant this whole time. "You are almost done. Let
me be useful."

AFTER PASS (charmResisted set):
The fog is thinner now. The navigator stands at the wheel, attentive to the
compass, speaking to no one. They do not remember the second register. They have
been feeling clear-headed since the first crossing.

There is nothing in the water here. There is no voice. There was a voice. It has
retreated to the place it retreats to when a person names it clearly and goes to
the fourth court anyway.

The sea is quiet. The water moves without agenda.

[LCA] HARBOR KEEPER — The Southern Anchorage

FIRST ARRIVAL (littorialComplete not yet set — triggers completion):
He examines the four seals in order. Port Aurel first — the harbor seal, clean
impression. Port Calice second — the bridge mark. Port Mireille third — the cape
crest. Port Solen fourth — the far harbor, stamped with the courier's counter-mark.

He nods once. He stamps the commission. He hands it back.

"South lanes," he says. "Open." He writes the date in the register. He does not
ask how it went.

RETURN VISIT (littorialComplete set):
"Four seals." He takes each one, examines it, sets it down. "Lady Aurel, Lady
Calice, Lady Mireille, Lady Solen." He looks at the set of them. "Most take three
months. You took —" He checks his register. He does not finish the sentence. He
stamps the commission. "South lanes are open. The water's yours."
