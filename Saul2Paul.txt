SAUL2PAUL — THE ROAD TO KESRA
Arc Design Document · Roll2Hit: The Shattered Codex
§FUTURE-01 · Status: Designed, Unimplemented

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CONCEPT

A new act following the travels of Saul of Tarsis. His conversion on the road.
His three missionary circuits across the inland sea. His arrest and voyage to
the imperial capital. Every node maps to a documented historical stop. Quests
dramatize events; they do not invent them. Source fidelity is the constraint,
not the decoration.

The conversion is not a cutscene. It is a world event. The player walks the
road to Kesra as Saul — enforcer, warrant-carrier, man with a list. He arrives
in Kesra as Paul. The world has not changed. The player's available actions
have changed entirely.

This is the first arc in Roll2Hit where a mid-arc identity shift rewrites what
the player can do.

THE MECHANIC — PRE- AND POST-CONVERSION

Pre-conversion (Saul):
  - Warrant authority: can compel NPCs through legal instruments
  - Order escort available at HR and along road nodes
  - INT/STR/CON primary — Saul operates through power
  - Arrest quests available (dissenter lists, search-and-detain)
  - Persuasion checks unavailable; Saul does not persuade, he commands

Post-conversion (Paul):
  - All warrant authority closed. Arrest quests locked permanently.
  - Rhetoric resources unlock: speeches, letters, NPC disposition chains
  - CHR/WIS/INT primary — Paul operates through presence
  - New quest type: "testimony" — describe what happened; crowd reaction varies
  - The companion system activates: Barnach, Silar, Timael travel with you
    and their presence modifies encounter outcomes

Trade summary: you lose the ability to compel. You gain the ability to persuade
people who cannot be compelled. This is not an upgrade. It is a reframing.

NODE MAP AND VIGNETTE TEXT

─── HR — HERATH ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Pre-conversion
Connections: Road south toward Kesra

Node text:
The city writes its authority in stone. The law courts have been here for three
hundred years; the newest wall is a century old; the oldest inscription above
the gate has been re-carved twice because the stone wore before the law did.

Saul of Tarsis has a desk in the lower court. On that desk: three warrants,
two authorization letters, and a list of names. The names are of people who
have been meeting in houses and saying things the court considers seditious.
The warrant language is precise: 'Bring them.' Not 'question.' Not 'invite.'
Bring.

He has done this before. The warrants are a bureaucratic instrument. He treats
them with the seriousness appropriate to bureaucratic instruments — which is
to say, complete and impersonal seriousness. He does not hate the people on
the list. He has decided that the list is correct. He has not examined that
decision recently.

The road to Kesra leaves from the east gate. He has already requisitioned
the escort. He leaves at first light.

NPC: The Court Registrar
Quote: "Warrants are countersigned. Three copies: one for you, one for the
escort commander, one for the Kesra magistrate. The prisoners are to be
returned to Herath for processing. Do not process them in Kesra — the
Kesra magistrate has standing to dismiss, and our magistrate does not."

Loot: Three Herath Warrants (key item) · Order of Escort (key item)
Battle: null
Sleep: true (inn, 5gp)

─── ROAD TO KESRA (conversion node) ─────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Conversion
Note: This node has no combat, no loot, no NPC. It is a single event.

Quest: quest_road_kesra — "The Light at Noon"
Type: Conversion event. No pass/fail. The light comes regardless.

Node text:
The road is well-maintained for the first half day. After that the stones give
way to packed earth and the escort falls back to march-pace. The dust is the
same colour as the sky at this hour — noon, flat white, the sun directly
overhead with no shadow to navigate by.

The light, when it comes, is not theatrical. It does not arrive with sound.
It arrives with the absolute certainty of a fact that has already been true
and is simply now being acknowledged. The road does not change. The dust does
not change. The sky does not change.

Saul of Tarsis falls.

He cannot say how long he is down. When he looks up, he cannot see. The
escort commander is asking him something. He cannot hear the words correctly.
He says: 'Lead me to the city.' He means Kesra. He means: I cannot complete
this journey on my own and I require assistance. Both things are true for
the first time simultaneously.

The warrants are still in his coat. He does not use them again.

[WIS save DC 10 — determines the clarity of what is heard, not whether it
is heard. On a pass: full voice. On a fail: impressions only. Both outcomes
advance the node. Both outcomes convert. The WIS save is not a test of
worthiness — it is a measure of what the player carries into the encounter.]

Vignette text (WIS pass):
The voice says his name. Not the name he uses in the court. The name before that.
He does not know how the voice knows the name before that. He does not ask.

Vignette text (WIS fail):
Something happens on the road. He will spend three days working out what it was.
He will never work out all of it.

[State changes on this node:
  - S_story.saulConverted = true
  - Warrant authority: locked
  - Arrest quests: locked permanently
  - Companion system: activates
  - Player name in all subsequent dialogue: 'Paul' not 'Saul']

─── KS — KESRA ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Post-conversion, early
Connections: Road north to Herath · South to Dust Roads · Harbor east

Time gate: Player cannot leave until saulConverted === true AND blindDaysKS >= 3.
Day counter increments each time the player attempts to leave. On day 3,
Anath appears.

Node text:
The escort has left him at the gate of the lower city. They are not required
to wait — the warrant authority is his, not theirs, and he has not invoked it
since the road. They leave with the courtesy appropriate to leaving a superior
officer who has become someone else's problem.

He does not know anyone in Kesra who is not on the list. The list is in his
coat. He does not take it out.

Three days. He is in a house on the lower road. The innkeeper brings food at
noon and does not ask questions about his eyes. Someone else's servant —
he does not know whose — has been bringing water in the morning. He has not
asked for it. He has not told anyone he cannot see.

On the third morning, someone knocks.

Quest: quest_anath — "The House on the Lower Road"
Type: NPC encounter. Activates on day 3 (blindDaysKS >= 3).
Completes on: anathSightRestored === true

NPC: Anath
Quote: "I was told you were here. I was told to come." He sets the bowl down.
His hands are steady. His voice is not quite steady. "I have heard what you
did to the people in Herath. I have heard what you were coming here to do.
I am here anyway." He does not explain why. He does not need to. He places
his hands. The light returns.

Disposition: "I was afraid of you for three years. I was afraid of you when
I knocked on that door. I am choosing not to let that matter." — Anath

State: anathSightRestored = true · sightRestored = true
Reward: 300 XP · Sight mechanics restored (if implemented)

[The basket escape: a second quest in Kesra, triggered when the city magistrate
(having learned Saul is in Kesra, now as a liability rather than an asset)
issues a detention warrant. The warrant authority turns against the warrant-
carrier. Quest: quest_basket_kesra — "Over the Wall." A basket, a rope, a
window in the city wall at night. Completes on escapedKesra = true.]

Quest: quest_basket_kesra — "Over the Wall"
Type: Stealth skill check. DEX · DC 12.
Hint: Get out of Kesra before the magistrate's watch closes the gate.
Vignette: The basket is large enough. The rope is long enough. The wall
is higher than it looks. Below the wall: the road to Herath is open. Above
the wall: the window is the only way out of a city that has been told to
hold you. The watch is on the gate. Not on the wall. Not yet.
Pass: Down. The road. The dark. The escort that escorted you in does not
recognize the man who left.
Fail: You wait another night. The window remains. So does the watch.
(Retryable: 1 day gate.)

─── DR — THE DUST ROADS ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Post-conversion retreat. Optional.
Connections: KS only (return path)

Node text:
No road. The roads end at Kesra's southern gate. Below the gate: a track
that the caravans use, maintained by foot traffic rather than civic investment.
Below the track: the desert proper, which is not hostile so much as indifferent.

He stays in the desert for a season. The duration is not specified in the records
he leaves behind — he mentions it in one letter as 'the time I spent in the
Dust Roads' and does not elaborate. Whatever happens there is not recorded
and is not recoverable by the player. This is intentional. Some things about a
person belong to the desert.

He returns from the Dust Roads changed in a way that is different from the road
to Kesra. The road to Kesra changed what he was. The desert changed what he
does with it.

NPC: None.
Battle: Desert encounter table (see Terrain: desert_caravan).
Loot: None.
Note: No quests unlock here. The Dust Roads is a resting node — time passes,
encounters occur, the player returns. The value is negative space.

─── HR (RETURN) — HERATH ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Early post-conversion
Time gate: 15 days. After 15 days, Hellenists threaten and player must leave.

Node text (return):
He has been here before. The court knows his name. The court knows his name
in a new context: Saul of Tarsis who went to Kesra with warrants and came back
without prisoners and is now, apparently, one of the people on the list.

Barnach vouches for him. This is more than Barnach expected to do and less
than what was needed. He does it anyway.

The city is complicated about this. Some people are afraid. Some people are
glad. Both groups have reasonable grounds.

He stays fifteen days. He speaks in the assembly halls. He argues with people
who disagree with him — he is very good at arguing, and was very good at it
before the road, and the conversion did not change this about him, which is
a point of some reflection.

On the fifteenth day, the Hellenists gather near the gate. Barnach says: go.

Quest: quest_barnach_vouches — "Vouched For"
Type: Disposition check. Requires barnachFavor >= 1 (Barnach must have been
spoken to in Herath before conversion; the favor is earned pre-arc).
Completes on: barnachVouchedHR = true.
Disposition: "He was there. I was not. I am vouching for what he told me,
which is everything he has." — Barnach

Quest: quest_hellenists_herath — "Fifteen Days"
Type: Time gate / forced move.
Activates: Day 15 at HR (return).
Completes on: player moves to TS.
Text: "They are at the gate. They are at the assembly hall. They are in the
square. Fifteen days is how long Herath was prepared to hold this tension.
It has held. Now it is releasing. Barnach says: 'Tarsis. Go to Tarsis. You are
from Tarsis. They will not follow you to Tarsis.'"

─── TS — TARSIS ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: The silent years.
Note: Paul stays in Tarsis for an indeterminate period (years, not days).
The time abstraction is handled by a "years passed" text on entry.

Node text:
He is from here. The streets are familiar in the way that streets are familiar
when you have not been to them since you were young and have become a different
person in the interval. The house he grew up in is still standing. Someone
else lives there. He does not knock.

He works. He has a trade — tentmaking, the craft he learned before he learned
the law. It is meticulous work and the city has no particular opinion about
tentmakers one way or the other, which is exactly the quality he requires
of a city right now.

The years in Tarsis are in no document he wrote. They are a gap in the record.
The things that happened in the gap happened to a person who was both Saul
and Paul and neither, settling into what he had become. The record resumes
when Barnach arrives at the gate.

NPC: Barnach (arrival)
Quote: "I have been looking for you for a year. Anthos needs you. I need you.
I am not going to tell you it's safe — you know it isn't. I am going to tell
you it's time."

[The Barnach arrival at Tarsis is quest_barnach_finds — 'A Year Looking.'
No skill check. No combat. Completes on: barnachFoundPaul = true. The reward
is the arc resuming.]

─── AO — ANTHOS ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: First journey, base of operations.
Note: This is where Saul is formally renamed Paul in the record. First called
by new name here. The name change is mechanical: all dialogue post-AO
uses 'Paul.' The player is notified once.

Node text:
Anthos is not trying to be anything in particular. It is too large to be a
village and not large enough to be a city and too mixed to be classified as
either. Merchants from four different peoples live in the same quarter
without anyone having organized them. They got there before the organizing
arrived and now they are too settled to move.

This is where the first journey forms. Barnach is here. Silar is here.
The commission comes from the assembly — the first time Paul has been sent
somewhere by a body of people rather than going by his own authority.
He has been places before on his own authority. This is different. He is
accountable to the people who sent him and to the people he will find.
He finds this clarifying.

He is called Paul here for the first time. He will be called Saul again once,
by someone who does not know the road, and it will not fit.

NPC: Assembly Elder Joach
Quote: "We are sending you. Barnach goes with you. Take what you need. The
mandate is: go to the cities you can reach and say what you know. That is the
whole of it."

Quest: quest_anthos_commission — "The Sending"
Type: Story beat. No check. Completes on: commissionReceived = true.
Reward: 150 XP. Companion system fully activates. Barnach joins party.
Disposition: "The mandate is simple because the work isn't." — Assembly Elder Joach

─── CI2 — THE COPPER ISLE ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: First journey, stop 1.
Connections: Harbor south to Lythros

Node text:
The harbor smells of copper smelting and salt, in roughly equal parts. The
island has been exporting copper since before anyone in Anthos was born. It
has a governor who is interested in unusual things — the kind of governor
who reads widely and holds opinions about ideas rather than only about policy,
which is either an asset or a liability depending on who arrives with an idea.

Ezzir is in the governor's service. This is his recommendation and the governor
finds his recommendations useful. He has been in the governor's service for
six years. He has a good record. He is also blocking the door.

Quest: quest_ezzir — "The Sorcerer's Opposition"
Type: WIS save vs blindness (DC 14). Combat not required; the confrontation
is rhetorical until Ezzir escalates.
Hint: Ezzir has the governor's ear. He will use it against you if you let him.
Do not let him have the first word.
Vignette: He is precisely positioned between Paul and the governor. This is
not an accident — Ezzir has been in rooms before and knows where to stand.
The governor is watching. He is watching the way a man watches two arguments
he has not yet decided between.
Pass (WIS ≥ 14): Ezzir's words stop working. This is visible. The governor
sees it. The governor has been watching for exactly this kind of evidence.
Fail: Ezzir gets the first word and the second word and Paul gets to recover
from both.
Disposition: "I have been serving this governor for six years. You have been
here eleven hours." — Ezzir

Quest: quest_governor_copper — "The Governor Listens"
Type: CHR check (DC 11). Activates after quest_ezzir resolved.
Post: Governor converted (govCopperConverted = true). Ezzir removed from
service. The door is open.
Disposition: "He said things I could not explain by the methods I usually use
to explain things. I find this interesting rather than threatening. It is
possible I find it interesting because it is true." — Governor of the Copper Isle

─── LT — LYTHROS ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: First journey, stop 2.
Timael joins here (youngest companion; picked up at Lythros).

Node text:
A small city in a wide valley. It has the particular civic pride of a place
that has been overlooked — they know they are not the capital and have decided
that this is fine with them. The market runs six days a week. The seventh day
the market stalls stay up but the vendors go to the assembly hall, which is
an arrangement that satisfies both commerce and attendance.

There is a man by the gate who has been by the gate his entire life. He has
a specific condition of the feet that is not treatable by the methods available
in Lythros. He knows this. He has made his peace with this. He is watching the
road with the patience of someone who has accepted their situation completely.

He is watching Paul specifically.

Quest: quest_lame_lythros — "The Gate"
Type: Faith check (WIS DC 10 — 'faith check' is flavored WIS).
Note: Pass or fail affects the crowd response; the healing occurs on a pass.
Pass: The man stands. The crowd witnesses. The crowd's response is immediate
and wrong: they call Paul a god. Paul corrects them at length. The crowd
is disappointed. They were ready to sacrifice to a god and are now being asked
to reconsider their entire cosmological framework in an afternoon.
Fail: The man does not stand. Paul continues. The city is unchanged. The man
goes home.
Disposition: "He looked at me. He did not look at the crowd. He looked at me
and said: stand. I have been told to stand before. No one has ever meant it
this way." — The Man at the Gate

Quest: quest_stoning_lythros — "Left for Dead"
Type: Survival event. Activates after quest_lame_lythros, regardless of outcome.
Note: The crowd that wanted to sacrifice to Paul becomes the crowd that stones
him. Both responses are to the same event. The crowd had expected a god.
What they got was a correction. Some crowds do not accept corrections.
Mechanic: HP drops to 1. Cannot be healed above 1 until node is left.
Timael, who has been watching from the market square, does not leave.
Disposition: "He got up. Everyone else left. I decided to stay." — Timael

Timael joins as companion on leaving Lythros.

─── PL — PHILLAM ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Second journey, stop 1.
Lyra first convert. Prison earthquake event.

Node text:
The city sits on a river that has flooded twice in living memory and will flood
again. The buildings on the high ground are expensive. The buildings on the
lower ground are not, and have the particular character of buildings that have
been rebuilt before and know they will be rebuilt again.

Lyra sells purple cloth from a stall on the bridge. Purple cloth is expensive
and her clients are specific and she knows all of them by name and she has
excellent judgment about new clients. She watches Paul from across the bridge
for two days before she speaks to him. When she speaks to him it is because
she has already decided.

The prison is on the eastern road. It is a city prison, not an imperial one,
which means the cells are smaller and the guards are more bored. Paul and Silar
are in the inner cell. The earthquake, when it comes, opens every door.

Quest: quest_lyra_phillam — "The Purple Merchant"
Type: Dialogue encounter. No check. Lyra asks questions; Paul answers them.
Completes on: lyraConverted = true.
Disposition: "I have been listening to arguments all my life — merchants argue,
clients argue, the city council argues. He was not arguing. He was describing
something he had seen. I trust descriptions more than arguments." — Lyra

Quest: quest_prison_earthquake — "The Inner Cell"
Type: Timed event. Day counter: prison sentence (magistrate-imposed, triggered
by slave-owner complaint). On day N, earthquake event fires.
Mechanic: All cells open. Paul and Silar do not leave. The jailer, expecting
to find them gone (escape is a capital offence for a jailer), finds them
present. This has an effect on the jailer.
Note: The earthquake is not a roll. It is not a combat event. It fires because
it fires. Paul's decision to stay when the door opens is where the player's
agency lives.
Disposition: "The door was open. He was still there. I don't have a framework
for that." — Jailer at Phillam

─── AE — AETHON ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Second journey, stop 2.
The Unknown Altar speech. Rhetoric skill check.

Node text:
Aethon builds things. It has built more things than most cities of its size —
temples to forty different ideas, each one maintained with equal civic
conscientiousness, on the theory that if you maintain all the altars you have
covered all your bases. The city has not found this theory wanting. The altars
are well-maintained and the city is prosperous and the people are curious about
ideas in the way that prosperous people can afford to be.

There is an altar at the east end of the market that says: TO AN UNKNOWN ONE.
It has been there for two hundred years. Nobody knows who built it or what it
was for. It is maintained along with everything else. Every spring the priests
clean the inscription. Every spring the inscription remains: TO AN UNKNOWN ONE.

Paul has been standing in front of it for a long time.

Quest: quest_unknown_altar — "The Unknown Altar"
Type: Rhetoric skill check. INT + Proficiency, DC 12.
Partial outcome (result 9–11): Some of the crowd stays. They will think about it.
Full outcome (result 12+): The crowd follows. The Aethon assembly invites him
to speak formally. He does. He speaks about the altar. He says: the one you
have already been maintaining — I can tell you who that is.
Fail (result < 9): The crowd disperses politely. A few scholars ask questions
afterward. Aethon is curious but will not be moved today.
Vignette text:
The market crowd assembles the way Aethon market crowds always assemble:
partly out of interest, partly out of boredom, partly out of the habit of
stopping when someone is speaking. They are not hostile. They are waiting to
see whether this is worth their time. They have good judgment about what is
worth their time. They have had practice.

He stands in front of the Unknown Altar and starts with what is already there.
Disposition: "He did not argue against our altars. He stood in front of the
oldest one and said: I know who you meant." — Aethon Market Observer

─── KR — KORATH ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Second journey, stop 3. 18-month stay.
Prisca + Akil node. Tentmaking. Longest stay of any city.

Node text:
Korath is a harbor city built on two harbors — one facing east, one facing west,
both active, both profitable, both generating the kind of civic complexity that
comes from two different trading cultures meeting at a land crossing and
deciding to make a city there rather than fight about it.

Prisca and Akil have a workshop on the street behind the east harbor. They make
tents. It is precise, skilled work and they do it well and the quality is
consistent because Akil does the cutting and Prisca does the finishing and
they have been doing this for eleven years and the division is established.
They hire Paul when he arrives because he knows the trade and because he
will work for the room in the back.

He stays eighteen months. He earns his rent in tent canvas. He writes letters
to the cities he has already been to. Some of the letters survive; some of
them are the most important things he will ever write; he does not know which
ones yet.

NPC: Prisca + Akil (joint dialogue)
Prisca: "The room is in the back. The rate is what you can produce.
We don't get involved in your other business."
Akil: "The canvas comes first. When the canvas is done, the rest is yours."

Quest: quest_korath_letters — "The Work and the Letters"
Type: Time passage. 18-month counter (abstracted to 3 node-visits minimum).
Completes on: korathLettersWritten = true.
Note: The letters written in Korath are readables — short, formal, containing
specific instructions Paul is sending to specific cities. Each one is a piece
of something ongoing. The player can read them. They should read them.
Letter 1: To Phillam — on unity. Letter 2: To Anthos — on how to behave
in assembly. Letter 3: To communities not yet visited — on the nature of hope.
Disposition: "The letters he sent from here — I read them thirty years later.
I recognized the man who wrote them as the man I knew. The man I knew cut tent
canvas in this workshop and argued with sailors at the harbor and came home
and wrote until late." — Prisca, recalled

─── EF — EPHRATH ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Third journey. 3-year stay. Temple riot.

Node text:
Ephrath's economy runs on the temple district. The temple district runs on
silver. The silversmiths make models of the temple — small ones for households,
large ones for shrines, the full range. It is a good trade. It has been a good
trade for generations. The models are sold in every market in the inland sea.
The workshops are established, the supply chains are stable, and everything
is working correctly.

Paul has been here three years. The silversmiths have noticed.

Quest: quest_temple_riot — "Riot in the Marketplace"
Type: Escape-route decision tree.
Trigger: demetrius_riots === true (Demetrius the silversmith has organized
the craftsmen's guild against Paul; the guild meeting tips into the streets).
Decision points:
  A) Through the theater (crowds, exits, risk of getting cornered)
  B) Through the tannery district (slower, smells, less crowded)
  C) Wait it out inside (Ephrathite allies offer shelter; 24-hour time lock)
Each path resolves differently. Each path succeeds. The point is not
whether Paul escapes — he does — but how, and what it costs.
Vignette:
The riot begins the way riots begin: someone is angry and someone else was
already angry about something else and they find each other in a crowd.
Demetrius is angry about commerce. The guild is angry about commerce. The
crowd is angry about general instability and the temple-model trade is as good
a symbol as any. By the time it reaches the market square it has become
the kind of crowd that does not remember how it started.
Disposition: "He was in the city three years. Nobody thought about the
silver shops until he had been there three years. We should have thought
about the silver shops." — Ephrath Guild Merchant, retrospective

─── MT — MELTA ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Voyage and shipwreck. 276 aboard.

Node text:
The ship has been at sea for fourteen days. Fourteen days of weather that the
captain described on day one as 'manageable' and has not described since because
he has stopped speaking to passengers and is speaking only to the wheel.
There are two hundred and seventy-six people aboard. Paul has been counting.
He counts because the count matters. He does not know why it matters yet.

The grounding happens at night. The ship hits the shallows at speed and does
not come off. The hull holds. It will not hold until morning.

The soldiers want to kill the prisoners — standard protocol; a prisoner who
survives a shipwreck is a prisoner who can escape. The centurion stops them.
The centurion stops them for Paul, specifically, which Paul did not ask for
and could not have arranged.

Two hundred and seventy-six people reach the shore of Melta before dawn.
Paul has been counting all night. The count is correct.

Quest: quest_shipwreck_melta — "Two Hundred and Seventy-Six"
Type: Survival event. No attack roll. Completes on: melteShoreReached = true.
Note: The storm is not a combat encounter. The shipwreck is not a skill check.
Everyone survives because they survive. The centurion's intervention is not
explained. Paul's count — precise, ongoing, conducted while the ship was
coming apart — is the only active thing the player does. The count is the
prayer.
Disposition: "He counted us all night. I asked him why. He said: because each
one is the count." — Melta Shore Survivor

Quest: quest_snake_melta — "It Did Nothing"
Type: Passive event. Fires on melteShoreReached.
Note: Do not roll. Do not explain. Play it completely straight.
Vignette:
A fire has been built on the shore. Paul is gathering wood — this is not
beneath him; it has never been beneath him — and a snake comes out of the
bundle. It bites his hand. It is a viper. Everyone on the shore knows what
a viper bite does. They are watching. They have been watching all night and
now they are watching this.

He shakes the snake off into the fire. He keeps gathering wood.
Time passes. He does not swell. He does not die. He keeps working.

The people who have been watching change their minds about who he is. They
had one theory before the snake. They have a different theory after. Neither
theory is exactly correct. The correct thing is more specific and less
available to a crowd on a shore at dawn after a shipwreck.
Disposition: "We thought he was a murderer — escaped justice, the sea caught
him at last. Then the snake. Then he just — kept working." — Melta Islander

─── ST — THE SEAT ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Act: Final. Under house arrest. Open ending.

Node text:
The Seat is the largest city he has ever been in. It is also the most organized
city he has ever been in, and the most indifferent. It has seen everything.
It has processed everything. It continues.

He is under house arrest in a rented house. The conditions are comfortable
by the standards of imperial detention — he can receive visitors, which he
does, constantly; he can write letters, which he does, constantly; he cannot
leave, which he does not, because he has visitors to receive and letters to write.

He has been here two years. The letters he writes here are different from
the letters he wrote in Korath — shorter, more specific, more aware of what
remains to be said versus what can remain unsaid. He is running the calculation
that people run when they know the remaining column is smaller than the spent one.

The arc does not end here. It stops here.

[This is the design intention: The Seat node has no final quest. No boss. No
resolution. The player arrives, receives the last letter written by Paul to
Timael ('I have finished the course. I have kept faith.'), and the node simply
continues to exist. Paul receives visitors. He writes letters. The city moves
around him. The arc does not give the player a completion marker because the
arc does not end — it simply reaches the last documented stop and continues
from there as a fact that no longer requires the player's presence to remain true.]

Final letter: "I have done what I set out to do. I have done more than I set
out to do because the thing I set out to do was the wrong thing, and the right
thing turned out to be larger. I am not complaining about this. I have kept
faith. The rest is not mine to carry." — Paul, last letter to Timael

NPC: Timael (final)
Quote: "He never asked me to call him Saul. He never asked me not to. I called
him Paul because that was who he was when I knew him. I knew him from Lythros.
I stayed because he got up." He looks at the letter. "He said to give it to
whoever needed it most. I have been holding it for two years. It seems like
you need it most."

VOICE RULES

Eight rules for writing this arc.

  1. Paul does not explain himself. He describes what he saw and what it meant.
     The difference matters. Explanation is defense. Description is record.

  2. Barnach speaks before Paul does. He has been in the room longer.
     He knows how the room reads people.

  3. Timael does not explain why he stayed. He has not examined it.
     Do not ask him to.

  4. Every city has its own economy and that economy shapes how it receives Paul.
     Korath is commerce. Aethon is ideas. Ephrath is silver. The city type
     determines what the opposition is made of.

  5. The snake at Melta: no roll, no explanation, no theory. He shakes it off.
     He keeps working. The crowd changes their mind. That is the whole scene.

  6. The letters are not summaries. They are specific instructions to specific
     people about specific situations. They sound like someone who has been
     somewhere and is writing to someone who hasn't.

  7. The warrants remain in the inventory after conversion. They are not
     removed. They are simply never presented again. The player can read them.
     The text does not change. What has changed is the person carrying them.

  8. The arc ends at The Seat without a completion marker. Paul does not retire.
     He does not receive a prize. He receives visitors and writes letters.
     The arc stops because the documented record stops. Not because it is over.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE THORN

One design element from the source material that has no mechanical analogue
yet and must be handled carefully.

Paul writes, in a letter from Korath, about 'a thorn in the flesh' — something
that troubles him, that he asked three times to have removed, and that was not
removed. He does not specify what it is. He never specifies what it is.
The source material is deliberately silent.

The arc must preserve that silence. The thorn is not named. It is not cured.
It is carried. The mechanic (if any) should be: a passive debuff with no cure,
applied at some point post-conversion, never explained, never resolved. The
player notices it. The arc does not comment on it.

If the thorn is implemented as a status effect, it should read:
  [Thorn] — A persistent condition. Source unknown. It has been present since
  the road to Kesra, or before it — the timing is unclear. Three times the
  question was asked. The answer was: the grace is sufficient.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CROSS-REFERENCE: THE ARCHIVIST THEME

The Saul/Paul arc belongs to the same thematic tradition as the 14.225 chain.

The 14.225 chain's central question: is the archive still maintained?
The answer: the archive still has an archivist. The act of following the
evidence is the answer.

The Saul/Paul arc's central question, from the road to The Seat:
what do you do with a record that cannot be fully explained?

The warrants are a record. The letters are a record. The snake at Melta is
a record. Paul counts the 276 survivors because the count matters — because
the individual matters — and the count is the form the record takes.

Both arcs are about people who kept careful account of things in the face of
institutions that preferred the account to remain unclosed, unfiled, or silent.
Y. Gurt stayed at the lake and sent data anyway. Solm wrote the same entry
every morning for eighty-eight days waiting for someone who could close it.
Paul writes letters to cities he has already left.

The archive outlasts the archivist.
But the archive still has an archivist.
Both things are true.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

Time unit: The arc requires a day/week counter as primary time unit.
The hour counter is insufficient — the journeys span years.
Proposed: a 'journey day' counter that increments on each node move.
Journey days are not wall-clock hours. They are steps.

Source fidelity: every node maps to a documented stop. Do not invent stops.
The invented material is in the texture — the voice, the detail, the object
that carries weight. The structure comes from the source.

CO-002 — MUFFAT STATE CONSOLIDATION: STATES 5+6 INTO ONE VISIT
Status: CLOSED — implemented Layer 97
Source critique: Muffat accumulates six dialogue states — one too many for
comfortable tracking. States 5 (antecedentMet → Solm direction) and 6
(cycle4NoteRead → Cycle 4 revelation) require two separate return visits to DK
after the player has already done the arc's heavy work. If the player is not
paying close attention to the chain, these states are easy to miss entirely.
The design chose granularity over economy and the character suffers for it.

PROBLEM IN CONCRETE TERMS:
After completing quest_antecedent_01 (antecedentMet = true), the player must:
  Visit 1 — DK: see State 5 (Solm direction), receive quest_solm_01
  Visit 2 — SF: complete quest_solm_01 (solmFound = true)
  Visit 3 — DK: see State 6 (Cycle 4 crate) via quest_muffat_05
  Visit 4 — DK: see State 6 dialogue

That is three mandatory DK visits and one SF visit after the Antecedent Chamber.
The arc earned two of those visits. It did not earn four.

THE FIX: Consolidate the crate-opening (quest_muffat_05) into the same DK
visit as the Solm direction (State 5). Muffat opens the crate in the same
scene she tells the player where Solm is. Both quest_solm_01 AND the Cycle 4
revelation happen in one visit. The player leaves DK with two active waypoints
(SF for Solm, already at DK for the crate) instead of having to return.

IMPLEMENTATION:

1. Rewrite Muffat's NPC_DIALOGUE State 5 (currently keyed to antecedentMet)
   to deliver BOTH the Solm direction and the crate opening in one scene.

NEW STATE 5 TEXT (replaces current antecedentMet state):
"I sent Solm to the Map Shop three months ago. Dusk does not ask." She turns
to the shelf and lifts the nearest crate onto the counter. She does not pause
before doing it. The question is closed; the reason not to open it is closed
with it. "You have been to the terminus."

She removes the packing material. Receiver array, Category 4, as expected.

   — Push Shipping Manifest (Intercepted) to S_story.inventory inline
   — quest_muffat_05 will auto-complete on next quest check (completeFn
     already checks cycle4NoteRead)

3. Remove quest_muffat_05 from its current separate activation/reward handler
   OR keep it but have the reward fire silently (no separate visit required).

4. State 6 (post-cycle4NoteRead, currently the Cycle 4 reflection speech)

CO-003 — SAUL2PAUL FUTURE AUDIT: FLAG STATES THAT CAN BE SKIPPED
Status: OPEN (design note, not yet implementable — applies to arc when built)
Source critique: Extended character quoteFn chains work only when the player
is paying close attention. States that activate on specific flags can be
missed entirely if the player returns to a node before the activating
condition is true and then never returns.

THIS CO APPLIES FORWARD — to the Saul2Paul arc when implemented, and to
any future multi-state quoteFn chain.

RULE: Any NPC with 4+ quoteFn states that activates on sequential flags
MUST have at least one of the following:
  (a) A quest waypoint pointing at the NPC when the new state becomes active
  (b) A world event message on the HUD when the flag triggers: "Return to
      [NPC Name] — something has changed."
  (c) The state's activation condition also fires a new active quest
      (so the journal acts as the reminder system)

SAUL2PAUL APPLICATION:
The Saul2Paul arc has companion NPCs (Barnach, Silar, Timael) who travel
across nodes and update their dialogue at each stop. These are sequential
states with no natural reminder system — the player may move through a node
without speaking to a companion and miss a state permanently.

FIX FOR SAUL2PAUL COMPANIONS:
All companion dialogue states should be delivered as one-time injections
(the _getNPCDialogue injection pattern, or equivalent) rather than
conditional quoteFn states. One-time injections fire on the FIRST visit
to the node after the condition is true, regardless of whether the player
actively clicks the NPC. This guarantees delivery.

Alternatively: companions speak unprompted when the player enters a node
(a node-entry trigger, not an NPC-click trigger). This is a new mechanic
not yet in Roll2Hit — worth building for this arc specifically, since the
companion system requires it.

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA (when Saul2Paul is implemented):
- Companion voice lines at each node are delivered as one-time injections
  OR via a node-entry trigger, not as passive quoteFn states
- No companion line can be permanently missed by not clicking the NPC
- Any NPC with 4+ states has a waypoint/journal reminder when state advances
